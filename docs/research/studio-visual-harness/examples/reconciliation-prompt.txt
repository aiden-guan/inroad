ART DIRECTION — start with an idea, not a style adjective.

Creative range: Unexpected. Explore a more surprising conceptual leap or material collision. Keep one legible idea; complexity alone is not creativity.

Medium: Sculptural still life. A distinctive physical construction, dramatic scale and carefully controlled light.

Respect a specific user-supplied scene, medium, palette, placement or minimal treatment. Those choices take precedence over automatic exploration. Develop its craft; do not replace it with an unrelated metaphor.

For an open brief, privately explore three genuinely different concepts: different visual mechanisms and compositions, not three colors of the same scene. State the link between the message and each subject before choosing the strongest. Consider transformation, impossible scale, visual analogy, unexpected juxtaposition or meaningful repetition only when relevant.

Possible media for exploration: sculptural. A selected medium constrains all concepts; vary the idea within it.

Reject an idea that could advertise any company after swapping the caption. A laptop, coffee, floating device, generic stock worker or arbitrary glossy object is not a concept. Use a literal object when explicitly requested or meaningfully transformed by the idea.

Resolve the chosen concept into: a concrete subject and action; one visual surprise; dominant silhouette and viewpoint; material/mark-making; light and shadow; background; palette roles; exact calm copy area. Specify what the viewer notices first, then discovers second. Use rich texture or gradients when they serve that idea, not as automatic decoration.

Canvas: 1200×627; use: LinkedIn launch graphic. Reserve one clearly specified left or right third for typography, choosing the side that supports the concept.

Keep essential details at least 6% of the width from the edges and make the subject readable at thumbnail size.

Make the picture and typography one planned composition. Do not shrink an entire poster into an image slot, cover the subject with copy or fill the calm area with visual noise.

Brand palette: #172B4D, #FF694F, #F2EDDF. Assign background, subject and accent roles deliberately; do not mechanically use the first color as the background. Without a user palette, treat these as a starting point, not a compulsory house style.

REFERENCE TECHNIQUES — transfer the reasoning, never the brand appearance. These are creative studies, not performance evidence:

Robinhood: The case study frames financial access as a larger brand story. Technique: Translate an abstract financial idea into an ambitious physical scene. Do not substitute decorative coins or invented performance graphs for meaning. Source: https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/robinhood/

Vitaminwater: A beverage identity offers a reference for using color to organize distinct experiences. Technique: Use a purposeful color field and a sensorial focal object. Make the material finish and crop specific to the benefit. Source: https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/vitaminwater/

Bose: A sound brand offers a lens for expressing an invisible experience through a physical identity. Technique: Show an effect on a relevant material: vibration, compression or resonance. Specify the physical behavior rather than adding generic waves. Source: https://wearecollins.com/case-studies/bose/

OUTPUT CONTRACT
