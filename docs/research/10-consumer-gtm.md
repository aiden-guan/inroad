# Win one community before buying growth

Early consumer GTM comes down to concentration, then a retention gate, then amplification. Lenny Rachitsky's study of 100+ B2C startups found that **more than 50% won their first 1,000 users by going, by hand, to where a "super-specific" audience already gathers**. None succeeded with more than three strategies, so Inroad's consumer mode should draw its daily moves from one primary channel and at most two test channels. Paid acquisition, referral engineering and growth hires should wait for two signals. The first is a cohort retention curve that flattens at or above category benchmarks: roughly 25%/45% "good"/"great" six-month user retention for consumer social, 30%/50% for transactional and 40%/70% for consumer SaaS. The second is at least 40% of engaged users saying they would be "very disappointed" without the product. After that point, virality multiplies the other channels rather than replacing them: at a typical K below 1, a seed cohort ultimately yields 1/(1−K) users. Marketplaces start on the constrained side, which was supply in 14 of the 17 marketplaces Lenny studied, inside one geography or category. Creator programs work as cheap searches. Founders pick nano and micro creators on median views, seed them at an expected 20–40% post rate, and pay about $150–$350 per deliverable in small-creator marketplace data. Subscription LTV has to be modeled on vendor data showing that only about 27–28% of annual subscribers renew. The most encodable layer is compliance. FTC disclosure and review rules, Apple's guideline text on reviews and invites (quoted verbatim), CAN-SPAM, mailbox-provider rules and the TCPA translate directly into block and warn rules for every draft Inroad produces. One caveat applies throughout. Network blocks and an exhausted search budget meant only Apple, Android and GitHub pages were read directly, so most benchmarks below are vendor data or readings of search summaries, and they are labeled that way.

## Each consumer model starts from a different default

Inroad's B2B mode assumes one motion: founder-led outbound to a named buyer. Consumer mode needs a router, because the evidence points each business model at a different first channel, growth mechanism, headline metric and binding constraint. The table summarizes the defaults built up in the sections that follow. Those sections carry the citations and provenance labels, and the labels govern how hard Inroad should enforce each number.

| Model | Default first channel | Mechanism to engineer once retention holds | Headline health metric | Binding constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer app / subscription | Gathering spots of a super-specific audience, then ASO, Apple featuring and creators | Creator content amplified as Spark/Partnership ads; referrals paid in product currency | Cohort plateau vs category "good"; annual-plan renewal (~27–28% median, vendor) | Paywall/trial design and store fees |
| DTC / e-commerce | Owned audience and creator seeding before launch | Retention flows timed to second purchase; customer affiliates at 10–25% | CM2/CM3 and MER; 90- and 365-day repeat rate | Contribution margin; retail as the scale channel |
| Marketplace | Direct recruitment of the constrained side, usually supply, in one geography or category | Supply-side referral, single-player tools, risk reversal | Fill/match rate and "zeros" | Liquidity in the atomic market |
| Social / community | One bounded community at a time (school, dorm, team) | Inherent invites, within strict invite rules | Density inside the community; D7 retention; power-user curve | Atomic network size; invite compliance |
| Prosumer tool | Online gathering spots and Product Hunt (inference) | Collaborative and embedded virality | Activation rate; free-to-paid (B2B/prosumer SaaS reference) | Thinnest evidence; founder-content guidance unsourced |

## More than half of early consumer winners went where users already gathered

The most-cited dataset on early consumer acquisition is Lenny Rachitsky's. It began as a May 2020 study of 40+ consumer apps and was revised in July 2022 on the basis of "researching 100+ B2C startups, talking to 30+ founders" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/consumer-business-find-first-users), via search summary). Its headline is that **"the single most popular (over 50%!), effective, and highest-ROI strategy for finding your first 1,000 users is to find an existing gathering spot for your super-specific audience."** Secondary summaries of the 2020 version add that most apps relied on a single strategy and **"no one found success from more than three"** ([Founder's Journal](https://foundersjournalpod.morningbrew.com/how-to-get-your-first-1000-users/)). Outreach to friends and colleagues was a major growth driver for about 20% of startups, also per a secondary summary.

Lenny's 2019 interviews with operators at 17 marketplaces point the same way. The **median marketplace used two supply levers** (mean 2.5), direct sales was the most common, and SEO and paid were "not common" ([Lenny on X](https://twitter.com/lennysan/status/1198997647807275009), via search summary). Paul Graham's 2013 essay sets the operating stance: "You can't wait for users to come to you. You have to go out and get them" ([paulgraham.com](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html), via search summary). The canonical examples are hands-on. Tinder's founders pitched USC sororities and fraternities ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/consumer-business-find-first-users)), Etsy staff recruited sellers at craft fairs, and Dropbox's founder posted a demo video on Hacker News ([echai.ventures recap](https://echai.ventures/stream/how-biggest-apps-got-first-1000-users-lenny-rachitsky-breakdown), secondary). Lenny calls starting with "a *very* narrow audience" one of the most counterintuitive parts of building a large B2C business ([Lenny on X](https://x.com/lennysan/status/1546873103262707712)).

Two channel frameworks turn that pattern into sequencing logic. Brian Balfour's Four Fits holds that products are built for channels, that "channels do not mold to products," and that a channel must fit the business model's ARPU-to-CAC economics ([Balfour, 2025 rewrite](https://blog.brianbalfour.com/p/the-four-fits-a-growth-framework), via search summary). A widely repeated gloss says product-channel fit means **"70%+ of their growth from one channel."** That line comes from secondary write-ups and was not found in Balfour's own text ([GTM Foundations](https://gtmfoundations.substack.com/p/why-product-market-fit-isnt-enough)). Weinberg and Mares' Bullseye process from *Traction* is explicit. Brainstorm at least one idea for each of 19 channels, run small, cheap tests on the two or three most promising, focus on the one or two that clearly outperform, and rerun the exercise when a channel saturates ([Sprints & Sneakers](https://www.sprintsandsneakers.com/insights/the-bullseye-framework-what-is-it-and-how-do-you-apply-it), secondary).

For Inroad this yields a hard structural rule: the daily list of at most five moves should come from **no more than three channels**. During a fixed test window the moves spread across two or three test channels. After the window, at least three of the five go to the channel that won. The default first move for any consumer company is to name the super-specific audience, list where it already gathers online and offline, and draft the post, DM or event pitch for the founder to deliver. That fits Inroad's draft-only model. The stage boundaries are the notes' own synthesis, not sourced numbers: 0 to 1,000 users on unscalable tactics, then 1,000 to 10,000 testing scalable channels such as content, ASO and referral loops, with paid acquisition last.

Community channels come with rules that bind harder than folklore. Reddit's "9:1" self-promotion ratio is an informal Reddiquette rule of thumb. Reddit stopped enforcing the "1 in 10" rule sitewide in 2017, and its current sitewide spam policy contains no ratio ([RedReplier](https://redreplier.com/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rule), secondary quoting r/modnews). What binds is each subreddit's own rules: some ban promotion outright, and some cap it at 10% of a user's activity within that community (secondary). Inroad should therefore require a "subreddit rules checked" confirmation on every Reddit draft. It should keep self-promotion to no more than about 10% of the founder's activity in each subreddit, prefer build-in-public and answer-first formats, and never draft anything that implies sockpuppets or coordinated voting. Reddit's own spam and content-policy pages could not be fetched. No sourced norms were found for Discord servers, Facebook groups or niche forums beyond the platform anti-spam rules covered in the compliance section.

The evidence on organic short-form video is weaker still. The only cadence guidance found is vendor advice to post **1–3 times a day per account**, starting at 1–2 a day for the first one to two weeks ([ReelCarousel](https://www.reelcarousel.app/blog/faceless-tiktok-accounts-promoting-apps), vendor). No independent data links posting volume to installs. The same vendor sources make three platform claims, none verified against the platforms' own policy pages ([ReelFarm](https://reel.farm/guides/faceless-marketing), vendor). TikTok photo posts are not eligible for Creator Rewards. YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" monetization policy reportedly targets image-only slideshows. Realistic AI-generated people need AI labels on YouTube, TikTok and Meta. Inroad can default to one or two drafted posts a day per account for a two-to-four-week test, continued only if reach holds up, but it should present that default as practitioner convention.

### Launch surfaces publish their own numbers

The most reliable launch rules come from Apple, whose developer pages were read directly. App Store Connect accepts three types of featuring nomination: New Content, App Enhancements and App Launch. Apple's help page recommends "a minimum lead time of 3 weeks" ([Apple, Nominate your app](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring)), while its marketing page says "at least two weeks' notice" and up to three months for wider consideration ([Apple, Getting featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)). Inroad should schedule the nomination at least three weeks before launch.

Search ranking combines text relevance (title, subtitle, keywords, primary category) with user behavior, including downloads and ratings. Apple warns that popular terms "are highly competitive – especially if you are a newer or smaller app," which argues for long-tail keywords first. It also warns that trademark or competitor names in keywords are "a common reason for App Store rejections" ([Apple, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)). Apple's own page claims custom product pages bring "an average increase of 2.5 percentage points in conversion" ([Apple, Custom product pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)). That is a platform claim, and the summary's exact framing may be imprecise. The hard limits below can be encoded as validators.

| Surface | Hard limit or rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| App name and subtitle | 30 characters each | [Apple, Product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/) |
| Keyword field | 100 characters, comma-separated, no spaces; no repeats of words in the name, subtitle or category; no plurals of words already included; no "app"; no competitor, trademark or celebrity names | [Apple, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/) |
| Promotional text | 170 characters; does not affect search ranking; editable without a new version | [Apple, Product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/) |

Product Hunt is now editorially curated. Its changelog says "our moderation team still does choose what to feature above the fold" ([Product Hunt changes](https://www.producthunt.com/changes), via search summary). Secondary sources put the featured share at about 10% after a January 2024 change, but they conflict with each other ([Awesome Directories](https://awesome-directories.com/blog/product-hunt-launch-guide-2025-algorithm-changes/), vendor/secondary). Product Hunt's own guide is unambiguous on one rule: "Never ask outright for upvotes or run anything that requires them" ([Product Hunt Launch Guide](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/17350772-launch-guide)). Inroad should treat Product Hunt as optional for mass-consumer products and more relevant for prosumer and tech-savvy audiences.

Kickstarter's overall success rate is about **39–42%** in third-party estimates ([Boost Your Campaign](https://www.boostyourcampaign.com/blog/crowdfunding-statistics-2026), secondary). A vendor relaying a statement by a Kickstarter employee reports that **about 31% of pre-launch followers become backers** and that **about 29% of funding arrives on day one** ([Prelaunch.marketing](https://prelaunch.marketing/blogs/academy/average-conversion-rates-for-kickstarter-followers), vendor; not verified with Kickstarter). From those inputs Inroad can offer a planning heuristic, labeled as vendor-relayed: followers needed ≈ (goal × 0.29) ÷ (0.31 × average pledge).

Waitlist folklore is weakly sourced. Vendor retellings agree that Robinhood had roughly a million people on its pre-launch waitlist, but they conflict on its referral mechanics, and none cites a primary source ([Queueform](https://www.queueform.com/blog/how-did-robinhood-get-1-million-users-before-launch-with-just-a-waitlist), vendor). Superhuman's waitlist is reported as both 180,000 and 275,000 ([Waitlister](https://waitlister.me/growth-hub/case-studies/superhuman), vendor).

### Paid acquisition waits for proof and for budget

In the sources found, paid acquisition rarely kickstarts a consumer company. Performance marketing ranked eighth of ten early supply levers in Lenny's marketplace data ([Lenny on X](https://twitter.com/lennysan/status/1198997653549277184)), and every first-1,000-user tactic he describes is unscalable and unpaid. When a founder does test paid, the binding constraint is the ad platform's learning threshold. Third-party guides say a Meta ad set usually exits the learning phase after **about 50 optimization events within 7 days**, and that changing the budget by more than about 20% resets learning ([AdManage.ai](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-learning-phase-guide); [Pixis.ai](https://pixis.ai/blog/ai-learning-phases-why-advertisers-kill-campaigns-too-early/); both vendor, not verified against Meta). That implies a minimum weekly test budget of roughly 50 × target CPA per ad set, or about $500 a week at a $10 CPA. If that is unaffordable, the founder should optimize for a cheaper upstream event such as trial start.

Measurement is better than the panic after Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) suggested. AppsFlyer reports **about 50% global ATT opt-in** among users who see the prompt (April 2025), and **about 30%** once users who never see it are counted ([AppsFlyer newsroom](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/post-att-growth/); [AppsFlyer blog](https://www.appsflyer.com/blog/measurement-analytics/att-opt-in-measurement/); vendor). Adjust reportedly showed about 35% in Q2 2025 ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/mobile-app-marketing-statistics-2026-install-data), secondary).

For iOS apps, Apple Search Ads is usually the highest-intent first paid test. SplitMetrics' 2025 benchmark across its top-15 categories shows an average **9.7% tap-through rate, 66.2% tap-to-install conversion and $2.25 cost per tap** ([App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/apple-ads-search-results-benchmarks-report-2025/), vendor). AppTweak's 2026 data puts the median cost per tap at $1.91 in the US and $0.92 globally ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/apple-ads-benchmarks), vendor). The two use different methods and should not be combined. Generic cost-per-install benchmarks are unreliable. Liftoff's casual-gaming CPIs ($1.41 on iOS, $0.14 on Android) cover games only ([Liftoff](https://liftoff.ai/2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/), vendor), and a widely repeated 2026 "global average CPI" of $2.24 on iOS could not be verified.

Inroad's paid gate should therefore require three things: evidence of retention or organic pull, enough budget for about 50 optimization events per ad set per week, and an attribution plan. That plan should rest on SKAN/AdAttributionKit aggregates, "how did you hear about us" surveys and incrementality tests rather than user-level ROAS. The PMF section adds the retention conditions that complete the gate. The evidence also supports a set of acquisition anti-patterns, each encodable as a warning on a proposed move: an ICP broader than one super-specific segment; more than three active channels; paid spend before there is retention or organic pull; a paid budget below about 50× target CPA per ad set per week; a community post with no confirmed rules check; competitor names in ASO keywords; a rating prompt wired to a button or not tied to a success moment; a crowdfunding launch with no pre-launch list; and an App Store launch with no featuring nomination submitted at least three weeks ahead.

## Creator programs are cheap searches priced on median views

For a budget of $0–$20K a month, the evidence points to nano creators (about 1K–10K followers) and micro creators (about 10K–100K). Tier boundaries are not standardized, though, and engagement benchmarks vary about tenfold across vendors because they use different cutoffs and denominators. HypeAuditor's 2025 report (vendor; 76M Instagram accounts) finds that nano-influencers make up 76% of Instagram influencers and have the highest engagement of any tier, **2.19%**, against an average Instagram engagement rate of 1.59% in 2024 ([HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/state-of-influencer-marketing-2025/); [HypeAuditor blog](https://blog.hypeauditor.com/2025-influencer-marketing-trends-insights-from-hypeauditor-s-latest-report/)). Another vendor puts Instagram nano engagement at 6.0% ([Qoruz](https://qoruz.com/blog/engagement-rate-benchmarks-to-aim-for-in-2025/), vendor). On TikTok, SociaVault's 2026 medians across 150,000 accounts run from **7.84% for nano down to 1.84% for mega** ([SociaVault](https://sociavault.com/blog/good-engagement-rate-tiktok), vendor), and HypeAuditor's view-based nano figure is 11.6% ([Reply200 summarizing HypeAuditor](https://reply200.com/blog/tiktok-engagement-rate/), secondary). The rule for Inroad is benchmark hygiene. Store every benchmark with its platform, tier cutoffs, year and denominator (followers or views), and compare a creator only with a like-for-like number. No source linked any engagement threshold to sales.

Practitioners score creators on fit and views instead. Cal AI's co-founder favored creators with "very tight communities" and used a rough 50K-follower minimum, while weighting views more heavily ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/)). The team priced deals on the **median** views of a creator's recent videos ([Jake Castillo on X](https://x.com/jakecastilloooo/article/2104597759252717610), via search summary). Inroad's selection score should therefore weight six signals: audience fit, median views of the last 10–12 posts, views-to-followers ratio, how specific the comments are, how recently the creator posted, and whether past sponsorships were properly disclosed. Follower count should serve only as a filter. Red flags include followers with no profile photo or posts, generic comments, and mismatches such as 100,000 followers drawing about 100 likes per post ([Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/blog/influencer-marketing/fake-followers-and-likes-on-instagram/)). The FTC says it does not intend to hold brands vicariously liable for unknowingly hiring an influencer with fake followers. Liability attaches when the buyer "knew or should have known" ([BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/what-the-ftcs-final-rule-means-for-fake-indicators-of-social-media-influence-and-fake-review-websites.html)).

No rigorous study compares creator reply rates for email and DMs ([ClickAnalytic](https://www.clickanalytic.com/es/influencer-outreach-email-vs-dm/)), so outreach rules rest on practitioner consensus. Use a listed business email. DM first on TikTok, where creators live in the app. Write to a listed manager rather than going around them. Keep the first touch under about 200 words with a single firm call to action ([Elev8or](https://www.elev8or.io/blog/influencer-outreach-email-templates), vendor). Modash, a vendor, reports that successful programs template about 80% of each message and spend 5–8 minutes personalizing it ([Modash](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-outreach-templates)). Its survey found that **72% of marketers send one or two follow-ups**, and one practitioner it published sends "two follow-ups, max": the first a week after the opening message, the second two weeks later ([Modash newsletter](https://newsletter.modash.io/p/influencer-outreach)).

Reply-rate figures are vendor rules of thumb with no stated methodology: 8–20% overall, or nano 40–60%, micro 15–25% and macro under 5% without an introduction through a manager ([StarNgage](https://starngage.pro/blog/email-vs-dm-vs-creator-marketplaces-analyzing-outreach-response-rates-for-maximum-campaign-success), vendor). Inroad should plan on 10–25% for cold outreach to nano and micro creators, and label that range unverified.

Payment terms are an outreach lever in their own right. **41% of creators** name payment delays as their biggest problem with brands (Influencer & Crowd DNA, March 2025, via eMarketer; [Gigapay](https://www.gigapay.com/blog/why-creators-put-fast-paying-brands-first)). **87%** have been paid late, paid the wrong amount or not paid at all (Tipalti & Lumanu survey; [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/new-campaign-report-reveals-why-creators-arent-getting-paid-time/1930824)). And 74% have stopped working with a brand after feeling undervalued (Digiday, 2024, via [CreatorPulse](https://creatorpulse.io/blog/why-creators-stop-working-with-brands)).

Every Inroad outreach draft should therefore say who the founder is and cite one specific recent post by the creator. It should state the offer (product, fee, commission, or "paid, share your rate"), the deliverables, usage rights and timeline, the disclosure expectation, and the payment terms, where the notes recommend paying within 7–15 days. A draft should never offer "exposure," imply that a gift obliges a positive post, or ask for a free test video. It should follow up at most twice, and never after a no. TikTok's standalone Creator Marketplace stopped taking new campaigns after 10 March 2025 and now operates inside TikTok One. It offers a single invite with no follow-up, which suits one-off buys more than ongoing relationships ([Insense](https://insense.pro/blog/tiktok-creator-marketplace), secondary).

Early programs move up a ladder from gifting to paid deals to affiliate commissions to paid amplification. The planning numbers below are almost entirely vendor or marketplace data.

Cal AI's arc is the best-documented recent case, and every number in it is founder-reported and unaudited. Its first paid story post reportedly cost about $2,000 and drove about 1,000 downloads in two days, about 100 of which became paying users ([Founder Bureau](https://founderbureau.com/news/cal-ais-30-million-rise-fueled-by-a-network-of-micro-influencers); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/cal-ai-s-viral-playbook-from-one-influencer-story-post-to-a-10m-second-act)). That is roughly $20 per paying user, from a single anecdote. The program then grew to about 250 creators on retainer, priced on views. Running influencer videos as ads capped profitable spend at about **$5,000 a day**, which pushed the team to commission creator content made for ads. Influencers reportedly drove about $2M a month in revenue, and paid ads lifted that to about $5.7M ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/)). Reported revenue ranges from "$30M+ ARR" to "$50M ARR," and accounts give different dates for the MyFitnessPal acquisition ([Virlo](https://signal.virlo.ai/p/cal-ai-myfitnesspal-acquisition-growth-breakdown)).

The pattern generalizes into an arc Inroad can encode. Start with a tiny, instrumented test. Scale the creators and formats that work, priced on views. Amplify the winners as Spark or Partnership ads once the rights are secured. Commission ad-specific content once organic reposts saturate. The evidence for amplification is dated and comes from the platforms. A 2022 TikTok performance-auction test reported **37% lower CPA and 69% higher conversion** for Spark Ads ([Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/tiktok-music-promotion/tiktok-spark-ads-vs-non-spark-ads), secondhand). The "up to 30% CPA reduction" claimed for Meta Partnership Ads appears only in unsourced blogs. Mechanically, TikTok Spark authorizations last 7, 30, 60 or 365 days, and a caption reportedly cannot be edited once authorized, so the disclosure has to be in place first ([JoinBrands help](https://help.joinbrands.com/en/articles/6602080-tiktok-spark-ad-code)). Meta Partnership Ads need the creator's permission, which can be revoked ([Later](https://later.com/blog/branded-content-ads/)).

Briefs should give structure, not scripts. A good brief offers three to six hook options, at least two of which differ in angle, plus three to five ranked key messages, with each video doing one job. It adds a short do/don't list, a list of approved claims and a mandatory disclosure block ([Influee](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-brief-template); [Hustler Marketing](https://www.hustlermarketing.com/blog/how-to-write-a-ugc-creator-brief-that-actually-gets-good-content/)). The claims list matters because the FTC holds advertisers liable for misleading endorsements, and non-expert endorsers liable for unsubstantiated performance claims ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2023/07/ftc-endorsement-guides)).

Measurement needs three layers: a unique code and link for each creator, a "how did you hear about us" question at checkout or onboarding with a "which creator?" follow-up, and platform metrics for amplified posts. Codes and links "provide the cleanest direct attribution available" but "undercount actual influence" ([Fairing](https://fairing.co/resources/measurement-faqs/how-do-you-measure-influencer-marketing-performance), vendor). No source measured the size of that undercount. For apps, an attribution vendor recommends one link per creator and a **14–30-day attribution window**, tied to trials and subscriptions rather than installs ([Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/blog/influencer-attribution-tracking-promo-code-vs-tracking-link-mobile-app), vendor). Inroad should report two CACs per creator: direct CAC, from codes and links, and blended CAC, which adds survey mentions. It should judge creators mostly on blended CAC and on how their content performs as paid ads.

Campus and ambassador programs suit network-effect products that need density. In 2022, BeReal reportedly paid students $30 per referral and $50 for a download plus a review ([Brown Daily Herald](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/02/new-social-media-app-tells-students-to-bereal)), and TechCrunch reported that at least some of its growth was manufactured ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/22/bereal-hype-or-hit-what-to-know-about-the-gen-z-photo-sharing-app-climbing-the-charts/)). Inroad should pay ambassadors for measured activation, such as "five friends added" or activity in week two, and never for reviews, which now conflict with app-store and FTC rules. For DTC brands, the cheaper route is an affiliate program for existing customers at 10–20%. Enterprise platforms such as GRIN or Aspire, estimated in competitor blogs at roughly $1K–$2.5K or more a month, should wait until creator spend justifies them ([Social Snowball](https://socialsnowball.io/post/grin-vs-social-snowball); [SARAL](https://www.getsaral.com/academy/shopify-collabs-alternatives)).

## Virality multiplies other channels, and marketplaces start on the scarce side

The viral coefficient K is the number of invites (or exposures) per user times the conversion rate of those invites, compounded once per viral cycle. David Skok's long-standing argument is that cycle time matters more than K itself ([P2P Foundation wiki](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Virality_Coefficient), secondary). His worked example is **recalled, not re-verified this session**, though its arithmetic checks out with ten seed users and K = 2. Over 20 days, a two-day cycle yields 20,470 users, and a one-day cycle yields more than 20 million ([Skok, forentrepreneurs.com](https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/lessons-learnt-viral-marketing/), fetch blocked).

Secondary sources put realistic K for consumer products at **0.15–0.25** and call sustained K above 1 rare and temporary. The common attribution of that range to Andrew Chen is unverified ([eChai](https://echai.ventures/startingup/retention-referral/what-s-a-viral-coefficient-and-is-chasing-a-k-factor-above-1-realistic-for-my-kind-of-product), secondary). In practice, Inroad should always show a second number next to K: the amplification factor. When K < 1, a seed cohort of N₀ users eventually yields N₀ ÷ (1 − K) users in total. So K = 0.2 multiplies every other channel by 1.25×, K = 0.5 by 2× and K = 0.8 by 5×.

Inroad should flag any plan that relies on K > 1 beyond a launch window. It should push founders to shorten cycle time before trying to raise K, by putting the share moment at or just after the aha moment and deep-linking invitees past install walls. It should also classify the product's dominant viral type, because each type has different levers. Inherent virality is the Cash App case: Block's FY2023 10-K says "every time a customer sends or requests money, Cash App can potentially acquire a new customer" ([Block 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1512673/000162828024006354/sq-20231231.htm)). Collaborative virality is the Dropbox case, where the S-1 credits content shared with non-registered users ([Dropbox S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001467623/000119312518055809/d451946ds1.htm)). Embedded virality is the Hotmail signature, though its widely quoted "12 million users in 18 months" has no primary source ([TechCrunch excerpt](https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/18/ps-i-love-you-get-your-free-email-at-hotmail)). The remaining types are incentivized referrals and organic word of mouth.

The Reforge series by Balfour, Winters, Kwok and Chen argues that funnels "do not accurately represent how the fastest growing products grow" and that teams should "focus on growth loops" ([Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops), via search summary). Its taxonomy of loop types (viral, content/UGC-SEO, paid, sales) was recalled rather than retrieved. Inroad's procedure for finding the primary loop is an inference from these sources. First, list every way new users arrive. Then classify each source as a loop, meaning it was produced by existing users or by the product's output, or as a linear channel. The loop that brings the largest share of new activated users is primary, and its leakiest step becomes the weekly focus metric.

Public filings show what mature loops look like. Dropbox spent only **28% of revenue on sales and marketing in 2017**, and more than 90% of its revenue came through self-serve channels ([Motley Fool on the S-1](https://www.fool.com/investing/2018/02/28/dropbox-ipo-3-important-takeaways.aspx)). Block reported Cash App's acquisition cost, defined as paid marketing (including referral fees) divided by net-new transacting actives, at **under $5 in 2020** and "$10 or less" in 2022 ([Square Q4 2020 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1512673/000119312521052320/d128971dex991.htm); [Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/block-defends-higher-marketing-spending/619452/)). Loops amplify retention and cannot substitute for it, so loop engineering waits until the retention curve flattens.

Lenny Rachitsky's referral guidance, drawn from practitioner experience rather than a dataset, sets the target band. "At companies where referrals work best, anywhere from **15% to 50%** of their new user growth comes from referrals." He adds that fewer than 10% of users will make a successful referral, and that an extra $10 "likely won't cut it" as an incentive ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/this-week-16-building-a-referrals)). A summary of the same guidance says referral programs "add fuel to existing word-of-mouth; they can't create it."

The best-documented cases come from founder decks and SEC filings. Drew Houston's 2010 deck says that after paid search failed, a two-sided referral incentive "permanently increased signups by 60%" ([SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/vishaltx/dropbox-startup-lessons-learned-16991502)). Goodwater Capital's analysis attributes 35% of Dropbox's daily signups to referrals and 20% to viral features in 2008–2010 ([Goodwater](https://www.goodwatercap.com/perspectives/understanding-dropbox)). Robinhood's S-1 says **more than 80% of new funded accounts** in 2020 and Q1 2021 came organically or through referrals, without separating the two. Its reward was a lottery-like free stock worth $2.50–$225, with about 98% of users receiving $2.50–$10, capped at **$500 per user per year** ([Robinhood S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000162828021013318/robinhoods-1.htm)). PayPal paid both sides $20, then $10, then $5, and one account says cutting to $5 each showed no substantial impact on growth ([ReferralCandy](https://www.referralcandy.com/blog/paypal), secondary). Its often-quoted "7–10% daily growth" is unsourced.

The encodable design rules follow from these cases. Make referrals double-sided by default. Pay in the product's own currency, such as storage, stock or credit, unless the product is money. Size the reward against LTV and A/B test two or three levels. Pay out on a qualifying action, such as a funded account or first purchase, rather than on signup, and cap rewards per year. Ask after the first success moment; this is an inference, since no A/B-tested timing benchmark was found. Design for power referrers rather than assuming a uniform referral rate.

Fraud controls are standard practice, though no published benchmark exists. They include holding payouts past refund and chargeback windows, deduplicating by device, payment instrument, phone and identity, blocking self-referral, setting velocity limits and keeping clawback terms. If referrals stay below about 10% of new users after optimization, Inroad should point the founder to product and activation work rather than bigger rewards.

Scarcity is a launch tactic, and its value decays. Gmail invites sold for up to $150 on eBay in 2004, then fell to $2–5 after a new invite round in early June ([Wikipedia, History of Gmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Gmail), citing PC World). Clubhouse's global downloads peaked at 9.6M in February 2021 and had fallen **90.6%** by April, per Sensor Tower estimates ([WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/feed/clubhouse-hype-dissipates-as-downloads-and-ranking-recede/en-GB/2008)). The app dropped invites in July 2021 amid competition ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/clubhouse-opens-to-everyone-ends-invite-only-policy)). Waitlists earn their keep as fit filters. Superhuman used a pre-signup survey to keep out users it could not yet serve. Re-segmenting alone raised its Sean Ellis score from 22% to 33% (32% in one summary), and it later reached 58% ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)).

Inroad's waitlist rules are as follows. Show each person their queue position and let referrals move them up, rewarding with earlier access rather than cash. Use the signup form to collect segmentation data, and admit cohorts in order of fit, not signup order. Time-box the gate to weeks or a few months. Open before competitors copy the product, or before a platform gap caps the market, as the lack of an Android app did for Clubhouse. Slow admissions when waitlist-to-activation conversion or new-cohort retention drops. The backfire signal to watch is hype metrics rising while week-1 and week-4 retention of admitted cohorts falls.

NFX says it has identified 16 types of network effects and attributes 70% of the value created by tech companies since 1994 to network effects, based on its own three-year study ([NFX](https://nfx.com/post/network-effects-manual), self-reported). The notes' version of the NFX category list, and the argument that data network effects are weaker than founders claim, were recalled rather than retrieved. Andrew Chen's *The Cold Start Problem* (2021, based on 100+ founder interviews) supplies the operating unit: the **atomic network**, "the minimum viable network of users needed for a stable, usable product to scale" ([Frog Capital review](https://frogcapital.com/think-frog/the-cold-start-problem-by-andrew-chen/)). Chen sized it in a 2022 interview at about **2–3 people for Zoom and 5–10 for Slack** ([Food On Demand](https://foodondemand.com/01062022/andrew-chen-examines-the-cold-start-problem-for-platforms-like-uber-snackpass/)). He also stresses winning the "hard side" first: the roughly 1% of users who do most of the work ([YouExec summary](https://youexec.com/book-summaries/the-cold-start-problem-by-andrew-chen), secondary). Before launch, Inroad should require every network product to define its atomic network in three parts: a minimum user count, a shared unit (team, dorm, school, city block or category) and a weekly activity threshold.

Social products show the density mechanic at campus scale, with anecdotal numbers. Dustin Moskovitz recalls 1,200–1,500 Harvard registrants in Facebook's first 24 hours, though estimates of first-month penetration conflict ([History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/2004-facebook-founded-mark-zuckerberg/)). tbh reportedly reached about 40% of its first Georgia high school within a day or two, then rolled out one school at a time ([Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/tbh-doing-things-that-dont-scale/), secondary). Fizz reported 5,100 Stanford users with about 80% weekly active in January 2022 (founder figures, [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2022/01/23/from-buzz-to-fizz-anonymous-social-platform-takes-off-at-stanford/)). BeReal's daily users fell to about 6M by late spring 2023 after its paid-ambassador growth ([Wikipedia, BeReal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeReal)).

The density rules are as follows. Pick one bounded community small enough to saturate in days. Build anticipation, then open to everyone at once; before launch, the tbh/Gas team accepted no follow requests on the app's social account, then accepted them all on launch day ([Raymond Chai, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/raymondchai_nikita-bier-pulled-off-one-crazy-viral-launch-activity-7114171678456233984-FrvO), secondary). Treat reaching 40% or more of the community within 48 hours as an aspirational anecdote, not a norm. Expand to adjacent communities only after strong day-7 retention.

Marketplace cold start inverts the default. Lenny's research found that **14 of 17 marketplaces** focused on supply first; Rover was among the few that were demand-constrained, because "Supply was easy" ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-kickstart-and-scale-a-marketplace)). Secondary summaries say 16 of 17 constrained the market by geography or category before expanding ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-supply-acquisition), secondary).

Early seeding was manual. DoorDash began as a site with roughly eight restaurant PDF menus and a Google Voice number, with the co-founders as the only drivers and a flat $6 fee. It made its first delivery on January 12, 2013 ([Vator](https://vator.tv/2019-01-04-when-doordash-was-young-the-early-years/)). Airbnb's founders photographed New York listings door to door; the widely repeated 2–3× booking lift has no primary Airbnb source ([Snappr](https://www.snappr.co/enterprise-blog/photography-that-launched-an-empire-how-airbnb-transformed-their-business-with-professional-photography), vendor). Poshmark ran about four virtual "Posh Parties" a day, with active users about 17% above normal during them, and grew local meetups from 162 events in 2018 to 546 projected for 2019 ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/startups/poshmarks-s-1-highlights-cutting-marketing-expenses-led-to-profitability/); [Modern Retail, 2019](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/how-poshmark-uses-small-local-events-to-drive-engagement-and-retention/)). Faire took the risk off the demand side with net-60 terms and free returns on a retailer's first order with each brand. It also charges brands no commission on orders from retailers they bring in through Faire Direct links ([Faire](https://www.faire.com/support/retailers); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/faire)).

Liquidity should be tracked as a match or fill rate within a window that suits the category: minutes for ride-hailing, days or weeks for high-friction categories ([Point Nine](https://writing.pointnine.com/p/wtf-is-marketplace-liquidity)). a16z's "zeros", sessions where a user shows intent but gets no match, make a good daily alert ([a16z](https://a16z.com/13-metrics-for-marketplace-companies/)). No universal threshold is verified. The often-cited "60% search-to-fill" claim could not be traced to Marketplace Pulse, its claimed source ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/marketplace-liquidity-the-physics-of-supply-and-demand-matching/)). Inroad should set a baseline in week one, require week-over-week improvement inside the constrained market, and allow expansion into new geographies or categories only once fill and repeat rates are stable. It should flag Craigslist-style cross-posting, and listing suppliers without permission, as historical tactics that carry terms-of-service and legal risk today.

## A flattening retention curve, not a launch spike, unlocks scale

Two PMF tests can be encoded. The survey test asks "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?" and treats **40% "very disappointed"** as the line. In Rahul Vohra's account, companies that struggled to find growth "almost always had less than 40%" ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)). Who gets surveyed matters. Superhuman surveyed only users who had "used Superhuman at least twice in the last two weeks" ([Founder Labs](https://writings.founderlabs.io/p/how-superhuman-went-from-22-to-58), secondary). One write-up stresses that the threshold is a rule of thumb from Ellis's experience, not a law ([IdeaPlan](https://www.ideaplan.io/case-studies/superhuman-product-market-fit), secondary). In 2015, Hiten Shah asked 731 Slack users the same question and 51% said "very disappointed" ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/), per extract).

Superhuman's engine raised its score from 22% to 33% by narrowing to the personas who were already very disappointed. It then reached 58% by mining what those high-expectation customers loved and fixing what held back the "somewhat disappointed" users for whom the main benefit, speed, resonated. It politely ignored everyone else. Inroad should compute the score only over eligible respondents, both overall and for the top persona. It should always show the sample size and a confidence interval. At n = 40 the 95% interval is about ±15 points, so 34% on 40 responses is statistically indistinguishable from 40%; this calculation is the notes' own.

The behavioral test is a cohort curve that stops decaying and plateaus at or above category benchmarks. Lenny Rachitsky and Casey Winters' survey of practitioners supplies the benchmarks Inroad should encode ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29); [Casey Winters](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/what-is-good-retention-an-exhaustive-benchmark-study-with-lenny-rachitsky); both via search summary). The six-month window comes from a secondary summary ([Founders Fuel](https://foundersfuel.beehiiv.com/p/good-great-retention)). The two SaaS rows are the nearest references for prosumer tools; that mapping is an inference.

| Category | "Good" user retention | "Great" user retention |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer social | ~25% | ~45% |
| Consumer transactional | ~30% | ~50% |
| Consumer SaaS | ~40% | ~70% |
| SMB / mid-market SaaS | ~60% | ~80% |
| Consumer SaaS net revenue retention | ~55% | ~80% |

The post itself includes a section on why low retention can be acceptable, so these numbers are context, not pass/fail lines. The 2015 Andrew Chen and Quettra data, from more than 125M Android phones, still frames the first days. The average app lost **77% of its DAUs within three days** of install and 90% within 30. Top apps lost users at about the same rate after day one ([Andrew Chen, 2015](https://andrewchen.com/new-data-shows-why-losing-80-of-your-mobile-users-is-normal-and-that-the-best-apps-do-much-better/)), so the biggest early lever is the value delivered on days 0 and 1.

For consumer subscription, a16z calls retaining **30–40% of users and revenue by the end of the first year** "best practice." It notes that the fastest-growing consumer AI companies now show revenue retention above 100% ([a16z](https://a16z.com/the-great-expansion-a-new-era-of-consumer-software/)). It also proposes reading AI-era cohorts as M12/M3, treating month three as the committed base so that early "tourist" users don't distort the result ([a16z](https://a16z.com/ai-retention-benchmarks/)). Inroad should call a curve "flat" when the loss from one period to the next stays near zero, within a tunable 1–2 points, for three consecutive periods. It should compare the plateau with the category's "good" value and recheck it by channel, because users acquired through paid channels arrive with different intent ([Manuel Fusco](https://manuelfusco.substack.com/p/retention-curve-plateau-before-scaling), secondary).

For engagement, the Power User Curve is a better diagnostic than DAU/MAU. It is a histogram of users by the number of days they were active in a week (L7) or a month (L28/L30) ([Andrew Chen](https://andrewchen.com/power-user-curve/)). A hump at the right end, and a rightward shift across successive cohorts, are the healthy signs ([a16z](https://a16z.com/16-ways-to-measure-network-effects/)). The familiar DAU/MAU bands (10–20% typical, 50%+ exceptional) could not be traced to a primary source. Inroad should show DAU/MAU only for products meant for daily use, and label those bands as heuristics.

Activation benchmarks come from Lenny Rachitsky and Yuriy Timen's survey of 500+ products. Activation rate, users who reach the activation milestone divided by users who complete signup, **averages 34%, with a 25% median**; for SaaS alone the figures are 36% and 30%. Marketplaces and e-commerce come out lowest, because their milestone is usually a first purchase ([Lenny on LinkedIn, October 2022](https://rw.linkedin.com/posts/lennyrachitsky_what-is-a-good-activation-rate-activity-6990690292412665856-S_du?trk=public_profile_like_view), via search summary).

The two famous aha numbers trace back to their founders, but both are simplified rules of thumb. One is Chamath Palihapitiya's "get any individual to 7 friends in 10 days" for Facebook ([Startup Archive](https://www.startuparchive.org/p/chamath-palihapitiya-on-the-growth-principles-that-got-facebook-to-billions-of-users)), which secondary accounts place at the 2012 Growth Hackers Conference. The other is Stewart Butterfield's observation that teams who had exchanged 2,000 messages had "actually tried Slack," and that 93% of them were still using it ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/from-0-to-1b-slacks-founder-shares-their-epic-launch-strategy/), via extract). Secondary sources distort the Slack figure into "93% paid conversion."

Mode's critique that aha moments are "often round numbers picked in the middle of a range" argues for computing a threshold rather than borrowing one ([Mode](https://mode.com/blog/facebook-aha-moment-simpler-than-you-think/)). The method has four steps. Pick a retention outcome, such as activity in week 4 or week 8, or a second purchase within 60 days. Test candidate thresholds of the form "at least N actions within D days" for both retention lift and coverage. Choose the threshold at the elbow of that curve. Then revalidate it on a later cohort. RevenueCat finds that **55% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on Day 0** ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps), vendor). That is why the weekly review must always include a first-session funnel, with median and p75 time to aha.

Inroad's B2B deal pipeline should give way to Jonathan Hsu's growth accounting. Each period's active users split into new, retained, resurrected and churned, and the **Quick Ratio, (new + resurrected) ÷ churned, must exceed 1** for the product to grow ([Hsu, Social Capital](https://medium.com/swlh/diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth-4a8a449fddfc)). Hsu calls a ratio of 1 to 1.5 "a so-so situation for a consumer app" and treats about 1.5 as average for consumer. He says subscription businesses should run higher, prefers a ratio above 4 for enterprise SaaS, and warned in a 2015 deck that below 2 "your churn is probably too high" ([Hsu Part 2](https://medium.com/swlh/diligence-at-social-capital-part-2-accounting-for-revenue-growth-551fa07dd972); [WMD 2015 deck](https://www.slideshare.net/500startups/wmd-2015-jonathan-hsu)). The bands Inroad can display are inferences built on Hsu: red below 1, amber from 1 to 1.5, green above 1.5, and a ratio of at least 2 as the healthier bar for subscriptions. The ratio should sit next to gross churn, because heavy paid acquisition can mask churn.

The headline number should be a North Star metric chosen according to Amplitude's "game" (attention, transaction or productivity) and broken into breadth, depth, frequency and efficiency inputs. Instacart's "total monthly items received on time" is the worked example ([Amplitude North Star Playbook](https://info.amplitude.com/rs/138-CDN-550/images/Amplitude-The-North-Star-Playbook.pdf)). Each experiment attaches to one input.

The weekly operating cadence can follow Sean Ellis's growth meeting, from *Hacking Growth* and his 2016 SaaStr talk. It runs 60 minutes, is held on Tuesdays, and is run by the growth lead, with ideas scored by ICE beforehand ([Graham Mann's notes](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/hacking-growth-sean-ellis); [Ellis, SaaStr 2016 slides](https://www.slideshare.net/seanellis/building-a-companywide-growth-culture-saastr-annual-2016/14-Weekly_Growth_Meeting_Agenda_15)). RICE, scored as (Reach × Impact × Confidence) ÷ Effort, is the alternative when reach varies widely. Impact is rated 3, 2, 1, 0.5 or 0.25, and confidence 100%, 80% or 50% ([Tempo](https://www.tempo.io/guides/rice-score-prioritization-framework-product-management), secondary; the original Intercom post was not retrieved). The block order and timings below are Ellis's. The consumer-mode content is the notes' adaptation.

| Ellis block | Minutes | Consumer-mode content for Inroad |
|---|---|---|
| Metrics review and focus area | 15 | North Star and its inputs; growth-accounting waterfall and Quick Ratio; newest cohort's plateau vs the category "good"; first-session funnel and time to aha |
| Last week's tests | 10 | Shipped vs not shipped; completion of daily moves |
| Key learnings | 15 | Experiment readouts plus insights from user conversations |
| Select this cycle's tests | 15 | ICE, or RICE when reach varies; each test tied to one North Star input |
| Idea pipeline health | 5 | Backlog growth by channel and loop |

Most seed-stage consumer startups should not run classic A/B tests on retention metrics. Ronny Kohavi's rule of thumb on Lenny's Podcast (July 2023) is that "unless you have at least tens of thousands of users, the math, the statistics just don't work out" ([Lenny's Podcast Wiki](https://lennyrachitsky.wiki/articles/ab-testing)). Secondary recaps describe about 200,000 users as the "magical place" for detecting changes of around 5%, though the recaps conflict on that pairing ([Medium](https://medium.com/@shenjiejie2017/lessons-from-ronny-kohavi-on-a-b-testing-039aabed6543)). GrowthBook's notes from a Kohavi webinar list about 100,000 users for a 10% baseline conversion rate, and say that with 10,000 users "you are nowhere unless your metric converts at around 50%" ([GrowthBook](https://www.growthbook.io/blog/lessons-learned-from-ronny-kohavi-and-luke-sonnet-running-trustworthy-experiments)).

The standard approximation n ≈ 16·p(1−p)/δ² per arm, at α = 0.05 and 80% power, reproduces that order of magnitude. Detecting a 5% relative lift on a 10% baseline takes about 57,600 users per arm, while a 20% relative lift on a 30% baseline takes only about 930. Inroad's gate follows: if eligible weekly users ÷ number of arms × 4 weeks falls short of n at a realistic minimum detectable effect (default 10% relative), it should recommend something other than an A/B test. The alternatives are big swings, before/after cohort comparisons and qualitative research. When a test does run, it should target only leading indicators such as activation or D1/D7 retention.

User research needs its own floor. Teresa Torres defines continuous discovery as "at a minimum, weekly touchpoints with customers," grounded in specific past stories ([Maze](https://maze.co/blog/making-better-product-decisions-with-teresa-torres/)). The Moesta/Spiek switch interview models adoption as Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit and reconstructs recent switchers' timelines ([jobstobedone.org](https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/)). Inroad can default pre-PMF founders to three to five conversations a week across newly activated, power and churned users. That target is an inference that goes beyond Torres's weekly minimum. Interviews should be recruited through behavioral triggers, with a $25–$50 gift card for 30 minutes. That amount is the midpoint of conflicting vendor ranges, which one guide says plateau around $50 ([Lyssna](https://www.lyssna.com/blog/incentives-for-research-participants/); [Koji](https://www.koji.so/docs/research-participant-incentives)).

Ellis and Balfour agree on the sequence. Ellis puts PMF at the base of his growth pyramid and asks whether a stall is "an acquisition problem, is it a conversion problem, or is it a core product problem" ([Mixpanel](https://mixpanel.com/blog/sean-ellis-growth-hacking-2)). Balfour says teams "probably only have enough resources to work on retention or scaling up acquisition" ([Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/podcasts/podcast-brian-balfour-on-creating-meaningful-growth)). No source gives a numeric consumer threshold for starting paid acquisition or hiring for growth, so the gate below is an inference assembled from the sourced benchmarks.

Inroad should block or warn on "scale paid spend" unless six conditions all hold. The latest three or more cohorts must flatten. The plateau must meet the category's "good" level at month six; for subscriptions, annual-plan retention after year one should be at least about 27% (the RevenueCat median) and ideally 30–40% (a16z). The Ellis score must be at least 40% on n ≥ 40 in the target segment. The Quick Ratio must exceed 1 without counting paid users. A separately tracked paid cohort must also flatten. And the paid cohort's LTV must cover CAC within the founder's payback tolerance. A dedicated growth hire should wait for the PMF signals plus one repeatable channel. For fundraising context, a16z notes that Series A companies "often have no more than 12 months of usage and retention data," which is why M12/M3 matters ([a16z](https://a16z.com/revenue-benchmarks-ai-apps/)). No 2024–2026 growth-rate bar for consumer seed or Series A rounds was sourced, so any weekly growth target Inroad shows must be set by the founder.

## Annual renewals near 27% hold subscription LTV near 1.4x year one

Subscription monetization benchmarks come almost entirely from vendors measuring their own customers. The largest dataset is RevenueCat's *State of Subscription Apps 2026*, which covers about 115,000 apps and $16B+ in revenue by RevenueCat's account; TechCrunch described 75,000+ developers and $11B+, a discrepancy that remains unresolved ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)). Its headline is that hard paywalls reach a median **10.7% conversion by Day 35, versus 2.1% for freemium**. That figure is probably download-to-paid, but summaries also call it trial-to-paid, so the definition needs checking. Meanwhile, one-year retention of annual subscribers is **27% versus 28%**, a difference RevenueCat calls statistically negligible ([RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Trials of 17–32 days convert at a **42.5% median, versus 25.5% for trials under four days**. Yet 55% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on Day 0, and about 46% of apps are moving to trials of four days or fewer (same source).

Adapty's vendor data adds that onboarding paywalls convert 0.85% of views, versus 0.23% for paywalls shown later. It also finds that 93% of first purchases happen on install day, though later paywalls still produce 38% of subscription revenue ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/state-of-paywalls/)). Superwall found that multi-page onboarding paywalls converted at 12.41%, versus 9.07% for single-page ones, across 40M+ paywall opens in February–May 2026 ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/new-postmulti-page-onboarding-paywalls-convert-37-better-than-single-page-heres-why), vendor).

Plan mix shapes LTV. RevenueCat's 2025 report found "cheap annual plans retain up to 36% of users after a year, while high-priced monthly plans retain just 6.7%" ([RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch)). Adapty finds that monthly subscribers retain at twice the rate of weekly ones after 12 months ([PPC Land on Adapty](https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/)). AI apps keep 21.1% of annual subscribers at 12 months, versus 30.7% for non-AI apps ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/10/ai-powered-apps-struggle-with-long-term-retention-new-report-shows)). Refunds run 3–4% in most categories, with a median of 3.4% in North America and 7.7% in India and Southeast Asia ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)).

These numbers give Inroad four default positions. First, start new apps with an onboarding paywall and keep a secondary paywall inside the app. Second, push back when a founder defaults to a 3-day trial "to speed up revenue," and recommend testing trial length on revenue per install at D35/D60 rather than on trial-to-paid alone. Third, push back on plan mixes heavy in weekly or high-priced monthly plans for products meant to build LTV. Fourth, model LTV on observed renewals. At an annual renewal rate r of about 0.27–0.28, LTV ≈ net annual price × 1 ÷ (1 − r), or about **1.4× the first-year net price**, not a multi-year multiple. Inroad should flag any model that assumes renewal above about 35% without the app's own data.

Base rates argue for humility. Only **17.3% of new subscription apps reach $1K MRR within two years**, and 4.6% reach $10K. Monthly launches rose from about 2,000 in January 2022 to 14,700 in January 2026, and apps launched in 2025 or later earn about 3% of subscription revenue ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Adapty finds that 95% of subscription revenue goes to the top 10% of apps. It also ranks locale tests (translation and currency) highest for LTV uplift at 62.3%, and raw price-change tests last ([PPC Land on Adapty](https://ppc.land/95-of-app-subscription-revenue-goes-to-top-10-adaptys-2026-benchmark-report/)). Inroad should therefore run locale and paywall-format tests before testing price points.

Web checkout pays off only if its conversion holds up. With card processing at 2.9% + $0.30, web checkout needs about **90% of in-app conversion to break even against a 15% Apple fee**, and about 74% against a 30% fee ([Built by Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/app-link-out-checkout-6-rates-2026); the notes derive the 74% figure from a $10 sale). In Superwall's early test in mid-2025, conversions fell about 11.9% on the web while net proceeds rose about 19.85% ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/initial-data-is-in-app-to-web-conversion-rates-after-the-app-store-ruling), vendor). In-app purchase must stay available alongside the link, and existing Apple-billed subscribers cannot move to web billing without cancelling and resubscribing ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-anti-steering-ruling-monetization-strategy), vendor). Inroad should flag the risk that a court-set fee or the Supreme Court ruling changes this math in 2027.

Freemium benchmarks need explicit denominators, because they differ about tenfold depending on the definition. The "2–5%" folklore comes mostly from B2B and prosumer SaaS. Lenny Rachitsky and Kyle Poyar's 2023 data on 1,000+ mostly B2B and prosumer products measures the share of new accounts paying within six months. They rate **3–5% good and 6–8% great for self-serve freemium**, and 8–12% good and 15–25% great for free trials ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-a-good-free-to-paid-conversion)). That is the most relevant reference for prosumer tools. ChartMogul's report rates reverse trials 4–6% good and 8–12% great on a small sample, and puts trials that require a credit card at about 30% ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)).

For consumer mobile freemium, the reference point is RevenueCat's 2.1% Day-35 median. The ceiling at scale is Duolingo, with **12.7M paid subscribers on 140.6M MAU in Q2 2026, about 9.0%** ([Duolingo Q2 2026 letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/3c8277ee-bc94-4f5d-9b77-0db3e46f88b8)). That ratio is the notes' calculation and is approximate, because MAU is a quarterly average. From these figures the notes infer a ladder for paying users ÷ MAU: under 2% weak, 2–5% typical, 8–9% best in class at scale. B2B ranges should carry the label "SaaS reference, not consumer."

The famous unit-economics thresholds are imported from B2B. The 3:1 LTV:CAC rule is credited to David Skok's SaaS Metrics (around 2010), as a rule of thumb from mature SaaS companies ([Foundry CRO](https://foundrycro.com/blog/ltv-cac-ratio-benchmarks-2026/), secondary). Payback targets come from SaaS too: Bessemer's portfolio targets of under 12, 18 and 24 months for SMB, mid-market and enterprise, and Benchmarkit's 16-month B2B median on 2025 data ([Marqeable](https://www.marqeable.com/blog/cac-payback-period-benchmarks), secondary). No payback threshold specific to consumer investors was found.

Inroad should therefore make payback a parameter the founder sets, defaulting to 12 months, and always compute it on contribution margin. It should show paid CAC and blended CAC side by side, with blended CAC including creators, seeding, agencies and tools. Before Series A it should prefer realized 12-month cohort LTV over formula LTV, and compute cash payback separately for annual prepaid plans. It should flag LTV:CAC calculated on revenue rather than margin, and any 3:1 target applied with less than 6–12 months of cohort data.

For DTC brands, the steering metrics are contribution-margin layers and MER (marketing efficiency ratio). The layer labels are not standardized. CM2 is the margin after COGS, shipping, fulfillment and payment fees, which is "what most ecommerce operators mean by contribution margin," and CM3 also subtracts marketing ([Polar Analytics](https://www.polaranalytics.com/post/contribution-margin-formula-for-ecommerce-how-to-calculate-it)). An agency attributes to Common Thread Collective a "healthy" CM3 of 20–35% of net sales, with under 15% a danger zone; that attribution is secondhand and not confirmed on CTC's own site ([Hawk Digital](https://hawkdigital.au/insights/ecommerce-unit-economics-cm1-cm2-cm3)). MER is total revenue ÷ total marketing spend, so it does not depend on attribution ([Daasity](https://www.daasity.com/post/marketing-efficiency-ratio-mer)), though Triple Whale defines it the other way up. The gates follow algebraically. Break-even MER = 1 ÷ CM2%, and target MER = 1 ÷ (CM2% − target CM3%). A brand with 58% CM2 that targets 25% CM3 therefore needs MER of about 3.0×.

The benchmarks Inroad can use for DTC sanity checks are vendor data with explicit windows. Littledata's 2023 sample of about 2,800 Shopify stores averaged **1.4% session conversion**; the top 20% exceeded 3.2%, and mobile converted at 1.2% versus 1.9% on desktop ([Littledata](https://www.littledata.io/ecommerce-conversion-rate)). A 1,299-store panel shows median second-order rates of **6.6% within 30 days, 11.4% within 90 and 19.5% within 365** ([By the Numbers](https://www.bythenumbersapp.com/blog/second-order-curve-follow-up)), consistent with Adtribute's 12.03% median at 90 days ([Adtribute](https://www.adtribute.io/data/benchmarks/repeat-purchase-rate)). Median time to second purchase is 15–35 days across verticals, per one agency ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-time-to-second-purchase-by-vertical-2026)). Klaviyo attributes about 27% of store revenue to email on average, by its own attribution ([Klaviyo](https://www.klaviyo.com/marketing-resources/ecommerce-benchmarks)). Post-purchase surveys get at least 60% response for the average Fairing client ([Fairing](https://fairing.co/blog/the-post-purchase-survey-a-vital-marketing-attribution-tool), vendor).

Inroad should treat MER and cohort contribution as ground truth, surveys as directional, and platform ROAS and Klaviyo-attributed revenue as overlapping vendor signals. It should challenge projections of 4–5% sitewide conversion from paid social, or of 40%+ repeat purchase within 90 days for a non-subscription product. It should time follow-up flows to the category's typical time to second purchase. TikTok Shop is now a meaningful channel in beauty and supplements. Third-party trackers estimate its US GMV at about $15.1B in 2025, up 68% ([Ringly](https://www.ringly.io/blog/tiktok-shop-statistics-2026), secondary). Its shoppers overlap heavily with Amazon's, so once a brand sells on Amazon the right denominator is blended MER across DTC and marketplaces.

The DTC cases that can be traced share a sequence that contradicts the story that paid social scales DTC. Glossier launched in 2014 to an existing audience: Into the Gloss already had more than 1M monthly visitors, and Emily Weiss estimated that 90% of revenue came from fans and word of mouth, per secondary profiles from around 2017 ([Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/community/people/emily-weiss)). Graza sold out within a day of its January 2022 launch, with about $100K in first-week sales and no paid advertising, per a Klaviyo customer story (vendor). It then scaled through retail to about 13,300 distribution points by mid-2024 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/06/07/how-graza-olive-oil-co-founder-andrew-benin-built-the-business.html); [New Hope](https://www.newhope.com/brands/graza-bold-single-source-olive-oil-started-spanish-kitchen)). Olipop, sold in 50,000–65,000 retail locations and valued at about $1.85B in February 2025, and Feastables, with about $250M in 2024 revenue (per investor documents reported by Bloomberg, via secondary sources) and 30,000+ retail locations by October 2025, both scaled on retail plus an owned or celebrity audience ([Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/27693-olipop-valued-at-185-billion-following-latest-funding-round); [FemFounded](https://femfounded.org/case-studies/feastables/)).

Inroad's DTC launch coaching should follow that sequence. Build an owned audience before launch, then launch DTC to validate demand and margin without paid spend. Add creator seeding and the founder's story next. Start Meta and TikTok paid ads, with creative testing, only once CM2 and the repeat-purchase curve are known. Time retention flows to the second-purchase window, and add subscription or replenishment for consumables. Add Amazon, TikTok Shop and retail last, once sales-velocity data supports conversations with retail buyers.

Marketplace monetization has one non-negotiable rule: net revenue = GMV × take rate. Inroad should flag any pitch that presents GMV as revenue ([a16z Marketplace Glossary](https://a16z.com/the-marketplace-glossary/)). Take rates depend on what is counted. A third-party reading of Etsy's FY2025 10-K shows gross merchandise sales down 5.3% while revenue rose 2.7%, because the all-in take rate rose from 22.3% to 24.2% on ads and services, against a 6.5% transaction fee. Third-party calculations put eBay near 13.9%, Airbnb near 13.4% and Uber at roughly 25–26.9% ([Sujit Karki](https://www.sujitkarki.com.np/posts/platform-take-rate-trend-2026); [Flexprice](https://flexprice.io/questions/typical-digital-marketplace-take-rates)). An early take rate well above category norms, for example above 20% for physical goods, needs a justification such as payments, fulfillment or ads value; otherwise Inroad should expect leakage. CAC must be computed separately for each side. a16z's 13 marketplace metrics serve as the diagnostic checklist, with match rate, time to match, retention cohorts and concentration first ([Marketplace Library](https://www.marketplacelibrary.com/article/jeff-jordan-li-jin-darcy-coolican-andrew-chen-13-metrics-for-marketplace-companies)).

## Compliance is the best-sourced layer, so Inroad should hard-block it

The benchmarks mostly came through vendors and summaries. The rules governing consumer GTM are better sourced: Apple's guidelines were read directly, and much of the rest surfaced as primary-source text through search. That makes them suitable as block-and-warn lint rules on every draft. Because Inroad drafts but never sends, its compliance job is to build obligations into the outreach, briefs, contracts and copy it writes, and to prompt founders to audit what creators publish. Liability stays with the brand.

In the US, the FTC's revised Endorsement Guides were published July 26, 2023 (88 FR 48092). They require material connections, such as payment, free product, commission, or a family or employment tie, to be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Online, that means "unavoidable": a disclosure on the influencer's profile page but not on the post fails ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/07/26/2023-14795/guides-concerning-the-use-of-endorsements-and-testimonials-in-advertising); [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2024/07/17/a-year-later-revisiting-ftcs-updated-endorsement-guides/)). The FTC's influencer guidance calls for clear terms such as "ad" or "sponsored." It rules out "sp," "spon," "collab," and a stand-alone "thanks" or "ambassador." Video disclosures belong in the video itself, where audio and visual together are more likely to be noticed, and creators should not assume a platform's disclosure tool "is good enough" ([FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)). Disclosures go above the "more" button, not in comments, and affiliate commissions must be disclosed ([FTC, What People Are Asking](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking)). Advertisers are expected to monitor what their endorsers post.

The UK casts a wider net. Under updated CMA/ASA guidance (the CMA's guidance for creators was updated September 3, 2025), payment, gifts, loaned products or affiliate links generally make a post an ad, even when the brand has no editorial control ([RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2025/cma-and-asa-publish-updated-influencer-guidance-on-social-media-endorsements/)). "Ad" placed up front is the safest label, and "gifted" alone is unlikely to be clear enough ([Sirency](https://www.sirency.com/blog/asa-influencer-rules-uk), secondary). Since April 6, 2025, the CMA can impose fines itself, without going to court, of up to the higher of **10% of global turnover or £300,000** ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/judge-jury-and-executioner-the-cma-publishes-its-final-consumer-enforcement-guidance)). The EU relies on the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive's misleading-omission rule. France's 2023 influencer law adds a commercial-partnership mention, "retouched image" or "virtual image" labels, and restrictions on categories such as cosmetic surgery and risky financial products ([EPRS briefing](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2025/779254/EPRS_BRI(2025)779254_EN.pdf); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12698728/)). All of these quotations came through search extracts and should be checked against eCFR and the CMA/ASA documents before they are encoded.

The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465) took effect on **October 21, 2024**. It bans fake and AI-generated reviews, incentives conditioned on the sentiment of a review, undisclosed insider reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, review suppression, and buying or selling fake followers or views. Knowing violators face civil penalties ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Incentives for reviews without a sentiment condition are allowed. But the FTC's own example, "Tell us how much you loved your visit" paired with a coupon, violates the rule ([BCLP](https://bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/part-3-what-the-ftcs-final-rule-means-for-incentivized-reviews.html)). Secondary sources report the penalty per violation as $51,744 when the rule took effect and $53,088 after the 2025 inflation adjustment ([Fourscore Law](https://www.fourscorelaw.com/resources/ftc-bans-fake-reviews-and-testimonials)).

App stores are stricter than the FTC. Apple disallows custom review prompts (5.6.1), forbids forcing users to rate or review an app to unlock functionality (3.2.2(x)), and threatens expulsion from its developer program for "paid, incentivized, filtered, or fake feedback" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), read directly). Google Play bans any reward for ratings, and cites a notification offering a discount for a high rating as an example violation ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898684?hl=en)). Amazon allows free product in exchange for reviews only through its Vine program ([About Amazon](https://aboutamazon.com/news/innovation-at-amazon/update-on-customer-reviews)).

Invite and referral loops carry the sharpest rules, and Apple's guidelines, read directly, contain five relevant provisions ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). **Guideline 5.1.2(v)** says apps must not contact people from a user's Contacts "except at the explicit initiative of that user on an individualized basis; do not include a Select All option or default the selection of all contacts." It also requires apps to show the user "What will the message say? Who will appear to be the sender?" before sending. Guideline 5.1.2(iv) bars building a contact database from that data. Guideline 5.1.1(v) says inviting friends is not core functionality, so it cannot gate sign-in. Guideline 5.1.2(i) bars conditioning compensation on enabling push notifications or tracking. Guideline 5.6.3 treats manipulating referrals as discovery fraud. Google Play likewise requires users to confirm both content and recipients before an app sends messages on their behalf ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034?hl=en)).

US law reaches the same place. The FCC's 2015 TCPA ruling made an app the "maker" of invitation texts it sends automatically with little user control. It also held that a contact's presence in an address book "does not, standing alone, establish consent" ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/tcpa-connect/fcc-issues-long-awaited-tcpa-declaratory-ruling-an)). LinkedIn agreed to a **$13M settlement** over two automatic reminder emails that users had not consented to ([Lieff Cabraser](https://lieffcabraser.com/?p=3837)). And under the FTC's 2008 CAN-SPAM rule, offering any consideration for forwarding a commercial email makes the company the responsible sender ([Willkie Farr](https://www.willkie.com/publications/2008/06/ftc-issues-new-rules-under-the-can-spam-act-of-2__)).

Inroad's safe defaults for invite flows follow from these rules. Use share sheets or the OS contact picker rather than full contact access, and have users select recipients individually. Show an editable preview that names the sender. Send SMS from the user's own messaging app, and use invite links rather than server-sent texts. Allow at most one reminder, disclosed up front and cancellable. Pay rewards only on a qualifying in-product action.

Consumer email and SMS rules differ sharply from B2B cold outreach. CAN-SPAM covers all commercial email. It requires non-deceptive headers and subject lines, a disclosure that the message is an ad, a valid physical postal address, and a working opt-out. Opt-outs must be honored within **10 business days**, and the mechanism must keep working for 30 days. Penalties run up to $53,088 per violating email, per the FTC guide, and that figure is inflation-adjusted each year ([FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).

Mailbox providers add technical requirements for bulk senders, at roughly 5,000 messages a day. Gmail requires SPF, DKIM and DMARC (a DMARC policy of p=none is acceptable) and RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe honored within 48 hours. It also requires keeping the user-reported spam rate below 0.1% and never letting it reach 0.3% ([Gmail sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en)). Microsoft has enforced SPF, DKIM and DMARC for senders above 5,000 a day since May 5, 2025 ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/outlook/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730)).

The EU (ePrivacy Directive, Art. 13), the UK (PECR regulation 22) and Canada (CASL) require opt-in consent for consumer marketing email, with narrow exceptions for existing customers ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=OJ%3AL%3A2002%3A201%3A0037%3A0047%3AEN%3APDF); [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/)). Under CASL, implied consent lapses **two years after a purchase and six months after an inquiry** ([CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)). Inroad's B2B cold-email playbooks therefore do not carry over to EU, UK or Canadian consumers, and every consumer email draft needs a field recording its consent basis.

For SMS, the FCC's one-to-one consent rule was vacated by the Eleventh Circuit on January 24, 2025, and must not be encoded as law ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/fccs-one-one-consent-rule-vacated-whats-next-tcpa-compliance)). The cross-channel "revoke-all" provision is delayed to **January 31, 2027** ([Burr & Forman](https://www.burr.com/telephone-consumer-protection-act/the-fcc-delays-effective-date-of-tcpa-revoke-all-rule-until-january-31-2027)). Quiet hours of 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. in the recipient's local time apply to telephone solicitations, and in early 2025 one plaintiffs' firm filed more than 100 class actions over marketing texts sent outside them ([Privacy World](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/03/new-class-action-threat-tcpa-quiet-hours-and-marketing-messages/)). Carrier rules, from the CTIA principles as paraphrased by messaging providers plus 10DLC registration, require an opt-in confirmation that names the brand, states message frequency, says "Msg & data rates may apply," and explains STOP and HELP ([Telnyx](https://telnyx.com/resources/ctia-sms-guidelines), secondary). Inroad should require recorded consent for every SMS draft, warn on any marketing text scheduled outside 8 a.m.–9 p.m. even when consent exists, never generate phone-number lists, and route sending to compliant SMS tools.

Rules on marketing claims and subscriptions round out the linter. Health-benefit claims generally need "randomized, controlled human clinical testing" ([FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)). Earnings claims must reflect typical results. Lyft's $2.1M civil penalty in 2024 for hourly-earnings ads based on its top fifth of drivers shows the risk, and small-print "not typical" disclaimers do not cure such claims ([FTC, Lyft](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/10/ftc-takes-action-stop-lyft-deceiving-drivers-misleading-earnings-claims); [FTC WealthPress complaint](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2123002WealthPressComplaint.pdf)). "Made in USA" labels require "all or virtually all" US content, and an April 2026 FTC letter extended the warning to social media posts ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-made-usa-standard); [FTC closing letter](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2026-04-14-marketing-holders-closing-letter.pdf)). Environmental claims still follow the 2012 Green Guides, with no final revision found ([Akin Gump](https://akingump.com/en/insights/blogs/speaking-sustainability/ftc-publishes-updates-to-its-green-guides-for-the-use-of-environmental-marketing-claims)). "Free" offers must state all their conditions "clearly and conspicuously at the outset" ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/guide-concerning-use-word-free-similar-representations)).

There is no federal click-to-cancel rule. The Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC's amendments to its Negative Option Rule on July 8, 2025, on procedural grounds. The FTC withdrew them on February 12, 2026 and opened a new advance notice of proposed rulemaking in March 2026 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-19-ftc-issues-new-advance-notice-of-proposed-rulemaking-on-negative-option-marketing)). Enforcement under ROSCA, the federal law on online subscriptions, continues: Amazon settled the FTC's Prime enrollment and cancellation claims for **$2.5B** in September 2025 ([Keller & Heckman](https://www.khlaw.com/insights/amazon-pay-record-25-billion-settle-ftc-claims-deceptive-prime-membership-signup-and)). California's amended automatic-renewal law (AB 2863, effective July 1, 2025) sets a de facto US standard. Law-firm summaries list express consent to auto-renewal separate from the rest of the contract, consent records kept for three years (or one year after termination, if longer), cancellation through the same medium used to sign up, and annual reminders ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-06-04-california-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-on-july-1-2025)). The UK's subscription regime under the DMCC Act, which includes a 14-day cooling-off period after renewals, is expected in 2027, though trackers conflict on the date ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/04/subscription-contracts)).

For young audiences, the amended COPPA Rule has been fully in force since its compliance date of April 22, 2026. It requires separate verifiable parental consent before a child's data is disclosed to third parties, for example for targeted advertising, and it bars keeping children's data indefinitely ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Kirkland](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/04/ftc-publishes-coppa-rule)). Apple's Kids Category bars third-party advertising and most third-party analytics ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Inroad should default consumer personas to 18+ and escalate any work aimed at young audiences.

Platform rules on founder posting and DMs converge on one principle: no bulk, automated or unsolicited outreach. Inroad's draft-only posture already respects it, but Inroad should still enforce it at the level of plans and sequences. X allows automated DMs only after a user contacts the account first, says "you may not send unsolicited Direct Messages in a bulk or automated manner," and requires its approval for AI-generated replies ([X Automation rules](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-automation)). Discord's Community Guidelines (updated August 29, 2025) bar unsolicited bulk messages and self-bots ([Discord](https://discord.com/guidelines)). Meta allows promotional business messages only within 24 hours of the person's last message, with a human-agent tag for manual replies for up to 7 days ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/messenger-platform/policy)). TikTok bans bulk account automation and bought engagement ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity)). Product Hunt bans mass messaging and requests for upvotes but lets founders ask people to "visit and comment" ([Product Hunt Community Guidelines](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/3615694-community-guidelines)). ## Documented APIs already cover an automated weekly review

Inroad's weekly growth review can be assembled almost entirely from documented APIs: reporting endpoints it pulls on a schedule, and webhooks that tools push to it. The table lists the connectors the notes rank highest, along with the constraints the integration has to handle.

The data lags shape the schedule. Because RevenueCat flags recent periods as incomplete, AppsFlyer lags 24–48 hours and Apple's sales data arrives the next day, the review should run midweek (for example, on Wednesday) for the previous Monday-to-Sunday week, and label any partial data. Inroad should detect errors caused by plan gating, such as AppsFlyer's premium endpoints or beehiiv's paid tiers, and explain them to the founder rather than failing silently. For webhook-first tools, it needs a receiver with signature verification and idempotency. The notes rank Stripe in the top tier of connectors, but its API details came from prior knowledge rather than verified sources. The same is true of the creator-discovery tools (Modash, GRIN, CreatorIQ), the referral tools (Rewardful, Viral Loops), the app-intelligence tools (Appfigures, Sensor Tower, AppTweak) and the social-listening tools, none of which were verified this session.

Free public data sources range from fully open to restricted. The Hacker News API needs no authentication and has "currently no rate limit" ([GitHub](https://github.com/HackerNews/API)). Apple's iTunes Search API is limited to "approximately 20 calls per minute" ([Apple Performance Partners](https://performance-partners.apple.com/search-api)). Google's Trends API was announced in alpha on July 24, 2025 and is open only to a small pilot group ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/07/trends-api)). Reddit now requires approval for Data API access under its November 2025 Responsible Builder Policy ([KeyAPI](https://www.keyapi.ai/blog/reddit-data-api-overhaul-major-changes-and-migration-guide/), secondary). Shopify's `/products.json` endpoint is readable on most stores, but it is described only by third-party developer sources ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/shan_yun_ab0c7ad30b46ad2d/shopifys-open-secret-every-store-exposes-productsjson-30ni)), so its use remains a gray area. Trustpilot's terms reportedly prohibit scraping without permission ([WebScraping.AI](https://webscraping.ai/faq/trustpilot-scraping/what-are-the-risks-of-trustpilot-scraping), secondary). The signals worth computing from these sources are week-over-week change in a competitor app's rating count (a proxy for install velocity), themes in competitors' reviews, Show HN and Ask HN mentions of a category, new product handles in competitor catalogs, and Product Hunt launches in a category.

## Conclusion

The research changes how Inroad should be built more than what it should say. The B2B architecture translates almost one for one. The deal pipeline becomes a growth-accounting waterfall with a cohort plateau beside it. "Next best move" becomes a stage-gated engine: moves at existing gathering spots first, loops once retention flattens, and paid only after the six-part gate. The outbound sequence becomes a creator or community draft with disclosure and rules checks attached. The deeper design implication is that provenance must be a first-class data field. The same named metric, whether engagement rate, freemium conversion, repeat rate or ATT opt-in, moves by up to an order of magnitude with its denominator and window. Every benchmark Inroad stores therefore needs its source type, date, denominator and window, and Inroad should show it as a labeled range rather than as a target.

The second implication is an asymmetry Inroad can exploit. The best-sourced material in the notes is the rulebook: Apple's guidelines, the FTC rules, CAN-SPAM, and the mailbox-provider and TCPA requirements. Most performance numbers, by contrast, are vendor-reported. That argues for hard blocks on compliance and soft, founder-adjustable warnings on benchmarks. And because nearly every platform bans bulk, automated or unsolicited outreach, Inroad's draft-only posture fits consumer GTM well. The real risk lies in what Inroad drafts: a review request tied to sentiment, a creator brief with no disclosure, an invite flow with "select all." That makes the linter, not the volume of moves, the product's most defensible feature.
