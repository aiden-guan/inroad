All findings below therefore come from search-engine result extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the full documents. ## 1. Creator selection: tiers, engagement benchmarks, audience fit, red flags

### Takeaway
For a $0–$20K/month budget, the evidence points to nano (≈1K–10K) and micro (≈10K–100K) creators, judged on **recent median views and audience fit, not follower count**. Engagement-rate benchmarks are real but inconsistent across vendors because they use different tier cutoffs and denominators (followers vs. views). Inroad should compare a creator against a benchmark computed the same way, never against a mixed number.

### Cited Findings

**Tier definitions (no standard exists)**
- Most sources define nano as 1K–10K followers and micro as 10K–100K. Meltwater starts nano at 500 followers and merges micro and mid at 10K–100K. Macro cutoffs vary: some use 100K–1M, others 500K–1M. — [Later](https://later.com/blog/influencer-pricing-benchmarks-the-complete-2026-guide/); [Meltwater](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/influencer-marketing-costs-rates-pricing); [SocialRails](https://socialrails.com/blog/influencer-pay-per-post-rates)
- SociaVault (2026, vendor) uses a finer TikTok scale: nano 1K–10K, micro 10K–50K, mid 50K–100K, macro 100K–500K, mega 500K+. HypeAuditor's mega tier is 1M+, so the two are not directly comparable. — [SociaVault](https://sociavault.com/blog/good-engagement-rate-tiktok); [Reply200 summarising HypeAuditor](https://reply200.com/blog/tiktok-engagement-rate/)

**Instagram engagement benchmarks (vendor data)**
- HypeAuditor *State of Influencer Marketing 2025* (vendor; dataset of 76M Instagram, 13.1M YouTube and 104M TikTok accounts): nano-influencers (1K–10K) are 76% of Instagram influencers and have the highest engagement rate of any tier, 2.19%. — [HypeAuditor 2025](https://hypeauditor.com/state-of-influencer-marketing-2025/)
- HypeAuditor: the average Instagram ER in 2024 was 1.59%, down 0.26 points from 2023. — [HypeAuditor blog](https://blog.hypeauditor.com/2025-influencer-marketing-trends-insights-from-hypeauditor-s-latest-report/)
- Conflicting tier numbers from the same underlying data:
  - A secondary summary of HypeAuditor data as republished by Influencer Marketing Hub gives nano 1.73% in 2024 (down from 2.53% in 2022), and micro and mega both 0.68%. It notes the Instagram figures are (likes + comments) ÷ followers and are not comparable with view-based rates. — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/influencer-marketing-statistics-2026-data-points)
  - Colorlib's table (nano 4–5%, micro 2.5–3.5%) does not match HypeAuditor's own page, so treat it as unreliable. — [Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/instagram-engagement-rate/)
- Qoruz (vendor blog) gives Instagram nano 6.0% and micro 3.5%, well above HypeAuditor. One H1-2025 analysis puts the overall Instagram average at about 0.45%. The spread across sources is roughly 10×. — [Qoruz](https://qoruz.com/blog/engagement-rate-benchmarks-to-aim-for-in-2025/); [Favikon](https://www.favikon.com/blog/what-is-a-good-engagement-rate-on-social-media)
- Influencer Marketing Hub *Benchmark Report 2025*: nano-influencers were 75.9% of Instagram's influencer base in 2024. — [IMH Benchmark Report](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report/) (via [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/influencer-marketing-statistics-2026-data-points))

**TikTok engagement benchmarks (vendor data, by tier)**
- SociaVault 2026 (150,000 accounts, median ER): nano 7.84%, micro 5.21%, mid 3.89%, macro 2.73%, mega 1.84%. — [SociaVault](https://sociavault.com/blog/good-engagement-rate-tiktok); [SociaVault Labs](https://sociavault.com/labs/reports/engagement-benchmarks-2026)
- HypeAuditor 2026 report (102M accounts, 2025 data, view-based): nano 11.6%; macro and mega both 6.8%. — [Reply200 summarising HypeAuditor](https://reply200.com/blog/tiktok-engagement-rate/)
- Emplicit / Influencer Marketing Factory (2025): about 7.5% for accounts under 100K, falling to 2.88% for 10M+. — [Emplicit](https://emplicit.co/tiktok-engagement-rate-benchmarks-2025/)
- TokPortal (4,909 accounts, last 12 posts, view-based) found a flatter curve: 1M+ averaged 9.13% vs. 7.89% for 100K–1M. Per-tier samples were only about 1,000 accounts. — [TokPortal](https://www.tokportal.com/data/tiktok-engagement-benchmarks)
- Creaticalc gives a 2026 TikTok median of 4.25%. — [Creaticalc](https://creaticalc.com/engagement-rate-benchmarks)

**Platform mix**
- Influencer Marketing Hub's 2025 report (as summarised by an agency) says TikTok overtook Instagram as the top platform for influencer campaigns: used by 69% of brands vs. 47% for Instagram. — [Flatline Agency](https://www.flatlineagency.com/blog/influencer-marketing-trends-in-2025/)
- Unverified brand-preference figure: 44% of brands prefer nano and 26% prefer micro. The figure is attributed to a Social Cat article, not to IMH, and could not be confirmed. — [VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/micro-influencer-statistics-2026/)
- IMH 2026 edition: 51.43% of respondents intend to expand nano-creator use. — [IMH Benchmark Report 2026](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-marketing-benchmark-report/)

**Practitioner selection signals**
- Cal AI co-founder Zach Yadegari favoured creators with "very tight communities" over raw follower counts. He used a rough 50K-follower minimum but weighted views more heavily. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/)
- Cal AI priced creators on expected views, using the **median** of a creator's recent videos. — [Cal AI co-founder Jake Castillo on X](https://x.com/jakecastilloooo/article/2104597759252717610) (as summarised in search results)
- On TikTok, many creators price by average views rather than followers. — [Talentir](https://www.talentir.com/blog/how-much-do-influencers-charge)

**Red flags for fake or low-quality audiences**
- Followers with no profile photo or no posts, and generic or gibberish comments, indicate fake followers or fake engagement. — [Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/blog/influencer-marketing/fake-followers-and-likes-on-instagram/); [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/de/resources/ways-to-check-if-your-brand-influencer-has-fake-followers/)
- Example mismatch: 100,000 followers with about 100 likes per post likely means many inactive or fake followers. Check by averaging likes and comments over recent posts. — [Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/blog/influencer-marketing/fake-followers-and-likes-on-instagram/)
- Sudden follower spikes, engagement out of line with the tier, and an audience geography or language that doesn't match the creator are further red flags (single vendor source). — [Qoruz](https://qoruz.com/blog/?p=2459)
- Some fake followers are normal, but "more than a few percentage points" is cause for concern. Low engagement can also mean the creator has been inactive rather than fraudulent. — [Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/blog/influencer-marketing/fake-followers-and-likes-on-instagram/)
- Legal angle: under 16 CFR §465.8 the FTC says it does not intend to hold brands vicariously liable for unknowingly hiring an influencer with fake followers. Liability attaches when the buyer "knew or should have known" the indicators were fake and used them for a commercial purpose (see Compliance). — [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/what-the-ftcs-final-rule-means-for-fake-indicators-of-social-media-influence-and-fake-review-websites.html)

### Inferences
- **Encodable selection rule:** Score candidates on:
  1. Audience fit (niche, geography, language, age, matched to the ICP).
  2. Median views of the last 10–12 posts, not the mean, because viral outliers skew it.
  3. Views ÷ followers ratio.
  4. Comment quality (specific vs. generic).
  5. Posting recency and consistency.
  6. Prior sponsored posts that were disclosed properly. This doubles as a compliance signal.

  Follower count is a filter, not a score.
- **Benchmark hygiene:** Store ER benchmarks with their tier cutoffs, platform, year and denominator (followers vs. views). Compare a creator only to a like-for-like benchmark. Reasonable flags from the cited data:
  - Instagram (follower-based): below about 1% for nano/micro deserves a closer look; well above 10% may signal engagement pods or purchased engagement.
  - TikTok (view-based): roughly 4–12% is typical for small creators.

  These thresholds are my synthesis, not sourced cutoffs.
- **Budget fit:** At $0–$20K/month, nano/micro creators let a founder run 20–100+ tests a month. That matters more early on than reach per post.

### Gaps
- None of the primary vendor reports (HypeAuditor, IMH, SociaVault) could be opened directly, so tier-level methodology (date ranges, how fraud was excluded) is unverified.
- No independent, peer-reviewed study was found linking any engagement-rate threshold to conversion outcomes. The claim that "ER predicts sales" is unproven in these sources.
- No reliable data was found on what share of followers are fake by tier, or on how accurate fake-follower detection tools are.

## 2. Outreach: channel, length, contents, response rates, follow-up, etiquette

### Takeaway
No rigorous study compares email and DM reply rates. Practitioner consensus:
- Use the business email when one is listed; otherwise DM, and DM first on TikTok.
- Keep the message short (under about 200 words).
- Make it specific: reference an actual post, and state product, fee or commission, deliverables, usage rights and timeline up front.
- Send one or two follow-ups.

Reply rates scale inversely with creator size. Creators' biggest complaints are late pay and being undervalued, so payment terms are themselves an outreach lever.

### Cited Findings

**Channel**
- No reliable head-to-head study of email vs. DM reply rates for creators was found; one guide states this outright. — [ClickAnalytic](https://www.clickanalytic.com/es/influencer-outreach-email-vs-dm/); [StarNgage](https://starngage.pro/blog/email-vs-dm-vs-creator-marketplaces-analyzing-outreach-response-rates-for-maximum-campaign-success)
- Unverified vendor claims: Instagram DM response rates for business enquiries average 5–10% lower than email, but nano creators often respond faster to DMs. Another source asserts DMs perform "vastly" better, with no data. — [StarNgage](https://starngage.pro/blog/email-vs-dm-vs-creator-marketplaces-analyzing-outreach-response-rates-for-maximum-campaign-success); [Favikon](https://www.favikon.com/blog/how-to-do-influencer-outreach-templates-and-tips-that-actually-get-replies)
- Practical rule: use email when a business address is listed; DM first on TikTok, where creators live in-app and rarely check a separate inbox. — [ClickAnalytic](https://www.clickanalytic.com/es/influencer-outreach-email-vs-dm/)
- TikTok's native Creator Marketplace (TCM):
  - The standalone TCM stopped taking new campaigns after 10 March 2025 and now operates inside TikTok One.
  - Brands can reach creators by direct invitation, open application or invitation link, and creators can negotiate price.
  - One guide notes there is a single invite with no follow-up and no integrated chat, so TCM suits one-off buys more than relationship outreach.
  - Creator eligibility thresholds conflict between sources (e.g., 10K followers and 100K video likes in the last 28 days vs. "varies by region").

  — [Insense](https://insense.pro/blog/tiktok-creator-marketplace); [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/tiktok-creator-marketplace/); [Hubfluence](https://www.hubfluence.io/blog/tiktok-creator-marketplace)

**Length, personalisation, contents**
- Modash (vendor): successful programs typically template about 80% of the email and spend 5–8 minutes per creator on personalisation. This works best when the list is pre-filtered for location, niche and audience. — [Modash templates](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-outreach-templates); [Modash guide](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-outreach)
- Modash on calls to action:
  - With a flexible budget, a soft CTA works.
  - With a tight budget and fixed requirements, "include all details a creator might need in your email and end with a firm CTA", e.g., ask for their rate for specific deliverables.

  — [Modash](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-outreach-templates)
- Third-party guides recommend emails under 200 words with a clear CTA that reference specific content and state deliverables and payment transparently. — [Elev8or](https://www.elev8or.io/blog/influencer-outreach-email-templates); [Connecsi](https://connecsi.net/blog/influencer-outreach-email-templates/)
- Unverified claim: personalised outreach that references specific content gets 3.2× the response rate of generic templates. No methodology was found. — [Scale Growth Digital](https://scalegrowth.digital/resources/social/influencer-outreach-email-template/)

**Reply rates (vendor rules of thumb, no methodology found)**
- Industry average reply rate: 8–20%. Another source gives an 8–15% positive response rate: micro 15–25%, macro 5–10%. — [Elev8or](https://www.elev8or.io/blog/influencer-outreach-email-templates); [Connecsi](https://connecsi.net/blog/influencer-outreach-email-templates/)
- By tier, another source gives nano 40–60%, micro 15–25%, and macro under 5% without a manager introduction. Yet another gives mid-tier 10–20% and macro/mega 3–10%. — [StarNgage](https://starngage.pro/blog/email-vs-dm-vs-creator-marketplaces-analyzing-outreach-response-rates-for-maximum-campaign-success); [MySocial](https://mysocial.io/blog/influencer-outreach)

**Follow-up cadence**
- Modash survey: 72% of surveyed marketers send 1–2 follow-ups. A Modash-published practitioner sends "two follow-ups, max": the first a week later, the second two weeks after that. — [Modash newsletter](https://newsletter.modash.io/p/influencer-outreach); [Modash examples](https://www.modash.io/blog/influencer-outreach-examples)
- Alternative cadences from third-party guides:
  - First follow-up 3–5 days after the initial email, final one 7–10 days later.
  - A 14-day sequence: check-in around day 3, campaign deadline around day 7, closing note around day 14.

  — [Connecsi](https://connecsi.net/blog/influencer-outreach-email-templates/); [Scale Growth Digital](https://scalegrowth.digital/resources/social/influencer-outreach-email-template/)
- Unattributed claims: one follow-up increases replies by "up to 49%". Campaigns with follow-ups average a 4.9% reply rate vs. 3% without. — [Favikon](https://www.favikon.com/blog/how-to-do-influencer-outreach-templates-and-tips-that-actually-get-replies); [Scale Growth Digital](https://scalegrowth.digital/resources/social/influencer-outreach-email-template/)

**Creator-side expectations (etiquette inputs)**
- 41% of creators name payment delays as their biggest pain point with brands (Influencer & Crowd DNA report, March 2025, via eMarketer). — [Gigapay](https://www.gigapay.com/blog/why-creators-put-fast-paying-brands-first); [InfMap](https://infmap.com/blog/why-creators-get-paid-late-and-what-it-costs-brands)
- 87% of creators have been paid late, paid the wrong amount or not paid at all (Tipalti & Lumanu survey). — [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/new-campaign-report-reveals-why-creators-arent-getting-paid-time/1930824); [Sporeboard](https://sporeboard.live/blog/why-creators-dont-get-paid)
- 74% of surveyed creators stopped working with brands after feeling undervalued (Digiday, 2024). Most leave silently by declining the second ask. — [CreatorPulse](https://creatorpulse.io/blog/why-creators-stop-working-with-brands); [Gigapay](https://www.gigapay.com/blog/how-to-reduce-creator-churn)
- Preference for long-term deals:
  - 45% of creators with 100K+ followers prefer long-term partnerships to one-offs (eMarketer).
  - 70% of creators overall prefer long-term campaigns (Sprinklr, 2024).

  — [Gigapay](https://www.gigapay.com/blog/how-to-reduce-creator-churn)
- Creators increasingly ask for deposits, terms of 10–15 days, late fees, or a 50% upfront / 50% on delivery split. — [InfMap](https://infmap.com/blog/why-creators-get-paid-late-and-what-it-costs-brands); [Influencer Advisory](https://influenceradvisory.com/blog/brand-collaboration/)

### Inferences
- **Encodable outreach rules for Inroad drafts:**
  1. **Channel:** Use the bio/business email if present; otherwise DM. TikTok-native creators get a DM first. If a manager or agency email is listed, address the manager, not the creator's DM, and do not bypass them.
  2. **Length:** Aim for 80–150 words on first touch; 200 is a hard cap. DMs should be 2–4 sentences, with details offered on reply.
  3. **Required elements:**
     - Who you are, with a one-line product description.
     - Why this creator, citing one specific recent post.
     - The concrete offer: product value, fee or commission, or "paid, share your rate".
     - Deliverables (count, format, platform).
     - Usage rights and whether whitelisting or Spark/Partnership ads are wanted.
     - Timeline.
     - A disclosure expectation ("#ad plus the platform's paid-partnership label").
     - A single firm CTA.
  4. **Never:**
     - Ask for posts or content in exchange for "exposure", or with neither product nor payment.
     - Imply that a gift obliges a positive post.
     - Ask for a "positive review".
     - Send mass-identical copy.
     - Ask for a free "test video" before agreeing terms.
  5. **Follow-up:** At most two. Follow-up 1 at day 5–7 (short, adds one new fact, such as a deadline or product detail). Follow-up 2 at day 14–21 (a polite close-out). Then stop and mark "no response". Never follow up after an explicit no.
  6. **Payment terms:** State terms in the first or second message (e.g., "paid within 7–15 days of posting"). Fast payment is a differentiator, given that 41–87% of creators report payment problems.
- **Expected funnel for planning:** Treat 10–25% reply rates for nano/micro cold outreach as a planning range, with large variance. These figures come from unverified vendor rules of thumb.

### Gaps
- No controlled or independently published study of creator reply rates by channel, length or personalisation was found. Every percentage above is a vendor rule of thumb.
- No primary data was found on Instagram's partnership-message inbox or creator-marketplace messaging mechanics (search returned nothing usable).
- No source quantified the effect of including the rate or budget in the first message on reply rate.

## 3. Deal structures and rate benchmarks (gifting, flat fee, affiliate, hybrid, pay-per-view, usage rights, whitelisting, exclusivity)

### Takeaway
Early consumer programs typically ladder:
1. Gifting/seeding (20–40% of creators post in a typical program).
2. Paid flat fees for creators who post well. Small-creator marketplace data puts the average paid deal at about $150–$350 per deliverable.
3. Affiliate or hybrid deals (10–25% commission is the common DTC range).
4. Paid amplification of winners via Spark/Partnership Ads, with usage rights and whitelisting priced as add-ons.

Pay-per-view (CPM) deals, typically about $1 per 1,000 views for clipping and higher for original creator posts, are a newer model popular with consumer apps such as Cal AI.

It reports 87.49% of surveyed marketers expect budgets to rise. Its per-tier rate table could not be retrieved. — [IMH Influencer Rates](https://influencermarketinghub.com/influencer-rates/)

**UGC (content-only) rates and add-ons (vendor and creator-guide data)**

  | Licence term | Add-on over base fee |
  |---|---|

A common rule of thumb is +30–50% for usage, whitelisting or rush delivery combined. — [Superscale](https://superscale.ai/learn/how-much-does-ugc-cost-real-pricing-breakdown-for-2025/); [Kristian Larsen](https://www.kristian-larsen.com/info/ugc-rates/)

**Gifting / product seeding post rates (vendor data)**
- A "well-run" seeding program sees a 20–40% post rate (consensus attributed to GRIN, SeedingOps and Aspire). Cohley (a seeding platform) says most PR-style programs see about 30%. — [Storika](https://www.storika.ai/guides/influencer-product-seeding); [Cohley](https://www.cohley.com/blog/introducing-product-seeding)
- Best-in-class or mature programs that combine gifting with affiliate incentives reach about 62% (attributed to SeedingOps). Repeat rounds raise post rates from about 20% in round one to over 40% by round four. — [Storika](https://www.storika.ai/guides/influencer-product-seeding); [Elev8or](https://www.elev8or.io/blog/influencer-gifting-and-product-seeding-guide)
- Post rate by tier: nano 55–75%, lower micro 35–55%, mid micro 20–35%, above 70K followers 10–20%. Passive "send and wait" programs get 10–20%, versus 35–60% with a structured follow-up sequence. — [InfluencerFee gifting guide](https://influencerfee.com/blog/influencer-gifting-program-guide/)

**Affiliate / commission / hybrid (vendor and agency data)**
- DTC commissions typically run 10–25% of net sale value. — [Track360](https://track360.io/blog/ecommerce-affiliate-commission-rates-structures-2026)
- By category:

  | Category | Commission |
  |---|---|
  | Apparel | 10–20% |
  | Beauty | 15–25% |
  | Supplements | 20–35% (20–40% in another source) |
  | Home | 8–15% |
  | Electronics | 3–8% |

  — [BrandsForCreators](https://brandsforcreators.com/rates/affiliate-commission-rates); [AffRef](https://affref.com/blog/affiliate-commission-rates-by-industry-2026)
- By creator tier: nano often accept 10–15% for physical products; micro 12–25%; mid-tier 10–25%. — [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/affiliate-program-for-influencers/)
- Hybrid and tiered structures: 15–25% on new customers and 5–10% on returning; tiered ladders such as 12% base rising to 18–20% for top performers. Affordability ceiling: max commission ≈ contribution margin × 30–50%. — [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/affiliate-program-for-influencers/); [Track360](https://track360.io/blog/ecommerce-affiliate-commission-rates-structures-2026)
- TikTok Shop affiliate commission: one source says 5–20%, another 10–50%. These conflict; check current platform terms. — [AffRef](https://affref.com/blog/affiliate-commission-rates-by-industry-2026); [HamsterGarage](https://www.hamstergarage.com/article/affiliate-marketing-for-dtc-brands-strategies)

**Pay-per-view / CPM deals (consumer apps)**
- **Cal AI:**
  - Paid creators on expected views, using the median of recent videos and a $5 CPM in the co-founder's example. — [Jake Castillo on X](https://x.com/jakecastilloooo/article/2104597759252717610)
  - Restructured deals so creators got their asking price in exchange for a required view threshold or a set number of videos. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/)
  - Reportedly paid about 250 influencers on retainer; the largest earned "tens of thousands per month" for four posts. These are founder stage remarks that TechCrunch did not independently verify. — [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-influencer-growth); [Founder Bureau](https://founderbureau.com/news/cal-ais-30-million-rise-fueled-by-a-network-of-micro-influencers)
- **Clipping / "content rewards" campaigns** (independent editors repost clips; paid per approved view):
  - The going rate is about $1 per 1,000 views; published rates mostly fall between $0.20 and $1.50. — [Lumina Clippers](https://luminaclippers.com/blog/what-is-a-clipping-campaign); [Viral.app](https://viral.app/blog/guides/clipping-campaigns)
  - Whop has shown $1.25 per 1,000 views for YouTube clips, a listing with a $1,400 budget at $1.50 per 1,000, and listings ranging from $0.85 to $3. Whop keeps a 10% fee and pays only on brand-approved posts.
  - The highest rate NPR found was $25 per 1,000 views, from an AI startup.

  — [Whop Content Rewards](https://whop.com/contentrewards/); [Shopiator](https://shopiator.com/blog/whop-clipping); [Lumina Clippers](https://luminaclippers.com/blog/how-much-do-clippers-make)
- TikTok's own Creator Rewards Program pays about $0.50–$1.00 per 1,000 views on original content and reportedly does not pay on sponsored content. — [Forkoff](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/clipping/cpm-rates-for-clipping)

**Paid amplification mechanics (Spark / Partnership Ads)**
- TikTok Spark Ads:
  - The creator enables "Ad authorization" on the post, picks a duration (7, 30, 60 or 365 days) and generates a code. The brand applies the code in TikTok Ads Manager (Creative library → Spark ads posts → Apply for authorization).
  - A post's caption reportedly cannot be edited after it has been authorised as an ad, so the disclosure must be in the caption before authorisation.

  — [JoinBrands help](https://help.joinbrands.com/en/articles/6602080-tiktok-spark-ad-code); [Archive help (creator guide)](https://help.archive.com/en/articles/10199084-creator-s-guide-to-providing-tiktok-spark-codes); [Archive help (Ads Manager)](https://help.archive.com/en/articles/11019419-creating-spark-ads-on-tiktok-ads-manager)
- Meta Partnership Ads require the partner's permission. Permission can be content-level (the creator enables "Allow brand partner to boost" on a tagged post) or account-level (covers posts that tag the advertiser). Creators can revoke permission at any time. — [Later](https://later.com/blog/branded-content-ads/); [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/meta-partnership-ads-guide-for-brands-2026)

### Inferences
- **Encodable deal ladder for a $0–$20K/month program:**
  1. Seed 30–60 hand-picked nano/micro creators with product, no posting obligation, but with disclosure instructions. Expect 20–40% to post.
  2. Offer posters a paid deal (roughly $100–$500 per deliverable for nano/micro, per marketplace medians) and/or 10–25% affiliate commission via a unique code and link.
  3. Buy usage rights or Spark/Partnership Ads access only for top performers, as a separate line item (+30–100%).
  4. For apps, consider view-based deals: a guaranteed minimum plus a CPM bonus, or "asking price for X views or N videos".
- **Contract terms Inroad should always surface in a draft:**
  - Deliverables.
  - Posting window.
  - Disclosure requirement.
  - Approval and edit rights (check claims and disclosure, not the creator's voice).
  - Usage rights: channel, paid vs. organic, duration.
  - Whitelisting or Spark code duration.
  - Exclusivity: category and window, priced separately.
  - Payment amount and terms (net 7–15 recommended).
  - Takedown and correction obligations.
- At this budget, exclusivity is rarely worth paying for. That is my inference from the add-on costs above, not a sourced rule.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve IMH's or Later's own methodology for per-tier rate tables. Most rate data is marketplace asking prices skewed toward small creators.
- No independent data was found on rev-share structures for subscription apps (e.g., % of first-year subscription revenue to creators). Only Cal AI's CPM and threshold anecdote is available.
- No verified official TikTok or Meta documentation was retrieved for Spark/Partnership Ads permission durations or caption-edit locks; all of this comes from third-party help docs.

## 4. Briefs and the UGC-creator model

### Takeaway
A good creator brief:
- Gives structure, not a script: 3–6 hook options, 3–5 key messages, a short do/don't list and mandatory disclosure and claims rules.
- Asks each video to do one thing.

The "UGC creator" model (brand pays for content and runs it from brand or Spark/Partnership ads) is a distinct, cheaper deliverable than influencer posting. Brands are shifting budget toward it. Cal AI's story suggests organic influencer videos alone hit a paid-scale ceiling, after which ad-specific creator content is needed.

### Cited Findings
- Brief guides recommend:
  - 3–6 hook options, based on what already performs or what you want to test.
  - At least two hooks that differ in pain point, angle or location.
  - Devoting about 50% of creative strategy time to the hook.

  — [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-brief-template); [Insense](https://insense.pro/blog/ugc-brief); [Hustler Marketing](https://www.hustlermarketing.com/blog/how-to-write-a-ugc-creator-brief-that-actually-gets-good-content/)
- Include 3–5 key messages, but each video should do one thing well. — [Influee](https://influee.co/blog/ugc-brief-template); [Hustler Marketing](https://www.hustlermarketing.com/writing-ugc-content-briefs-how-to-get-better-ads-out-of-the-deal/)
- Avoid word-for-word scripts ("viewers can tell when someone is reading or memorizing"). "Provide structure, not control" through hook options, sample direction or talking points. — [Hustler Marketing](https://www.hustlermarketing.com/blog/how-to-write-a-ugc-creator-brief-that-actually-gets-good-content/); [Insense](https://insense.pro/blog/ugc-brief)
- Typical do/don'ts: no competitor mentions, no unverifiable claims, correct product pronunciation, compliance restrictions. Keep the list short and relevant. — [Hustler Marketing](https://www.hustlermarketing.com/blog/how-to-write-a-ugc-creator-brief-that-actually-gets-good-content/); [SocialSky](https://www.socialsky.eu/en/articles/the-guide-to-briefing-a-ugc-creator-structure-script-and-template)
- UGC pricing is lower than influencer posting. Collabstr 2025: average UGC deal $177.68 paid vs. $212.21 for Instagram. Collabstr reports brands pivoting from TikTok campaigns to UGC. — [Collabstr 2025](https://collabstr.com/2025-influencer-marketing-report); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/collabstr-report-tiktok-campaigns-plummet-as-brands-pivot-to-ugc/)
- Cal AI ran influencer videos directly as ads, which capped profitable spend at about $5,000/day. The team then recruited creators to make ad-specific content. Influencers reportedly took the app to about $2M/month in revenue, and paid ads lifted it to about $5.7M/month (secondary write-ups of founder statements, unaudited). — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/); [SocialBurning](https://socialburning.com/examples/cal-ai)
- The FTC treats claims in creator content as the advertiser's responsibility:
  - Advertisers are liable for misleading endorsements and are expected to monitor endorsers.
  - Non-expert endorsers can be liable for unsubstantiated performance claims.

  So briefs must restrict claims to substantiated ones (see Compliance). — [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2024/07/17/a-year-later-revisiting-ftcs-updated-endorsement-guides/); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2023/07/ftc-endorsement-guides)

### Inferences
- **Encodable one-page brief template (synthesised from the above):**
  1. Product in one sentence, plus who it's for.
  2. The single job of this video, e.g., "show the 10-second onboarding" or "before/after".
  3. 3–6 hook options, marked as optional; the creator may write their own.
  4. 3–5 key messages ranked, of which the creator must hit #1.
  5. Approved claims list, with substantiation on file, plus a banned-claims list (health, medical, earnings, "best", competitor comparisons).
  6. Do/don't list (≤6 items).
  7. **Mandatory disclosure block, platform-specific:**
     - Spoken plus on-screen "ad" in the first seconds of video.
     - "#ad" or "Ad" at the start of the caption.
     - The platform toggle on (TikTok content disclosure, Instagram paid partnership, YouTube paid promotion).
  8. Deliverables, format and specs (length, aspect ratio, raw footage, number of hook variants).
  9. Usage rights, Spark/Partnership code request and duration.
  10. Timeline, review step (claims and disclosure only) and payment date.
- **Testing cadence (inference):**
  - Early on, brief 5–10 creators per concept with 3+ hooks each, then judge on platform metrics: hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions), hold rate, CTR, CPA.
  - Re-brief winners with variants of the winning hook or angle.
  - Promote winners via Spark/Partnership ads.

  No source was found that quantifies the number of concepts; treat this as a heuristic.

### Gaps
- No credible published data was found on how many concepts or hooks early brands should test per month, or on win rates of creator concepts. Iteration process on winners is not documented in the sources retrieved.
- No primary platform guidance on creative best practices (TikTok Creative Center, Meta) was retrievable.

## 5. Ambassador, campus, superfan and affiliate programs (with tooling)

### Takeaway
Campus and ambassador programs work best for network-effect consumer apps that need density within a community, such as BeReal, Fizz and Partiful. They pay per referral, a stipend, or fund events. Superfan and affiliate programs for DTC brands use 10–25% commissions, run on Shopify-native or mid-market affiliate tools. The documented numbers are mostly secondhand and old (2022–2025).

### Cited Findings
- **BeReal (2022):**
  - College students told *The Brown Daily Herald* BeReal paid them $30 per referral and $50 for an app download with a review, via PayPal or Venmo. BeReal declined to comment, though its website described a paid ambassador program.
  - Ambassador-run campus parties gave free admission if students downloaded the app and added five friends.

  — [Brown Daily Herald](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/02/new-social-media-app-tells-students-to-bereal); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2022/04/08/new-app-encouraging-students-be-real-sweeps-campus)
- TechCrunch (April 2022) reported that at least some of BeReal's growth had been manufactured to make it look viral. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/22/bereal-hype-or-hit-what-to-know-about-the-gen-z-photo-sharing-app-climbing-the-charts/)
- Paying $50 for "a download with a review" would now be high-risk under the FTC's consumer review rule if the review had to be positive (see Compliance §465.4). That is my inference, not a sourced finding about BeReal. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/08/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials)
- **Fizz:**
  - A Stanford case study says the app (then "Buzz") reached an estimated 95% of Stanford's approximately 7,600 undergraduates by mid-2022, largely through organic growth. — [Stanford Ethics in Society case study](https://ethicsinsociety.stanford.edu/sites/ethicsinsociety/files/media/file/case_study_fizz1.pdf)
  - A practitioner blog reports Fizz ambassadors slid paper slips under doors and on tables, and that Fizz paid student clubs per member who signed up. — [Allen Yao, Consumer App Growth Playbook](https://getresidualthoughts.substack.com/p/the-consumer-app-growth-playbook)
- **Partiful:** a reported $100/month ambassador stipend, with ambassadors throwing parties using Partiful as the RSVP tool. This is unconfirmed, and the author did not know the program's ROI. — [Allen Yao](https://getresidualthoughts.substack.com/p/the-consumer-app-growth-playbook)
- A Future (a16z-affiliated) guide describes college ambassador programs as a way to "kickstart network effects within a group of like-minded target users with potentially lower costs than … digital advertising". Ambassadors are typically paid a weekly or monthly stipend. — [Future](https://future.com/college-ambassador-program-how-to-for-startups/)
- **DTC superfan / affiliate tooling (vendor pricing, mostly from competitor blogs):**

  | Tool | Reported pricing |
  |---|---|
  | Social Snowball | From $199/month + 3% of affiliate-driven revenue, or $599/month flat; another figure cites an $899/month "Blizzard" plan |
  | GRIN | Unpublished; estimates range from about $999/month to $25K–$40K/year |
  | Aspire | Estimated $2,000–$2,499+/month on annual contracts |
  | Shopify Collabs | No pricing found in results |

  — [Social Snowball](https://socialsnowball.io/post/best-shopify-affiliate-app); [Archive](https://archive.com/blog/social-snowball-pricing); [Social Snowball vs GRIN](https://socialsnowball.io/post/grin-vs-social-snowball); [SARAL](https://www.getsaral.com/academy/shopify-collabs-alternatives)
- Commission ranges for affiliate and ambassador programs are given in §3 (10–25% DTC typical; higher for supplements and beauty).

### Inferences
- **When Inroad should recommend campus/ambassador programs:** for social, network-effect or campus-dense products (events, dating, messaging, study apps). Pay per activated referral or a monthly stipend tied to measurable activation, e.g., "5 friends added", rather than to app-store reviews.
- **When to recommend superfan/affiliate programs:** for DTC brands, recruit existing customers (from post-purchase survey answers and repeat buyers) into an affiliate program at 10–20% commission with a unique code. This costs nothing until sales occur, which suits a $0 budget.
- **Tool choice at early stage (inference):** Shopify-native or low-fee affiliate apps, plus a spreadsheet or CRM for creator relationships. Avoid enterprise platforms (GRIN, Aspire at roughly $1K–$2.5K+/month) until creator spend justifies them.
- **Compliance:** ambassadors and affiliates are endorsers with material connections, so the same disclosure rules apply, including the UK rule that affiliate links make content an ad.

### Gaps
- No primary, verified ROI data was found for any campus or ambassador program; BeReal, Fizz and Partiful figures are from student press or blogs.
- No retrievable data on Glossier's rep program economics, Gymshark's athlete program terms, or app-specific affiliate tools (e.g., RevenueCat-integrated affiliate payouts).
- Shopify Collabs pricing and commission mechanics were not confirmed in this session.

## 6. Measurement: codes, links, post-purchase surveys, halo, CAC vs. paid social

### Takeaway
Use three layers of measurement:
1. Direct: a unique code and deep link per creator.
2. Self-reported: a post-purchase or onboarding "How did you hear about us?" survey with a creator follow-up question.
3. Platform: Spark/Partnership ad metrics for amplified content.

Codes and links undercount because viewers search for the brand rather than click. For apps, installs are not the conversion; tie attribution to trials, subscriptions and renewals. Official platform data supports creator-style ads outperforming standard ads, but the figures are dated (2020–2022) and vendor-run.

### Cited Findings
- Fairing (post-purchase survey vendor):
  - Influencer impact often goes unseen because viewers buy days later without the link or code.
  - Promo code redemptions and UTM-tracked clicks "provide the cleanest direct attribution available and should be the foundation" but "undercount actual influence".
  - HDYHAU surveys can add an adaptive follow-up asking which creator the buyer saw.
  - Main drawback: answers are self-reported.

  — [Fairing measurement FAQ](https://fairing.co/resources/measurement-faqs/how-do-you-measure-influencer-marketing-performance); [Fairing HDYHAU guide](https://fairing.co/resources/guides/hdyhau-attribution-survey-best-practices-guide)
- Buyers often misremember or simplify multi-touch journeys in surveys. — [Flinque](https://www.flinque.us/influencer-marketing-attribution-why-its-so-hard-and-how-to-fix-it/)
- No source gave a measured undercount rate; one illustrative survey-vs-platform comparison was explicitly hypothetical. — [AdsX](https://www.adsx.com/blog/post-purchase-survey-attribution-setup-shopify)
- **Apps** (Airbridge, an MMP vendor):
  - Promo codes show who redeemed and links show who clicked, but neither shows trial start, subscription or renewal. That needs deep links, an MMP and server-to-server billing integration (e.g., RevenueCat).
  - Use one unique link per creator rather than per campaign.
  - Use a 14–30-day attribution window for influencer traffic rather than 7 days.
  - Instagram feed posts don't allow clickable links, and TikTok limits links to the bio, so many viewers search the app store instead.
  - The vendor's claim that combined link and code tracking improves blended ROAS by 30–50% is unverified.

  — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/blog/influencer-attribution-tracking-promo-code-vs-tracking-link-mobile-app); [Airbridge deeplinks](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/influencer-marketing-deeplinks)
- **TikTok Spark Ads vs. standard ads** (TikTok-run tests, reported secondhand):
  - A 2022 performance-auction test showed 37% lower CPA and a 69% higher conversion rate with the Spark Ads profile landing page.
  - A brand-auction test showed +134% video completion and +157% 6-second view-through vs. standard In-Feed ads.
  - Older headline figures (late 2020 to mid-2021) were 142% higher engagement, 43% higher conversion rate and 4% lower CPM.
  - Retailer James Allen recorded +25% CTR and +24% CVR for Spark vs. non-Spark ads.

  — [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/tiktok-music-promotion/tiktok-spark-ads-vs-non-spark-ads); [RZLT](https://www.rzlt.io/blog/spark-ads-partnership-ads-creator-paid-social); [The Cirqle](https://thecirqle.com/blog-post/tiktok-creators-running-tiktok-ads-that-convert)
- No official Meta CPA figure for Partnership Ads was found. "Up to 30% CPA reduction" appears only in an unsourced blog. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/partnership-ad-performance/); [Pulse Advertising](https://www.pulse-advertising.com/resources/social-media-news/which-paid-amplification-strategy-actually-works-in-2026/)
- **Early-program reality check (Cal AI anecdote):** the first paid story post cost about $2,000 and drove about 1,000 downloads in two days, of which about 100 became paying users. These are founder remarks that TechCrunch did not verify. — [Founder Bureau](https://founderbureau.com/news/cal-ais-30-million-rise-fueled-by-a-network-of-micro-influencers); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/cal-ai-s-viral-playbook-from-one-influencer-story-post-to-a-10m-second-act)

### Inferences
- **Encodable measurement setup for every creator deal:**
  - A unique code (e.g., `CREATOR10`) plus a unique link. For apps, use a deep link via an MMP or the store's campaign link and pre-fill the code.
  - An HDYHAU question at checkout or onboarding with a "which creator?" free-text follow-up.
  - Per-creator tracking of: cost, views, clicks, code uses, survey mentions, trials or first orders, paid conversions or revenue, and blended CAC.
  - A 14–30-day attribution window.
- **Halo rule:** Report two numbers, *direct CAC* (codes and links only) and *blended CAC* (codes, links and survey mentions). Judge creators mostly on blended CAC and on whether their content wins as paid ads.
- **Realistic expectations (inference from the Cal AI anecdote and seeding post rates):**
  - Most individual creator posts will not pay back directly.
  - Early programs are search processes: of 20–50 creators tested, a handful will drive most results.
  - The best outcome is often creative that wins when amplified via Spark/Partnership Ads.
  - Cal AI's first test implies about $20 per paying user ($2,000 ÷ ~100), but that is a single, unaudited anecdote.

### Gaps
- No independent study was found comparing creator-program CAC with paid-social CAC for early-stage brands; the only official performance figures are TikTok's 2020–2022 Spark Ads tests.
- No measured "undercount multiplier" (survey attributions vs. code redemptions) was found.
- Apple App Store Offer Codes and Google Play promo-code mechanics for creator attribution were not verified in this session.

## 7. Compliance: FTC, platform tools, UK ASA/CMA, EU (rules Inroad should enforce)

### Takeaway
In the US, any material connection (payment, free product, discount, affiliate commission, family or employment) must be disclosed **clearly, conspicuously and "unavoidably" in the content itself**:
- Use "Ad", "Advertisement" or "Sponsored" (or "#ad"), placed up front.
- Platform paid-partnership toggles are not sufficient on their own.
- Vague tags ("sp", "spon", "collab", "thanks", "ambassador") are not acceptable.

The brand is liable and must monitor creators. The 2024 FTC rule (16 CFR Part 465) adds civil penalties for:
- fake reviews and testimonials,
- buying reviews conditioned on sentiment (on review sites),
- buying fake followers or views.

The UK (CMA/ASA) is at least as strict:
- Gifts and affiliate links make content an ad even without brand control.
- Use "Ad" up front; "gifted" alone is insufficient.
- Since April 2025 the CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover.

The EU relies on the UCPD plus national laws, with France the strictest.

### Cited Findings

#### US — FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023)
- Revised Guides published in the Federal Register 26 July 2023 (88 FR 48092). — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/07/26/2023-14795/guides-concerning-the-use-of-endorsements-and-testimonials-in-advertising); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2023/07/ftc-advertising-endorsement-and-testimonial-guides)
- Online disclosures must be "unavoidable" to be clear and conspicuous. In an FTC example, a disclosure on the influencer's profile page but not on the post itself is **not** unavoidable. — [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2024/07/17/a-year-later-revisiting-ftcs-updated-endorsement-guides/)
- Relying solely on a platform's built-in disclosure tool "likely will not suffice". If a platform doesn't allow clear and conspicuous disclosures, it "should not be used to disseminate advertisements that require disclosures". — [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2024/07/17/a-year-later-revisiting-ftcs-updated-endorsement-guides/); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2023/07/ftc-endorsement-guides)
- The FTC "expects advertisers to be responsible for and monitor the actions of their endorsers". Advertisers are liable for misleading endorsements and undisclosed material connections, and may be liable for third-party statements they repost. — [Hall Render](https://hallrender.com/2024/07/17/a-year-later-revisiting-ftcs-updated-endorsement-guides/); [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/truth-or-consequences-ftc-revised-endorsement-guides-class-action-risk/)
- Endorsers can be liable for:
  - representations they know or should know are deceptive,
  - (non-experts) misleading or unsubstantiated performance claims,
  - failing to disclose material connections.

  Agencies and intermediaries can be liable for endorsements they help create or spread when they know or should know those endorsements are deceptive. — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2023/07/ftc-endorsement-guides); [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2023/december/ftc-new-guidelines-for-endorsements-and-testimonials)
- "Endorsement" now explicitly covers fake reviews, virtual influencers and social media tags. — [4As Health / Arnold & Porter](https://4ashealth.org/2023/07/05/ftc-endorsement-guidelines-update/)

#### US — FTC "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers" (FTC staff guidance, Nov 2019; still current)
- Use clear terms such as "advertisement", "ad" or "sponsored". "Thanks to Acme brand for the free product" can suffice if hard to miss. Hashtags like #ad or #sponsored are fine but not required. — [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers); [FTC PDF](https://www.bulkorder.ftc.gov/sites/bulkorder.ftc.gov/files/publications/1001a_influencer_guide_508.pdf); [FTC blog, Nov 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/disclosures-101-new-ftc-resources-social-media-influencers)
- Don't use vague or confusing terms like "sp", "spon" or "collab", or stand-alone terms like "thanks" or "ambassador". — [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers); [FTC PDF](https://ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508_1.pdf)
- "Don't assume that a platform's disclosure tool is good enough, but consider using it in addition to your own, good disclosure." — [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)
- Video: the disclosure should be in the video itself, not just the description. Disclosures in both audio and video are more likely to be noticed. — [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)
- Live streams: repeat the disclosure periodically so viewers who see only part of the stream get it. No interval is specified; "every 3–5 minutes" and "every Story frame or every 3–4 slides" are third-party practices, not FTC text. — [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers); [Kolect](https://www.kolect.ai/guides/ftc-influencer-disclosure)
- You can't talk about your experience with a product you haven't tried. — [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)
- Recalled from the FTC brochure, not re-verified this session because ftc.gov was unreachable:
  - Disclosure is triggered by any "financial, employment, personal, or family relationship" with a brand.
  - Financial relationships include free or discounted products and perks, even if the creator wasn't asked to post.
  - For image platforms such as Instagram Stories or Snapchat, superimpose the disclosure on the image with enough time to read it.

  — [FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)
- Third-party interpretation, consistent with the "unavoidable" standard: put the disclosure at the start of the caption, not buried among hashtags or below the "more" cut, and not only in the bio. — [Quasa](https://quasa.io/insights/ftc-disclosures-belong-in-the-content-not-behind-more-or-in-a-bio); [Termly](https://termly.io/resources/articles/ftc-requirements-for-influencers/)

#### US — FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, 2024)
- **Timeline:** announced 14 Aug 2024; published 22 Aug 2024 (89 FR 68034); effective 21 Oct 2024. It allows civil penalties against knowing violators and has "the full force and effect of federal law". — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials); [Consumer Finance Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2024/08/20/ftc-issues-final-rule-to-fight-fake-product-reviews/); [FTC 2025 warning letter template](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2025-Fake-Review-Warning-Template.pdf)
- **Six prohibited practices:**
  1. Fake or false reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones (§465.2).
  2. Compensation conditioned on review sentiment (§465.4).
  3. Undisclosed insider reviews (§465.5).
  4. Company-controlled "independent" review sites (§465.6).
  5. Review suppression (§465.7).
  6. Fake indicators of social media influence (§465.8).

  — [Fourscore Law](https://www.fourscorelaw.com/resources/ftc-bans-fake-reviews-and-testimonials); [FTC rulemaking page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/rulemaking-use-consumer-reviews-testimonials)
- **§465.4 (buying sentiment) scope:** it applies to consumer reviews "that appear on a website or portion of a website dedicated to receiving and displaying such reviews", not to social media testimonials. A conditional incentive can be express or implied. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/08/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-consumer-reviews-and-testimonials); [Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/commercial-litigation-update/dissecting-the-new-ftc-final-rule-that-regulates-fake-reviews-and-more)
- **Influencers under the rule:** the FTC's Q&A treats a hired influencer's promotional post as a (celebrity) testimonial. A business can be liable under §465.2(b) if it knew or should have known the influencer misrepresented their use of the product. — [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- **§465.8 (fake followers and views):** prohibits selling, distributing or buying fake social media indicators when the person knew or should have known they were fake and they are used to misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose. The FTC says it is not imposing vicarious liability on brands that unknowingly hire an influencer with fake followers. A business whose growth vendor secretly bought followers is liable only if red flags should have alerted it. — [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/what-the-ftcs-final-rule-means-for-fake-indicators-of-social-media-influence-and-fake-review-websites.html); [FTC Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)
- **Civil penalty per violation:**
  - $51,744 when the rule took effect.
  - $53,088 after the 2025 inflation adjustment, with reportedly no 2026 adjustment.

  Both figures are from secondary sources and need verification against the Federal Register. Claims that each review or each exposed consumer counts as a separate violation are unverified. — [Fourscore Law](https://www.fourscorelaw.com/resources/ftc-bans-fake-reviews-and-testimonials); [Digifeel](https://www.digifeel.io/blogs/infos/ftc-fake-reviews-rule)
- **Enforcement examples:**
  - In 2023 the FTC sent warning letters to the American Beverage Association, the Canadian Sugar Association/Institute and 12 registered dietitians over inadequately disclosed paid posts about aspartame and sugar.
  - In December 2025 the FTC reportedly sent warning letters to 10 companies under the Consumer Review Rule (secondary source).

  — [BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/ftc-warning-influencers); [Business Radar](https://www.businessradar.org/business/ftc-influencer-disclosure-reckoning-2026)
- A single Substack commentary argues that state-law class actions against brands (e.g., Celsius, Shein, 2025) are now a bigger disclosure risk than the FTC. This is a single source and unverified. — [Marketing Ethics Substack](https://marketingethics.substack.com/p/what-the-latest-ftc-influencer-enforcement)

#### Platform tools (required by platforms; not sufficient for FTC on their own)
- **Instagram/Facebook:**
  - The creator turns on the "Paid partnership" label via the branded content tool and tags the brand. The brand may need to approve the tag, which then gives it access to post insights.
  - Partnership Ads require the creator's permission (content-level or account-level), which is revocable.

  Several sources recommend adding a written "#ad" as well. Official Meta help pages could not be retrieved. — [Later](https://later.com/blog/branded-content-ads/); [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/meta-partnership-ads-guide-for-brands-2026); [Creator Lane](https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-branded-content-api-explained)
- **YouTube:** the creator ticks the "includes paid promotion" box in video details, which shows an "Includes paid promotion" overlay at the start of the video (one source says the first 30 seconds; banner timing is inconsistent across sources). The official help page could not be retrieved. — [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/paid-partnership-label-guide/); [NoobClaw](https://noobclaw.com/blog/paid-partnership-label-every-platform-2026/)

#### UK — ASA (CAP Code) and CMA (consumer law)
- The CMA and ASA published updated joint influencer guidance. The CMA's guidance for content creators was updated 3 September 2025. If a creator receives payment, a gift, a loaned product, free services, affiliate commission or any other commercial benefit, the post will generally be considered an ad. Consumer law requires disclosure **even where the brand has no editorial control**. — [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2025/cma-and-asa-publish-updated-influencer-guidance-on-social-media-endorsements/)
- Unsolicited gifts count as incentives, and the content must be labelled as an ad. Any content referring to a product the creator holds an affiliate link or code for counts as advertising. — [CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/uk-influencer-disclosure-asa-rules/); [SME Hype](https://smehype.com/uk-influencer-ad-disclosure-guide-2026/)
- **Labels:**
  - "Ad" is the safest label, placed at the start; it need not be a hashtag if prominent and clearly separated.
  - "Gifted" or "gift" alone is unlikely to be clear enough. One agency source lists #gifted as acceptable, but it contradicts the others and is treated as unreliable.
  - A generic "some links may earn me commission" footer is insufficient because it doesn't identify the specific advertising content.

  — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/asa-influencer-rules/); [Sirency](https://www.sirency.com/blog/asa-influencer-rules-uk); [CreatorFlow](https://creatorflow.so/blog/uk-influencer-disclosure-asa-rules/)
- The ASA published an influencer-transparency (ad disclosure) report in May 2025 and can publicly name non-compliant influencers. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/05/09/asa-publishes-new-report-on-influencer-transparency-102kaqy); [Boyes Turner](https://www.boyesturner.com/news-and-insights/asa-publishes-its-latest-influencer-disclosure-report)
- **DMCC Act 2024:** since 6 April 2025 the CMA can fine directly, without court, for consumer-law breaches, up to the higher of 10% of global annual turnover or £300,000. Lower tiers apply to administrative breaches (5% or £150,000) and investigatory breaches (1% or £30,000). — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/judge-jury-and-executioner-the-cma-publishes-its-final-consumer-enforcement-guidance); [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2025/cma-intensifies-consumer-law-enforcement-under-dmcca-2024); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/four-months-into-the-dmcc-navigating-cma-guidance-and-what-to-expect/)
- Commentary: the CMA expects brands to set a labelling policy, check posts and correct problems quickly. — [Gordons Partnership](https://gordonspartnership.com/insights/influencer-marketing-implications-of-the-digital-markets-competition-and-consumers-act-2024/); [Marketing Minute](https://www.marketingminute.co.uk/post/influencer-marketing-disclosure-rules-uk)

#### EU
- There is no dedicated EU influencer law; influencer marketing falls under horizontal rules. Under UCPD Art. 7(2), failing to identify commercial intent is a misleading omission. — [European Parliament EPRS briefing (2025)](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2025/779254/EPRS_BRI(2025)779254_EN.pdf); [BEUC (2023)](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/BEUC-X-2023-093_From_influence_to_responsibility_Time_to_regulate_influencer-marketing.pdf)
- The AVMSD separately requires commercial communications to be recognisable. An influencer must indicate "in a clear and understandable way" that content is an advertisement. — [BEUC](https://www.beuc.eu/sites/default/files/publications/BEUC-X-2023-093_From_influence_to_responsibility_Time_to_regulate_influencer-marketing.pdf); [Europe Consommateurs](https://www.europe-consommateurs.eu/en/topics/shopping/seller/influencers/)
- **France, Law n° 2023-451 (9 June 2023):**
  - Influencers must state that content results from a commercial partnership.
  - Images altered by filters or AI must carry "retouched image" or "virtual image" labels.
  - Promotion of cosmetic surgery, risky financial products such as crypto, and certain gambling is restricted or banned.

  — [Unyer](https://www.unyer.com/influencer-marketing-the-key-to-legal-compliance-in-europe/); [PMC article on the French influencer law](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12698728/)
- A 6 November 2024 amendment aligned the French law with EU law. Labels must be "clear, legible and identifiable" but no longer need to appear throughout the whole advertisement. — [IRIS Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/10208)
- Rules vary by member state; check national requirements. — [Talentir](https://www.talentir.com/blog/influencer-disclosure-rules)

### Inferences
- **Compliance checks Inroad should run on every outreach, brief and contract it drafts:**
  1. **Every** outreach that offers product, payment, commission or perks includes a disclosure requirement line.
  2. The brief mandates "Ad"/"#ad"/"Sponsored"/"Paid partnership with @brand" (or "Thanks @brand for the free product") at the **start** of the caption, above the "more" fold. It also requires the platform toggle **in addition to**, never instead of, the written disclosure.
  3. For video, the disclosure is spoken and shown on screen early. For Stories and images, it is superimposed on each frame. For live streams, it is repeated periodically.
  4. Block or warn on stand-alone "sp", "spon", "collab", "thanks", "ambassador", "partner", or "gifted" alone (UK), and on bio-only disclosure.
  5. Creators must have actually used the product, so seeding comes before content. Claims must be on the brand's approved, substantiated list.
  6. Never draft language that conditions compensation on positive sentiment ("post a positive review", "5-star review on the App Store for $X"). Never suggest buying followers, views or engagement. Never suggest staff or friends post reviews without disclosure.
  7. The brand must monitor posts and request corrections. Inroad should create a "check disclosure" task for each live post.
  8. **UK mode:** gifts, loans and affiliate links all equal an ad. Use "Ad" up front. Generic affiliate footers are insufficient.
  9. **EU mode:** clear identification of commercial intent in the local language. France adds a commercial-partnership mention, retouched/virtual-image labels and banned categories.
- Because Inroad drafts but never posts, its compliance role is to (a) bake disclosure obligations into outreach, briefs and contracts and (b) prompt founders to audit posts. It can't enforce what creators actually publish, and brand liability remains.

## 8. Case studies of early consumer brands and apps grown via creators

### Takeaway
The best-documented recent example is Cal AI, a calorie-tracking app:
- One paid story post, then a scaled network of paid creators priced by views.
- Then creator-made ad content once organic influencer posts hit a paid-scale ceiling.
- Reported revenue of tens of millions in ARR before an acquisition by MyFitnessPal. Figures are founder-reported and inconsistent.

Older DTC "classics" (Daniel Wellington, Glossier, Gymshark) are widely cited, but their headline numbers are unverified. Campus apps (BeReal, Fizz) show the ambassador variant.

### Cited Findings
- **Cal AI:**
  - The first customers came from one paid story post (about $2,000), which drove about 1,000 downloads in two days, of which about 100 became paying users. — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/cal-ai-s-viral-playbook-from-one-influencer-story-post-to-a-10m-second-act); [Founder Bureau](https://founderbureau.com/news/cal-ais-30-million-rise-fueled-by-a-network-of-micro-influencers)
  - The program scaled to about 250 creators on retainer, priced on views. An agency claiming involvement says 500+ creators on TikTok and Reels on performance-based CPM; that agency is self-interested. — [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/cal-ai-zach-yadegari-influencer-growth); [The Viral App](https://theviralapp.com/portfolio/cal-ai/)
  - Influencer videos run as ads capped profitable spend at about $5K/day, which led to ad-specific creator content. Influencers reportedly drove about $2M/month, and paid ads took it to about $5.7M/month. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/cal-ai-founder-zach-yadegari-breaks-down-the-influencer-playbook-behind-his-apps-growth/); [SocialBurning](https://socialburning.com/examples/cal-ai)
  - Revenue is reported variously as "$30M+ ARR", "$30M 2025 revenue" and "$50M ARR". The MyFitnessPal acquisition is dated variously December 2025 or March (2026). The figures are not audited. — [Jake Castillo on X](https://x.com/jakecastilloooo/article/2104597759252717610); [Virlo](https://signal.virlo.ai/p/cal-ai-myfitnesspal-acquisition-growth-breakdown); [daily.dev](https://daily.dev/posts/when-it-came-to-cal-ai-s-viral-success-a-small-army-of-influencers-paid-off-sys8cgvhr)
  - An earlier write-up: 100,000 downloads and $1M revenue in the first four months, bootstrapped. — [What a Startup](https://whatastartup.substack.com/p/two-gen-z-founders-bootstrapped-cal-ai)
- **BeReal (2022):** paid campus ambassadors ($30/referral; $50 for download plus review) and app-gated parties. TechCrunch reported that some growth was manufactured. — [Brown Daily Herald](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/02/new-social-media-app-tells-students-to-bereal); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/22/bereal-hype-or-hit-what-to-know-about-the-gen-z-photo-sharing-app-climbing-the-charts/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/bereal)
- **Fizz:** reached an estimated 95% of Stanford's ~7,600 undergraduates by mid-2022, using on-campus ambassador tactics and per-signup payments to clubs. — [Stanford case study](https://ethicsinsociety.stanford.edu/sites/ethicsinsociety/files/media/file/case_study_fizz1.pdf); [Allen Yao](https://getresidualthoughts.substack.com/p/the-consumer-app-growth-playbook)
- **Daniel Wellington:** gifted watches to creators for Instagram posts with a unique discount code per influencer. The widely cited "4,700% profit growth in 3 years" has no primary source found. One blog cites about $200 per partnership, attributed to a 2025 academic analysis. — [MarkHub24](https://www.markhub24.com/post/the-micro-influencer-strategy-framework-for-niche-brand-growth-how-daniel-wellington-and-glossier-r); [Influencer Hero](https://www.influencer-hero.com/blog-detail/top20-best-influencer-marketing-campaign-case-studies)
- **Glossier:** gifted products to micro-influencers and customers rather than paying celebrities. A "600% sales spike" credited to an HBS case has inconsistent timing across sources and is unverified. — [Everything-PR](https://everything-pr.com/tula-skincare-alo-yoga-other-successful-micro-influencer-campaigns); [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/brands-that-work-with-micro-influencers/)
- **Gymshark:** grew on micro-influencer partnerships on Instagram early, later shifting toward macro and mega creators plus an ambassador program. No verified numbers were found. — [Viral Nation](https://www.viralnation.com/influencer-lists/10-brands-that-skyrocketed-thanks-to-influencer-partnerships); [Socially Powerful](https://sociallypowerful.com/influencer/marketing/examples)
- **Lenny's Newsletter:** no piece focused specifically on creator or influencer programs for consumer apps was found. The closest are Nikita Bier's consumer-app virality playbook (TBH, Gas) and "How the biggest consumer apps got their first 1,000 users", which covers influencer endorsements in early Twitter-era examples. Full texts could not be read. — [Lenny's Newsletter: Nikita Bier](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-consistently-go-viral-nikita-bier); [Lenny's Newsletter: first 1,000 users](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-the-biggest-consumer-apps-got)

### Inferences
- **Pattern across cases (encodable as a playbook arc):**
  1. A tiny paid or gifted test with one to a few creators.
  2. Instrumented with codes or links.
  3. Scale the creators and formats that work, priced on views or performance.
  4. Turn winning creator content into paid ads (Spark/Partnership) with rights secured.
  5. Commission ad-specific creator content once organic reposts saturate.
- Cal AI's arc is the closest analogue for subscription apps. DW and Glossier are the analogues for DTC seeding plus codes. BeReal and Fizz are the analogues for network-effect apps (campus density). Their numbers should be presented to founders as "reported", not as benchmarks.

- No verified case studies of early DTC brands from 2024–2026 with creator-program economics were found (e.g., beverage or beauty brands). The search surfaced only listicles.
- Practitioner sources requested in the brief (a16z, First Round Review, Andrew Chen, podcasts) did not surface creator-program-specific content with numbers in this session's searches. Lenny's Newsletter pages were blocked by the proxy.
