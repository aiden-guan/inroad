## 1. How notable consumer companies got their first 1,000 users: primary-source channel taxonomy and how often each channel appears

### Takeaway
Lenny Rachitsky's research is the most-cited dataset: a May 2020 study of 40+ consumer apps, revised in July 2022 to cover 100+ B2C startups. Its central finding is that a small set of strategies (seven) accounts for nearly all early consumer growth. Most companies relied on **one**, a few used a handful, and **none succeeded with more than three**. The most common and highest-ROI strategy, used by **over 50%** of companies, is going to an existing gathering spot (online or offline) of a **super-specific** audience. Marketplace data shows the same concentration: across 17 marketplaces the **median was two** supply levers (mean 2.5), with direct sales the most common and SEO and paid rare.

### Cited Findings

- Secondary summaries put the sample at "40+ consumer apps." **Paul Graham, "Do Things That Don't Scale" (July 2013; historical, still applies)**
- The core claim: "The most common unscalable thing founders have to do at the start is to recruit users manually. Nearly all startups have to. You can't wait for users to come to you. You have to go out and get them." — [paulgraham.com/ds.html](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html) (via search summary); [Inc. summary](https://www.inc.com/business-insider/paul-grahams-counter-intuitive-startup-advice-do-things-that-dont-scale.html) [Primary via search summary]
- The "Collison installation": instead of asking a prospect whether they would try the beta, Stripe's founders would say "right then, give me your laptop" and set them up on the spot. — [Inc. summary](https://www.inc.com/business-insider/paul-grahams-counter-intuitive-startup-advice-do-things-that-dont-scale.html) [Secondary]
- Airbnb's founders went door to door in New York, recruiting new hosts and helping existing ones improve their listings. Accounts that they flew in to photograph listings are secondary only. — [paulgraham.com/ds.html (via summary)](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html); [ycinsight distilled](https://ycinsight.com/essays/do-things-that-dont-scale) [Primary via search summary / Secondary]

### Inferences
- **Encodable rule: at most 1–3 channels.** Across two Lenny datasets (consumer: none succeeded with more than three strategies; marketplace: median two levers), an early consumer company should run one primary acquisition strategy and at most two supporting ones. For Inroad: the daily list of at most 5 moves should come from at most 3 channels, and usually 1 primary.
- **Default first move:** identify the "super-specific who" and the existing places (online or offline) where they already gather, then show up there in person or by hand. This is the >50% strategy. It fits Inroad's draft-only model well: draft the post, DM, or event pitch, and the founder sends it.
- **Friends and personal network is a legitimate first channel** (~20% of companies), especially for social and invite-based products.
- **Marketplaces:** focus on the constrained side (usually supply) in one narrow niche or geography. Use direct sales or recruitment plus referrals or piggybacking. Deprioritize SEO and paid until liquidity exists.
- **Taxonomy for Inroad's consumer mode:** (1) offline gathering spots (campuses, events, craft fairs); (2) online gathering spots (subreddits, forums, Discords, HN/PH, existing platforms such as Craigslist); (3) personal network and friend invites; (4) targeted cold outreach to strangers; (5) influencers, paid or organic; (6) press; (7) scarcity/FOMO via invites or waitlists; (8) pre-launch community. Marketplaces add direct sales, subsidies, and employees as supply.

The 2022 list in summaries also omits the 2020 "FOMO" and "community" categories, so the two versions' taxonomies cannot be reconciled without the originals.
- Several company anecdotes in summaries could not be verified. Search results say "Snapchat's CEO showed the app to people one at a time at malls" and "Reddit used fake accounts"; one search summary itself flagged the Reddit claim as unconfirmed. Both were left out of the findings.

## 2. Organic short-form social and founder-led content (TikTok, Reels, Shorts, X, LinkedIn for prosumer)

### Takeaway
No independent, methodology-backed data was found on posting cadence, how many posts precede a breakout, or installs driven by slideshow, faceless or UGC accounts. Everything found comes from vendors selling slideshow, UGC or automation tools. Their consistent (but unvalidated) guidance is **1–3 posts per day per account** with format experimentation. The platform-policy facts that matter: TikTok photo posts are not eligible for Creator Rewards, YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" monetization policy reportedly targets image-only slideshows, and AI-generated realistic people require disclosure labels.

### Cited Findings
- Cadence guidance is vendor anecdote, not evidence. One guide recommends posting "1–2x a day for the first 1–2 weeks," moving to "1–3x daily only if reach holds up." Another calls one to two videos per day "the sweet spot." — [ReelCarousel, faceless TikTok accounts promoting apps (2026)](https://www.reelcarousel.app/blog/faceless-tiktok-accounts-promoting-apps); [UGCKing (2026)](https://ugcking.com/blog/how-to-make-tiktoks-without-filming) [Vendor]
- An automated slideshow pipeline example runs **3 slideshow posts per day of 3 slides each (63 images per week)**. — [Postiz blog](https://postiz.com/blog/automate-tiktok-slideshow-marketing-ai-agents-postiz) [Vendor]
- Faceless app-promotion accounts favor photo carousels because they need "no filming, no voice" and allow the most posts per hour. The operator is "typically an affiliate paid per install or subscription, not the app's developer." — [ReelCarousel](https://www.reelcarousel.app/blog/faceless-tiktok-accounts-promoting-apps) [Vendor]
- Policy constraints: TikTok photo posts do not qualify for the Creator Rewards Program. YouTube's monetization rules since July 2025 reportedly flag image-only slideshows as "inauthentic content." Realistic synthetic media (e.g., AI UGC characters) needs an AI label on YouTube, TikTok and Meta, but hands-only or screen-recorded videos do not. — [ReelFarm faceless marketing guide](https://reel.farm/guides/faceless-marketing); [Fastlane AI UGC guide](https://www.usefastlane.ai/blog/how-to-make-ugc-videos-without-showing-your-face) [Vendor; not verified against YouTube/TikTok policy pages]
- Reach is highly variable. The example figures in vendor content (a best video of 8,200 views in month one; a single 2.1M-view video over six weeks) are illustrative or hypothetical, not measured app outcomes. — [decidenett blog (Oct 2026)](https://decidenett.blogspot.com/2026/10/faceless-tiktok-accounts-niches-monetize-2026.html) [Low-quality secondary]
- No result reported download or install figures tied to slideshow volume, and none gave 2025 benchmarks for organic short-form-to-install conversion. — (search finding; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- **Treat 1–3 posts/day/account as a practitioner convention, not a benchmark.** Inroad could encode a default cadence of 1–2 drafted short-form posts per day per account for a 2–4 week test window, with continuation gated on reach. The evidence behind this is anecdotal and should be labeled that way to the founder.
- **Draft-only fits:** Inroad can draft hooks, slideshow copy (slide-by-slide) and captions. The founder posts.
- **Compliance guardrails Inroad should encode:** label realistic AI-generated people; do not plan YouTube Shorts monetization around image-only slideshows; and if affiliates or UGC creators are paid per install, disclosure rules (e.g., FTC endorsement guides in the US) apply. That last point was not researched here.
- **Measurement (inferred):** with no platform-provided install attribution for organic posts, teams typically use per-account link-in-bio tracking links, "how did you hear about us" onboarding surveys, and install spikes lined up with post timestamps. This was not sourced in this session.

### Gaps
- No primary platform guidance retrieved (TikTok Creator Academy, TikTok for Business, Instagram/Meta creator best practices, YouTube Shorts) on cadence, hooks, or the first-seconds retention metrics the algorithms weigh.
- No verified case studies of 2024–2026 consumer apps growing via slideshow, faceless or UGC account networks (e.g., Cal AI and other AI consumer apps). Searches were cut off by the budget limit.
- No evidence on "posts before breakout," X/LinkedIn founder-led content for prosumer products, or how teams measure organic short-form ROI.

## 3. Community channels: Reddit, Discord, Facebook groups, niche forums

### Takeaway
On Reddit, the "10% / 9:1" self-promotion rule is **not** a sitewide rule. It is an informal Reddiquette rule of thumb. Reddit stopped enforcing the "1 in 10" rule sitewide in 2017. The binding sitewide Spam policy has no ratio. The rule that actually binds is **each subreddit's own rules**: some ban promotion outright, and some use a 10% cap on a user's activity within that community. No sourced etiquette data was obtained for Discord, Facebook groups or niche forums.

### Cited Findings
- Reddit's Reddiquette page describes the 9:1 idea as "a widely used rule of thumb," and Reddit labels Reddiquette informal and written by redditors rather than by Reddit. — [Reddiquette, Reddit Help](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/205926439-Reddiquette); [RedReplier, "Is the Reddit 9:1 rule still real?"](https://redreplier.com/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rule) [Primary via search summary / Vendor]
- A 2017 r/modnews announcement read: "effective one week from now, we are going to stop site-wide enforcement of the so-called '1 in 10' rule." — [RedReplier](https://redreplier.com/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rule); [byog.red guide](https://byog.red/guides/reddit-self-promotion-rules/) [Secondary quoting primary; 2017 = historical, still the current state per these sources]
- The current sitewide Spam policy contains no ratio. It asks users to be thoughtful about how often they post and evaluates overall behavior. — [RedReplier](https://redreplier.com/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rule); [Redship guide (2026)](https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules) [Secondary; Reddit's Spam page itself could not be fetched]
- Reddit's moderator help content says some communities disallow promotion entirely, while others use a 10% cap on a user's posting and comment history *within that community*, with the other 90% helpful, organic content. The old self-promotion wiki that described 10% as a rule of thumb is marked "no longer updated." — [RedReplier](https://redreplier.com/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rule); [IntentLoom](https://intentloom.com/resources/reddit-marketing/self-promotion-rules/) [Secondary]
- **Conflict:** some 2026 guides describe 9:1 as a sitewide guideline. That conflicts with Reddit's own documents and those sources appear less reliable. — [forkoff.xyz (2026)](https://forkoff.xyz/blog/reddit-marketing/reddit-self-promotion-rules-2026) vs [Reddiquette](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/205926439-Reddiquette)

### Inferences
- **Encodable Reddit rules for Inroad:**
  1. Before drafting any Reddit post, require a check of that subreddit's sidebar rules and wiki (promotion allowed? designated self-promo threads or days? flair? minimum account age or karma?).
  2. Default to a conservative cap of no more than ~10% of the founder's activity *in that subreddit* being self-promotional, even where not required.
  3. Prefer value-first formats (build-in-public story, lessons learned, answering questions) with the product mentioned only where the subreddit allows.
  4. Never draft content implying vote manipulation, sockpuppets or coordinated upvoting. Reddit's Content Policy prohibits these; the policy page was not fetched this session.
- Since Inroad drafts and never posts, it should attach a "rules checked?" confirmation step to every community move.

### Gaps
- Reddit's Spam policy and Content Policy pages, and Reddit's moderator self-promotion article, were not fetched directly (domain blocked). Exact wording on vote manipulation and multiple accounts is unverified this session.
- No sourced norms for Discord servers, Facebook groups, Hacker News "Show HN" rules, Indie Hackers or niche forums.
- No outcome data (signups per post, conversion) for Reddit-led consumer launches.

## 4. Launches: Product Hunt, App Store / Google Play featuring, waitlists and pre-launch, Kickstarter/crowdfunding, press

### Takeaway
**Apple featuring** is a documented, free channel. Nominate in App Store Connect with **at least 3 weeks' lead time** per the App Store Connect help, though Apple's marketing page says "at least two weeks" and "up to three months in advance." Pair it with **pre-orders** (2–180 days before release) and **In-App Events** (promotable up to 14 days ahead, up to 31 days long). **Product Hunt** is now editorially curated: moderators choose what is featured, and secondary sources say only about 10% of launches are featured, so its value for consumer products is uncertain. **Kickstarter** succeeds for roughly 39–42% of projects (third-party estimates). The strongest predictor in practitioner data is the pre-launch follower base: about 31% of followers convert to backers, per a Kickstarter employee as reported by a vendor. Robinhood- and Superhuman-style waitlist claims are poorly sourced, vendor-retold stories.

### Cited Findings
**Apple App Store featuring [Primary, read directly]**
- Featuring Nominations in App Store Connect come in three types: **New Content** (content, offers or events), **App Enhancements** (features or significant updates) and **App Launch** (launch or pre-order). Submitters can be Account Holder, Admin, App Manager or Marketing roles. — [Apple, Nominate your app for featuring](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring)
- Lead time: "we recommend submitting plans as early as possible, with a minimum lead time of 3 weeks." — [Apple, App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring). **Conflict:** Apple's "Getting featured" page says "at least two weeks' notice" and "up to three months in advance" for wider consideration. — [Apple, Getting featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)
- Nomination contents: name, type, detailed description (purpose, priority, update details), publish date or range, platforms, countries, localizations, an optional attached In-App Event (iPhone/iPad only), up to **5 supplemental URLs** (e.g., TestFlight public link, art) and optional details (accessibility, inclusivity, team story). Bulk CSV import is supported. No frequency limit is stated. — [Apple, Nominate your app for featuring](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring)
- Editorial criteria, with "no checklist of requirements": user experience, UI design, innovation, uniqueness, accessibility, localization, and the product page ("Compelling screenshots, app previews, and descriptions, as well as positive ratings and reviews"). "All apps and games are eligible for App of the Day and Game of the Day stories." Editors' Choice is curated per storefront. Apple may request promotional artwork by email. — [Apple, Getting featured](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/getting-featured/)

**Apple pre-orders [Primary]**
- For new apps, the release date must be **2 to 180 days** after the pre-order is published (2–365 days for existing apps entering new regions). On release day the app downloads automatically to pre-orderers' devices and they are notified. Apple does **not** notify pre-orderers of date changes or removal. Pre-orders are discoverable in search, can appear on Today/Games/Apps tabs if featured, and can be promoted with Apple Ads. Product page optimization tests can run on pre-order pages. The `preorder_date` receipt field / `AppTransaction.preorderDate` can be used to reward pre-orderers. — [Apple, Pre-orders](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/pre-orders/)

**Apple In-App Events [Primary]**
- Up to **15 approved** and **10 published** events at once; each up to **31 days** long; promotable up to **14 days** before start. Event name up to 30 characters, short description 50, long description 120. Badges: Challenge, Competition, Live Event, Major Update, New Season, Premiere, Special Event. Events appear on the product page, in search results (event card for past downloaders, screenshots for others), and in editorial and personalized recommendations. Apple gives no quantified lift. — [Apple, In-App Events](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/in-app-events/)

**Product Hunt (2024–2026)**
- Product Hunt's own changelog: "We've removed the 'Featured | All' toggle on the leaderboard in favor of a 'See all' expander… our moderation team still does choose what to feature above the fold." — [Product Hunt changes](https://www.producthunt.com/changes) [Primary via search summary; undated in summary]
- Secondary claims: only about 10% of launches are featured after an algorithm/curation change around January 2024; one blog says the featured rate fell to 25% in January 2024 and was about 16 products per day by September 2024. The figures conflict and are not from Product Hunt. — [Awesome Directories (2025)](https://awesome-directories.com/blog/product-hunt-launch-guide-2025-algorithm-changes/); [shno.co PH statistics](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/product-hunt-launch-statistics) [Vendor/Secondary]
- Editors reportedly judge on usefulness, novelty, craft and creativity, with undisclosed weighting. Blogs claim votes from established accounts weigh more, comments and sustained activity beat a single spike, and suspicious patterns are discounted. None of this was verified against Product Hunt documentation. — [Flowjam 2025 guide](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/how-to-get-featured-on-product-hunt-2025-guide); [Blazon agency 2026](https://blazonagency.com/post/product-hunt-launch-guide) [Vendor]

**Waitlists and pre-launch (Robinhood / Superhuman)**
- Robinhood: most sources agree on about **1 million waitlist signups before launch** (circa 2013–2014; historical). Accounts of the referral mechanics conflict: queue position moved by referrals, tiers such as "refer three people, move up 100 places," or free stock. — [Queueform](https://www.queueform.com/blog/how-did-robinhood-get-1-million-users-before-launch-with-just-a-waitlist); [Waitlister case studies](https://waitlister.me/growth-hub/blog/case-studies-successful-product-launches-powered-by-waitlists) [Vendor; details unverified]
- Superhuman: access was gated by an onboarding survey that screened out users the product could not yet serve, and/or a live ~30-minute onboarding call. Existing users could refer people on the waitlist. Waitlist size is reported as 180,000 in one source and 275,000 in another. — [Waitlister Superhuman case study](https://waitlister.me/growth-hub/case-studies/superhuman); [First to Market](https://www.firsttomarket.co/p/two-moves-to-turn-a-waitlist-into) [Vendor/Secondary; conflicting]

**Kickstarter / crowdfunding (hardware, DTC)**
- Overall success rate: about **39–42%** (third-party estimates; "39.11% worldwide as of 2025" in one, "~41.98%" in another). Category success rates (Statista, Jan 2025): comics 67.65%, dance 61.11%, theater 59.72%. — [Boost Your Campaign, crowdfunding statistics 2026](https://www.boostyourcampaign.com/blog/crowdfunding-statistics-2026); [Coolest Gadgets](https://coolest-gadgets.com/kickstarter-statistics/); [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/235397/distribution-of-kickstarter-funds-raised-by-successful-projects) [Secondary]
- Pre-launch followers: a Kickstarter employee reportedly said the average conversion of pre-launch followers to backers is **31%**; **29%** of total funding typically arrives on day one; and the Kickstarter dashboard reportedly says pre-launch followers may be "up to 300% more likely to back" than post-launch followers. — [Prelaunch.marketing academy](https://prelaunch.marketing/blogs/academy/average-conversion-rates-for-kickstarter-followers) [Vendor relaying a Kickstarter staff statement; not verified with Kickstarter]
- Vendor KPI: aim for about 10% conversion of qualified leads within 24 hours of launch. A rule of thumb, not a validated benchmark. — [Prelaunch.marketing](https://prelaunch.marketing/blogs/academy/average-conversion-rates-for-kickstarter-followers) [Vendor]

### Inferences
- **Encodable App Store launch timeline (iOS consumer apps):** at T-minus 3+ weeks (ideally up to 3 months), submit an App Launch featuring nomination with a TestFlight link and founder story. Publish the pre-order 2–180 days before launch and drive pre-orders from waitlist and community. Create an In-App Event, promotable 14 days ahead, for launch or major updates. Each major update gets its own "App Enhancements" nomination. All of this is free and suits Inroad's drafting model (Inroad drafts the nomination text).
- **Kickstarter rule:** because roughly 30% of followers back and about 29% of funding lands on day one (vendor-relayed figures), required day-one pledges imply a minimum follower count: followers needed ≈ (goal × 0.29) ÷ (0.31 × average pledge). This is an inferred planning heuristic; flag the inputs as vendor-relayed.
- **Product Hunt for consumer:** with editorial featuring at about 10% (unverified), Inroad should treat PH as optional and secondary for consumer products. It is more relevant for prosumer and tech-savvy audiences. Founders should never draft "please upvote" mass asks.
- **Waitlists:** referral-to-skip-the-line mechanics are folklore-level evidence. Inroad can suggest them, but should not cite Robinhood numbers as benchmarks.

## 5. SEO, programmatic SEO for consumer web, and ASO

### Takeaway
Apple's own docs give exact, encodable ASO rules. Name is 30 characters, subtitle 30, the keyword field 100 (comma-separated, no spaces, no repeats of words already in the name or subtitle), and promotional text 170 (**not indexed**). Use up to 10 screenshots, with the first 1–3 visible in search, and up to 3 app previews of 30 seconds each that autoplay muted. Search ranks on text relevance (title, subtitle, keywords, primary category) plus user behavior (downloads, ratings and reviews). The ratings prompt shows at most **3 times per 365 days**. Up to **70 custom product pages** can be keyword-targeted. Product page optimization allows **3 treatments, one test at a time, up to 90 days**. Consumer-web programmatic SEO could not be researched.

### Cited Findings
**Apple product page and search [Primary]**
- App name up to 30 characters; subtitle up to 30; keywords 100 characters total, "comma-separated, no spaces between terms"; promotional text up to 170 characters ("doesn't affect your app's search ranking"); up to 10 screenshots; up to 3 app previews of up to 30 seconds each, autoplaying muted. — [Apple, Creating your product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Keyword rules: no plurals of words already included, no category names or the word "app," no repeated words (including words already in the name, subtitle or category), avoid special characters, and never use trademarks, celebrity names, competitor names or irrelevant terms ("a common reason for App Store rejections"). Popular terms "are highly competitive – especially if you are a newer or smaller app"; less common terms "drive lower traffic, but are less competitive." — [Apple, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/); [Apple, Product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/)
- Ranking: "Search results are based on a number of factors, including text relevance (matches for your app's title, subtitle, keywords, and primary category), as well as user behavior (downloads, ratings and reviews, and more)." The primary and secondary categories are indexed. Search results can include LLM-generated "app tags" based on App Store Connect metadata. — [Apple, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)
- Display: rating and "up to three screenshots or app previews may display in search results depending on the platform and image orientation." Make the first screenshots "highlight the essence of the app," one main benefit per screenshot. The description's first sentence is the only text visible before "more." Promotional text can change anytime without a new version; the description requires a version update. — [Apple, Product page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/); [Apple, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)

**Ratings and reviews [Primary]**
- "You can prompt for ratings up to three times in a 365-day period." Ask "when users are most likely to feel satisfaction with your app, such as when they've completed an action, level, or task. Make sure not to interrupt their activity." — [Apple, Ratings and reviews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/)
- The summary rating is per territory and can be reset with a new version ("we recommend using this feature sparingly"). Resetting does not remove written reviews, and "having few ratings may discourage potential users." Respond to reviews in App Store Connect; the reviewer is notified and may update. If you can't respond to all, prioritize the lowest-star reviews and those about technical issues, and reply promptly after major releases. — [Apple, Ratings and reviews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/)
- Ratings "can influence how your app ranks in App Store search." — [Apple, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/)
- Google Play In-App Review API: there is a "time-bound quota" whose exact value is not published ("an implementation detail" that can change). Calling it more than once in a short period, such as "less than a month," might not show a dialog. Do not ask questions before or while showing the card ("Do you like the app?" / "Would you rate this app 5 stars?"), do not modify or overlay the card, and do not trigger it from a call-to-action button. Requires Play Core 1.8.0+ and Android 5.0+. — [Android Developers, In-App Review](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/in-app-review)

**Custom product pages and product page optimization [Primary]**
- Up to **70** custom product pages (screenshots, promotional text and previews vary, each with its own URL). Pages can be assigned keywords to appear in organic search, with each keyword combination unique to one page. They can be used in Apple Ads and in ad networks via SKOverlay or SKStoreProductViewController identifiers. Deep links require iOS/iPadOS 18+. — [Apple, Custom product pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)
- Apple's own performance claim, as summarized from the page: "an average increase of 2.5 percentage points in conversion when referring people to a custom product page," shown alongside a "1.6%" default-page figure. The summary's exact framing may be imprecise, and this is a platform claim, not independent data. — [Apple, Custom product pages](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)
- Product page optimization: "A test can include up to three treatments" (icon, screenshots, previews); "A test runs for 90 days or until you manually stop it"; "You can create one test at a time"; results report impressions, conversion rate, improvement and confidence. Apple recommends waiting for "at least 90% confidence." Tests without icon changes can be reviewed independently of an app version. Requires iOS/iPadOS 15+. — [Apple, Product page optimization](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page-optimization/)

### Inferences
- **Encodable ASO checklist for Inroad (iOS):**
  - Name of 30 characters or fewer, containing the brand plus the top keyword.
  - Subtitle of 30 characters or fewer, stating the value, with no repeated words from the name.
  - 100-character keyword field with no spaces after commas, no repeats or plurals, no competitor names.
  - Screenshots 1–3 carry the core benefit, one benefit per screenshot.
  - Promotional text for news only, since it is not indexed.
  - Rating prompt only after a success moment, at most 3 per year.
  - Respond to all 1–2 star reviews after each release.
  - Run one PPO test at a time with up to 3 treatments, stopping at 90% or more confidence.
  - Build custom product pages per paid or creator channel, with keywords mapped to intent.
- **Early-stage keyword strategy (inferred from Apple's trade-off statement):** new apps should target less competitive long-tail terms first, since popular terms are "highly competitive – especially if you are a newer or smaller app."
- **Android parity:** never put a "Rate us" button wired to the In-App Review API, and assume at most about one prompt per user per month.

### Gaps
- Google's spam policies on "scaled content abuse" and site reputation abuse (relevant to consumer programmatic SEO) could not be fetched (developers.google.com blocked), and no consumer pSEO case studies (e.g., template or listing pages) were retrieved.
- No independent App Store conversion-rate benchmarks by category (impression-to-install, product-page-to-install) were retrieved.
- Google Play store listing limits (title, short description) and Play's custom store listings and experiments were not retrieved.

## 6. Paid acquisition: when to start, minimum test budgets, creative cadence, post-ATT realities, benchmarks

### Takeaway
Early-stage evidence says paid is **rarely** what kickstarts a consumer company: Lenny's marketplace data lists paid among the "not common" early levers, and his consumer research frames first-1,000 tactics as unscalable and non-paid. Once paid starts, post-ATT measurement is better than feared. AppsFlyer reports about **50% global ATT opt-in** among users shown the prompt (April 2025), but about **30%** if users who never see the prompt are included, and other vendors report about 35% or lower. Meta's learning phase is widely cited (unverified here) as needing about **50 optimization events per ad set per 7 days**, which sets a de facto minimum budget of roughly target CPA × 50 per week. Benchmarks are mostly vendor-published and vary sharply by methodology.

### Cited Findings
**Timing**
- In the marketplace data, performance marketing ranked #8 of 10 supply levers, and "Not common: SEO, Paid." — [Lenny on X, Nov 2019](https://twitter.com/lennysan/status/1198997647807275009); [list](https://twitter.com/lennysan/status/1198997653549277184) [Primary via search summary; 2019 = historical]
- Lenny's first-1,000 strategies are "doing things that don't scale … direct engagement rather than paid channels." — [echai.ventures recap of Lenny 2020](https://echai.ventures/stream/how-biggest-apps-got-first-1000-users-lenny-rachitsky-breakdown) [Secondary]

**Meta learning phase and minimum budget (third-party; not verified against Meta)**
- The learning phase usually ends after about **50 optimization events within 7 days** per ad set. One newsletter reports accounts exiting at about 20–30 events, which Meta has not confirmed. — [AdManage.ai guide](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-learning-phase-guide); [Aimerce newsletter](https://newsletter.aimerce.ai/p/stuck-in-meta-s-learning-phase-here-s-how-to-fix-it) [Vendor]
- Budget heuristics: a daily budget floor of target CPA × 50 ÷ 7, or at least 10× target CPA per ad set. Budget edits greater than about 20% at once, creative swaps and audience changes reportedly reset learning. A single vendor claims learning exits stretched to 7–14 days after an April 2026 Meta update (uncorroborated). — [Pixis.ai](https://pixis.ai/blog/ai-learning-phases-why-advertisers-kill-campaigns-too-early/); [Cometly](https://www.cometly.com/post/facebook-ads-learning-phase-optimization) [Vendor]

**ATT opt-in and attribution**
- AppsFlyer (April 2025 release): about 50% of users consent to tracking globally, "up 10 points since ATT launched"; France just under 51%, Germany 47%. iOS ad spend grew 26% from 2023 to 2024 vs 10% on Android. — [AppsFlyer newsroom, "Mobile ad market thrives 4 years after ATT"](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/post-att-growth/) [Vendor primary]
- AppsFlyer Q1 2024: opt-in 51% for gaming and 46% for non-gaming, among users who saw the prompt. Including users who never see it (restricted or system-level denial), the rate drops to about 30%. - **Conflict:** Adjust reportedly showed about 35% average opt-in in Q2 2025 (via a roundup), and a 2026 stats page claims 27% with no verifiable source. The differences come from denominators (prompted users vs all users) and app mix. — [Digital Applied 2026 stats](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/mobile-app-marketing-statistics-2026-install-data); [AdExchanger on why opt-in numbers vary](https://www.adexchanger.com/data-driven-thinking/att-opt-in-rates-the-picture-so-far-and-the-ugly-truth-behind-why-the-numbers-vary-so-widely/) [Secondary]

Games only, so this is an indirect reference for consumer apps. — [Liftoff 2025 Casual Gaming Apps Report](https://liftoff.ai/2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/); [Liftoff blog highlights](https://liftoff.ai/blog/highlights-2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/) [Vendor primary]

- 2024 report overall trial-to-paid median was 37.3% (third-party citation). — [PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/trial-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmarks) [Secondary]

### Inferences
- **Encodable "start paid" gate for Inroad:** do not suggest paid as a primary move until there is (a) evidence of retention or organic pull from unscalable channels, and (b) enough budget to hit roughly 50 optimization events per ad set per week. Weekly test budget ≈ 50 × target CPA per ad set. Example: a $10 target CPA needs about $500/week per ad set (about $70/day). If that is unaffordable, optimize for a cheaper upstream event such as trial start or registration. The 50/7 threshold is third-party-cited and should be verified with Meta.
- **iOS-first consumer apps:** Apple Search Ads is usually the most intent-rich first paid test, with tap-to-install around 66% and CPT around $1–2.25 averages (vendor data). Pair ads with a custom product page per keyword theme.
- **Funnel sanity checks for subscription apps:**
  - Download-to-trial around 5–7% is median for the region; 15%+ is top quartile in North America.
  - Trial-to-paid of 35–45% is typical by category.
  - Hard paywalls show about 5x higher D35 download-to-paid than freemium (10.7% vs 2.1%). This is a model choice that changes viable CAC.
- **Attribution reality:** assume no more than about 50% of iOS users are user-level attributable (and about 30% of all users). Plan for SKAN/AdAttributionKit aggregates, onboarding "how did you hear about us" surveys, and incrementality-style tests rather than user-level ROAS.

## 7. Channel strategy frameworks: Four Fits, Bullseye/Traction, "one channel at a time"

### Takeaway
Two frameworks give precise, encodable sequencing rules. **Brian Balfour's Four Fits** says products must be built for channels (channels don't mold to products), and that the channel must fit the model's ARPU/CAC economics. A third-party reading adds that a company with product-channel fit typically gets **70%+ of its growth from one channel**. **Weinberg and Mares' Bullseye** (Traction, 2014/2015): brainstorm at least one idea for each of 19 channels, run cheap tests on the 2–3 most promising, then focus on the 1–2 that clearly outperform, and rerun when a channel saturates. Both match the empirical Lenny finding of one channel, at most three.

### Cited Findings
- Four Fits: Market-Product Fit, Product-Channel Fit, Channel-Model Fit and Model-Market Fit. Balfour says all four are needed to reach $100M+ in a venture time frame, and that the fits influence each other, so none can be treated in isolation. — [Brian Balfour, Four Fits for $100M+ Growth](https://brianbalfour.com/four-fits-growth-framework) [Primary via search summary; original circa 2017, historical]
- Balfour's 2025 rewrite for the AI era says products are built for channels, not the other way around. His older HubSpot-era essay: "channels do not mold to products." — [Balfour, The Four Fits: A Growth Framework for the AI Era](https://blog.brianbalfour.com/p/the-four-fits-a-growth-framework); [Balfour, Building a growth framework towards $100M](https://brianbalfour.com/essays/hubspot-growth-framework-100m); [Substack note on rewrite](https://substack.com/@brianbalfour/note/c-159831185) [Primary via search summary]
- Channel-Model Fit: "your channels are determined by your model and vice versa," framed on an ARPU-to-CAC spectrum. The 2025 rewrite notes that some AI products' freemium models can't make freemium economics work. — [Balfour 2025 rewrite](https://blog.brianbalfour.com/p/the-four-fits-a-growth-framework) [Primary via search summary]
- Third-party summary: a company with product-channel fit gets "70%+ of their growth from one channel." This is not confirmed in Balfour's own text. — [GTM Foundations](https://gtmfoundations.substack.com/p/why-product-market-fit-isnt-enough); [Antoine Buteau, Lessons from Brian Balfour](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-brian-balfour/) [Secondary]
- Bullseye / Traction (Weinberg and Mares, 2014; expanded 2015): 19 channels — viral marketing, PR, unconventional PR, SEM, social and display ads, offline ads, SEO, content marketing, email marketing, engineering as marketing, business development, sales, affiliate programs, existing platforms, trade shows, offline events, speaking engagements, community building, and targeting blogs. — [Jonathan Rintala book summary](https://jonathanrintala.com/book-summary/traction-a-startup-guide-to-getting-customers/); [Growth Method](https://growthmethod.com/traction-channels/) [Secondary]
- Bullseye process: "Brainstorm at least one idea for each of the 19 channels (outer ring). Pick the two or three most promising and run small, cheap tests with clear success criteria (middle ring). Focus your resources on the one or two channels that clearly outperform the rest (inner ring)." Five steps: brainstorm, rank, prioritize, test, focus. Rerun when a channel saturates or the stage changes. A podcast summary says Weinberg ran it six or seven times at DuckDuckGo (unverified). — [Sprints & Sneakers](https://www.sprintsandsneakers.com/insights/the-bullseye-framework-what-is-it-and-how-do-you-apply-it); [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/growth-frameworks/bullseye-framework); [SaaS Club podcast with Weinberg](https://saasclub.io/podcast/gabriel-weinberg-duckduckgo-2/) [Secondary]
- Empirical match: most consumer apps used one of the seven first-1,000 strategies, and none succeeded with more than three; marketplaces used a median of two supply levers (see Section 1). — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-the-biggest-consumer-apps-got); [Lenny on X](https://twitter.com/lennysan/status/1198997647807275009)

### Inferences
- **Encodable sequencing rules for Inroad's consumer playbook:**
  1. Pick the channel that fits the product and the model. Low-ARPU consumer apps cannot support sales-like, high-touch channels at scale, except temporarily in the unscalable phase.
  2. Test 2–3 channels cheaply, with explicit success criteria and a fixed window.
  3. Commit about 70%+ of effort to the one winner.
  4. Revisit only when the channel saturates.
- **Daily moves (at most 5):** in the test phase, spread moves across the 2–3 test channels; in the focus phase, put at least 3 of 5 moves into the primary channel.
- **Stage mapping (inference):** 0→100 and 100→1,000 use unscalable "go where they gather," personal network and targeted outreach (Lenny). 1,000→10,000 is where Bullseye testing of scalable channels starts (content/short-form, ASO, referral loops, community, then paid). The phase boundaries are the analyst's synthesis, not sourced numbers.

### Gaps
- Balfour's own wording on "one channel" focus, and the exact 70% claim, could not be confirmed (blog domains blocked).
- Traction's "50% rule" was not described in any retrieved source. From prior knowledge it is believed to mean spending about 50% of time on traction in parallel with product, but that is unverified this session and should not be encoded without checking.
- Casey Winters (Reforge) essays on growth loops and channel sequencing were not retrieved.

## 8. Most common early consumer GTM mistakes documented by practitioners

### Takeaway
A dedicated search for documented mistakes could not be run before the search budget ran out. The anti-patterns below come only from what the retrieved sources say or imply: starting with too broad an audience; waiting for users instead of recruiting them by hand; spreading across too many channels; leaning on a launch platform's algorithm; violating community rules; and starting paid before the product has pull or the budget can clear learning thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- **Too-broad audience:** Lenny calls starting with "a *very* narrow audience" "one of the most counterintuitive parts" of launching a large B2C business. — [Lenny on X](https://x.com/lennysan/status/1546873103262707712); [Step 2](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/consumer-business-super-specific-who) [Primary via search summary]
- **Waiting for users to come:** "You can't wait for users to come to you. You have to go out and get them." — [Paul Graham, 2013](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html) [Primary via search summary]
- **Too many channels:** "No one found success from more than three" strategies. — [Lenny 2020 via Founder's Journal](https://foundersjournalpod.morningbrew.com/how-to-get-your-first-1000-users/) [Secondary]
- **Leaning on SEO or paid too early in marketplaces:** both are "Not common" early levers. — [Lenny on X](https://twitter.com/lennysan/status/1198997647807275009)
- **Treating 9:1 as permission to self-promote everywhere:** subreddit rules override; some subreddits ban promotion entirely. — [RedReplier](https://redreplier.com/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rule) [Secondary]
- **Using competitor or trademark names in ASO keywords:** "a common reason for App Store rejections." — [Apple, Search](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/) [Primary]
- **Wiring rating prompts to buttons, or asking "do you like the app?" first:** prohibited by Google Play guidance; Apple advises asking only after task completion and without interrupting. — [Android In-App Review](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/in-app-review); [Apple, Ratings and reviews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/) [Primary]
- **Underfunded paid tests that never exit learning ("Learning Limited"):** linked to low budgets, narrow audiences, rare conversion events and weak tracking. — [AdManage.ai](https://admanage.ai/blog/facebook-ads-learning-phase-guide); [Cometly](https://www.cometly.com/post/how-to-improve-facebook-ads-learning-phase) [Vendor]
- **Kickstarter launches without a pre-launch follower base:** about 29% of funding arrives on day one, and pre-launch followers are reportedly up to 300% more likely to back. — [Prelaunch.marketing](https://prelaunch.marketing/blogs/academy/average-conversion-rates-for-kickstarter-followers) [Vendor]
- **Missing Apple featuring windows:** a minimum of 3 weeks' lead time is recommended. — [Apple ASC Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring) [Primary]

### Inferences
- Anti-patterns Inroad should flag (encodable as warnings on proposed moves):
  - an ICP broader than one super-specific segment;
  - more than 3 active acquisition channels;
  - paid before retention or organic pull is shown;
  - paid budget below about 50× target CPA per week per ad set;
  - a Reddit or community post without a confirmed rules check;
  - ASO keywords containing competitor names;
  - rating prompts not tied to a success moment;
