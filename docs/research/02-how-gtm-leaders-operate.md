# How elite seed–Series B GTM leaders actually operate (2025–2026)

## 0. Buyer facts that shape the job

| Fact | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| How far into the journey the buyer gets before talking to a seller | **61%** in 2025 (down from ~69%) | https://6sense.com/report/buyer-experience/ |
| First contacts started by the buyer, not the seller | **79%** | same |
| Deals won by a vendor on the "Day One" shortlist | **95%** (85% the year before); the favorite before first contact wins ~80% | same |
| B2B buyers who prefer a buying process with no sales rep | **61%** (Gartner, June 2025, n=632); a later survey reports 67% (secondhand) | https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience ; https://distributionstrategy.com/2026/03/most-b2b-buyers-prefer-rep-free-purchasing-as-ai-reshapes-sales/ |
| Buyers who actively avoid suppliers that send irrelevant outreach | **73%** | Gartner, same release |
| Rep time spent on non-selling work (admin, prep) | **70%** | https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/sales-ai-statistics-2024/ |
| Cold email reply rates in 2025 | roughly **3.4–5.1%** and falling (vendor data) | https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks ; https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-is-a-good-benchmark-for-reply-rates-in-cold-outreach |

**What this means:** the job is to get onto the Day-One shortlist before the buyer starts looking. That happens through trust: the founder's network, warm intros, peer rooms such as dinners, and founder content. Relevant, timely, human outreach comes second. Volume sending is a losing strategy.

Lenny's study of about 20 B2B companies found the same order of trust: personal network first, then going where customers already gather, then press, cold outreach and launches (https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-win-your-first-10-b2b-customers).

**Stage benchmarks**
- **$0–100K ARR:** the founder personally lands the first 10–20 customers, many from cold outreach (https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-founders-playbook-for-scaling-to-1-million-arr).
- **First sales hire:** around $1M ARR, after 7–10 customers. Hire two at once, because roughly 50% of these hires fail. The best profile is a former founder or a product-deep engineer who can stand in for the founder (Jen Abel, https://www.usetranscribe.io/yt/37fKFWdrMyA/enterprise-sales-playbook).
- **Vanta:** relied on word of mouth for its first ~600 customers, with no real website (https://review.firstround.com/vantas-path-to-product-market-fit/). It found early customers by emailing older YC companies and searching YC's internal forum for people mentioning compliance (Lenny, above).

## 1. Task inventory: cadence, time, and great vs. average

Time figures are my estimates for a single founding GTM operator at seed. Sources are cited where they exist.

### Daily

| Task | Time/day | Great | Average |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inbox and lead triage (speed to lead)** | 20–30 min | Replies to inbound and warm replies in under 1 hour. HBR found firms that responded within an hour were ~7× more likely to qualify the lead than firms that waited longer, and 60×+ more likely than those waiting 24h+ (https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711). | Replies the same or next day, so the lead goes cold. |
| **Signal scan** (funding, exec hires, job posts, launches, a target's posts, competitor news) | 15–20 min | 3–5 "why now" triggers a day, each tied to a named person and a specific angle. | Scrolls LinkedIn with no list. |
| **Prospecting "power hour"** (research plus writing) | 60–90 min | 10–20 hand-written notes of 3–4 sentences that open with a counterintuitive insight about the reader's role. Jen Abel writes every note by hand and avoids AI-style opening lines ([Lenny](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/master-founder-led-sales-jen-abel), [transcript](https://www.usetranscribe.io/yt/37fKFWdrMyA/enterprise-sales-playbook)). BVP says to use AI for research and personalization, not bulk email. | 500-contact sequences with merge fields. |
| **Meeting target** | ~2 hrs | PostHog's early daily target was 2 meetings, about 10/week or ~480/year, and each conversation unlocked ~2 more contacts (https://posthog.com/blog/first-10-paying-customers). | No meeting target; activity counted instead of conversations. |
| **Follow-ups** | 20–30 min | Every open loop has a dated next step. Instantly reports the first follow-up adds 40–50% more replies; a secondhand figure says ~48% of reps never send one. | "Just bumping this." |
| **Calls (discovery, demo)** | 1–3 hrs | 10–15 min of prep per call. Books the next meeting before hanging up; treats "I'll email you" as a soft no ([Lenny/Jen Abel](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/master-founder-led-sales-jen-abel)). | Demos on the first call; leaves without a next step. |
| **Post-call recap and CRM** | 5–10 min per call | Recap email within hours with decisions, open questions, owners and dates. Stakeholder map updated. | CRM updated Friday from memory. |
| **LinkedIn engagement** | 25–30 min | Van der Blom's routine: 15 min after posting replying and engaging with 5–10 peers, plus 5–10 thoughtful comments in 2nd/3rd-degree feeds (https://creatorscience.com/podcast/richard-van-der-blom-2/). | Likes only, or engagement pods (LinkedIn reportedly detects pods with ~97% accuracy, same source). |

### Weekly

| Task | Time/wk | Great | Average |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pipeline review** (Monday) | 30–45 min | 3–5 deals inspected for buyer evidence (details in §5). | Reads every deal aloud in round-robin. |
| **Account research for the top 20 accounts** | 2–3 hrs | Stakeholder map, trigger events, a hypothesis of the pain, and a warm path into each. | Firmographics only. |
| **Founder content** | 2–3 hrs | 2–3 posts (van der Blom: 2–4 per week is now optimal, down from 5–6). | Posts daily with no topic focus, or not at all. |
| **Warm-intro asks** | 1 hr | One list of no more than 20 targets per connector, plus a forwardable email for each (§3). | "Let me know if you know anyone." |
| **Call review** | 1 hr | Reviews 2–3 recordings for talk ratio, monologues and questions asked. | Never listens back. |
| **Customer check-ins and expansion** | 1–2 hrs | Proactive value reviews; asks for references and referrals at "win moments". | Only reacts to tickets. |
| **Competitive intel** | 30 min | Battlecard updated from what was actually heard on calls. | A one-time doc. |
| **Event pipeline** | 1–2 hrs | Invites, confirms and reminders for the next dinner; pre-booking meetings for the next conference. | Organizes everything the week before. |
| **Friday retro** | 30–45 min | Reply rate by segment and message, objections heard, wins/losses, one change for next week. | None. |

### Monthly and quarterly

- **Executive dinner or roundtable.** Monthly. Gainsight CEO Nick Mehta runs near-monthly dinners for 10–12 CS leaders as a customer-feedback ritual (https://docs.superhuman.com/@mehtaphysical/the-customer-first-toolkit-how-i-meet-500-customers-a-year/dinner-series-28).
- **Investor update** with a standing "asks" section.
- **Win/loss synthesis**, then an ICP and messaging refresh.
- **One case study or reference** produced.
- **Launch something.** YC's Kat Manalac argues launching is continuous, not one shot (https://thefoundingjourney.substack.com/p/yc-startup-library).
- **Quarterly:**
  - Pricing and packaging review.
  - Event calendar. Conference speaker applications close **6–9 months** ahead (https://oakesd.substack.com/p/stop-hating-conferences-how-to-sell).
  - Board metrics.

### Per deal

1. **Pre-call brief:** the person, the company, triggers, a pain hypothesis, 5 questions.
2. **Discovery:** about the problem, not the product. Measure the pain: if the buyer isn't already spending time or money on it, it isn't a priority (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jen-abel/).
3. **Multi-thread.** Gong found closed-won deals have **67% more contacts** than closed-lost (https://www.gong.io/resources/guides/the-data-backed-guide-to-multi-threading-and-team-selling). Upwork wins 58% of deals with at least 4 contacts involved (https://www.gong.io/win-rates). If procurement stalls, you are usually talking to someone too junior (Jen Abel).
4. **Co-author the deal** with the champion: demo, pilot, success metrics, ROI. Prefer short paid pilots whose agreed metrics automatically trigger the larger contract. Avoid free enterprise trials.
5. **Pricing:**
   - Enterprise lands at **$75–150K**. Avoid $10K deals: they give false product-market-fit signal and anchor price low.
   - Never discount without getting something back, such as a case study or a multi-year term.
   - Design partners rarely convert and are hard to upsell (Jen Abel sources above).
6. **Trust artifacts.** Security docs and a SOC 2 readiness letter. One speedrun founder's stalled enterprise deal moved the same morning he received Vanta's in-progress letter (https://speedrun.substack.com/p/ai-tools-for-gtm-and-sales).
7. **Close, then onboard.** The founding AE often runs onboarding and feeds insights to product (https://www.flowla.com/blog/what-it-means-to-be-a-founding-ae-in-2025).

## 2. Executive dinners and events

### 2.1 Why dinners are the format right now

- MKT1 (Emily Kramer) calls dinners "the new trade shows": about 12 people who are already in pipeline or evaluating, mixed with champions and warm accounts. It frames dinners as a differentiator in a market flooded with AI-generated content (https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/executive-dinners-are-the-new-trade-shows).
- Vendor benchmarks (treat as directional):
  - Event-sourced leads convert to opportunities at ~40%, against 4.82% for other channels (Vendelux, cited by SignalFire: https://www.signalfire.com/blog/curated-dinner-playbook-for-startups).
  - 35–60% of dinner attendees go on to a meeting.
  - Cost per senior opportunity is $1.4–2.5K at dinners versus $25–50K at summits (https://samaaro.com/b2b-events/executive-dinner-events-small-format-strategy-for-senior-b2b-pipeline/).
- I could not find a published, primary-source dinner playbook from Vanta, Ramp, Retool or Notion.
- Practitioner sources I did find:
  - SignalFire and BCV (VC platform teams).
  - Gainsight (Nick Mehta).
  - Murray Newlands (100+ dinners; "vetting is the entire game").
  - Gong co-hosting with 6sense (https://www.partnervista.co/blog-top-5-ways-to-turn-executive-dinners-into-pipeline).
  - Clay's community model: it reports 90+ city clubs and 250+ events per year (https://www.clay.com/fr/community-hub).

### 2.2 Goal and theme

- **One primary goal per dinner** (SignalFire). Pick one: accelerate open deals, create new pipeline, expand customers, gather market intelligence, or build thought leadership.
- **Make the theme a specific, live question.** "How CFOs are actually measuring AI ROI" beats "the future of AI."
  - Survey guests beforehand and adjust. BCV dropped AI topics when its survey showed AI fatigue (https://baincapitalventures.com/insight/the-bcv-approach-to-executive-dinners/).
  - Update themes as buyers' decisions change, e.g. AI governance for CISOs (https://murraynewlands.substack.com/p/what-ive-learned-running-100-executive).

### 2.3 Guest list: size, mix, vetting

**Size**
- 10–12 for the first dinners (SignalFire cap); about 12 per Newlands; 10–12 at Gainsight.
- Conversation fragments above 14 (Samaaro). Under 8 feels thin (https://getboothiq.com/blog/executive-dinner-playbook).
- With no private room, keep it under 10.

**Composition (12 seats)**

| Seat type | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hosts | 2 | Founder plus one: a GTM lead or an exec sponsor. No SDRs or AEs filling seats (Samaaro). |
| Happy customers or champions | 2 | Peer voice. Dinners where customers tell the story produce more pipeline than host-led ones (vendor). |
| Anchor | 1 | An industry leader or respected operator who gives a 5–10 minute framing (SignalFire). A senior customer host is ideal. Non-customer anchors may need a $2.5–10K honorarium (Samaaro). |
| Priority prospects | 6–7 | Open deals first (agreed with whoever owns the deal), then target accounts. If the champion can't come, ask which colleague would benefit. |
| Connector (optional) | 0–1 | An investor or advisor who knows several guests. |

**Vetting rules**
- Before inviting anyone, define 8–10 "must-attend" accounts. If 5 or more decline, reschedule (Samaaro).
- Match guests by seniority so they are true peers. No direct competitors (BCV).
- The cut test: would you be happy if this person were the only one who showed up? (getboothiq)
- Decline poor-fit guests, even friends or sponsors (Newlands).

### 2.4 Funnel math and timeline

- Senior buyers accept **15–25%** of invitations, so 12 seats need **50–80 invites** (Samaaro).
- With strong invite sequencing, one agency moved acceptance from ~12% to **30–45%** (https://www.ortusclub.com/blog/event-strategy/executive-attendance-service-b2b-events-strategy/).
- Show rate is about **70–90%**. One working funnel ran 18 confirmed → 1–2 late cancels + 1–2 no-shows → 12–15 attended (Ortus; https://vendelux.com/event-marketing/b2b-event-marketing-benchmarks).
- **Rule:** confirm about 15 to seat 12.

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| T-5 to 6 weeks | Pick theme and goal, build the must-attend list, line up the anchor and customer host. Book the venue (premium NYC rooms open on Resy 30 days out, per BCV). |
| T-3 to 4 weeks | Personal invites from a named senior person, not a mass email (getboothiq). Notes from a customer host convert better than notes from the company's own reps (Samaaro). |
| T-2 weeks | One follow-up (two touches total). If they decline, ask "Is there a colleague who should be there instead?" |
| T-1 week | Confirm dietary needs and send logistics. Over-confirm the backfill list. |
| T-48 hours | Briefing book to the internal team: headshot, title, deal stage, challenges, desired outcome, planned introductions. Run a team walkthrough (SignalFire). |
| T-1 to 2 days | **Personal reminder from the original inviter** that references what this guest wants from the evening. SignalFire calls this the most effective attendance lever. Offer Uber credit. |
| Day of (about 2 pm) | Short text: "See you at 6:30, private room upstairs, I'm on [phone]." |
| Never | Charge no-show fees. Damaged relationships cost more than empty seats (SignalFire). |

### 2.5 Invite copy (templates)

The core principle is to lead with the guest's business challenge and the specific people they will meet, not the restaurant (SignalFire). Keep it plain and personal, and say outright that there is no pitch.

**A. Founder to prospect**
```
Subject: Small dinner on [specific question] — [date], [city]

[First name] — I'm hosting 10 [CFOs / heads of platform eng] for dinner on [Tue Nov 12] at [venue] to compare notes on [specific question, e.g., "what AI spend is actually paying back"].

[Name], [title] at [peer co], is coming — given [their specific situation, e.g., your move upmarket], I think you two would get a lot from each other. [Anchor name] will open with 10 minutes on [topic].

No slides, no pitch, off the record. Would love to have you.

[Founder name]
```

**B. Customer host to peer**
```
Subject: Dinner — [topic]

[First name], I'm co-hosting a small dinner with [startup] on [date] for a few [role]s wrestling with [problem]. I've found these genuinely useful. Any interest? I'll make sure you're seated next to [name].
```

**C. 48-hour reminder from the inviter**
```
[First name] — looking forward to Thursday. You mentioned [X]; [Name] has dealt with exactly that, I've seated you together. 6:30 at [venue], private room upstairs. Uber's on us: [code]. Any dietary needs?
```

### 2.6 Venue and seating

**Venue**
- A private room with good acoustics: a different floor if possible, carpets or curtains, no all-concrete rooms. Visit at peak hours to test noise (BCV).
- 3–4 courses. Not a 2–4 hour tasting menu (SignalFire).
- Close to where guests are staying.
- Round tables. For more than 10 guests, use two rounds (getboothiq).

**Seating** is planned in advance:
- Customer host at one end, exec sponsor at the other.
- Seat priority prospects next to customers who faced the same problem, not isolated beside the host.
- The AE sits near, but not next to, priority accounts (Samaaro).
- Double-sided place cards are required; skip name badges (SignalFire).

### 2.7 Run of show (about 3 hours)

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 6:30–7:05 | Cocktails. Don't seat people as they arrive (SignalFire). |
| 7:05 | Seat. Host welcome under 5 minutes, plus norms: off the record, no recording, no attribution, no pitch (Newlands). |
| 7:10–7:25 | Introductions: name, role, what you're working on, plus one personal question (Gainsight uses "What's your secret superpower?"). |
| 7:25–7:35 | Anchor framing, 5–10 minutes. |
| 7:35–8:40 | Facilitated discussion over the main course. 2–3 prepared prompts plus 3–4 in reserve. The host draws in quiet guests and stops anyone dominating (Samaaro, Newlands). |
| 8:40–9:10 | Dessert at a side table or with seats switched, to mix the room. |
| 9:10 | Close with one observation that ties the discussion together, then personal thanks. Photos come from one designated person. |

**Prompt bank** for AI and dev-tool buyers (my synthesis):
- "What's one AI project you killed this year, and what told you to kill it?"
- "Where has AI actually changed a headcount plan, and where is it theater?"
- "What would you need to see before letting an agent touch production?"
- "What did your board ask about AI that you couldn't answer?"
- "What's a vendor promise you'll never believe again?"

**No-pitch rule:** the product comes up only if a guest asks (Samaaro). Sponsors get at most a brief introduction (Newlands).

**Lead capture:** between courses, step out and record a ~30-second voice note per guest covering needs, timing and next steps (getboothiq).

### 2.8 Follow-up

- **Within 24 hours:** internal debrief per guest:
  - their biggest challenge
  - what they wish a product did
  - peers they named
  - recurring themes

  Log it all in the CRM (SignalFire).
- **Next morning, before guests fly home:** a personal note that references a specific thing they said and proposes one concrete next step (getboothiq).
- **24–48 hours:** a group email with photos, 3–5 themes, and everyone's LinkedIn profile. Make any promised introductions with context on why (Newlands).
- **After that** (Samaaro), at most **5 touches in 90 days**, all human and none automated:

  | Timing | Touch |
  |---|---|
  | Week 1 | Specific follow-up |
  | Week 4 | A relevant piece of content |
  | Day 60 | Direct meeting request |
  | Day 90 | Deal in motion, or move to nurture |

### 2.9 Measurement

- Add a CRM field, "dinner attended", with date, theme, host and a summary.
- Pipeline created within 90 days counts as **sourced**; later pipeline counts as **influenced**.
- Report at 30 days (meetings), 90 days (pipeline), and 180 days to 12 months (closed-won) (Samaaro).
- **Key metrics:** attendee→meeting rate (target 35–60%), senior opportunities per dinner, cost per senior opportunity.
- **Cost:**
  - $3.5–7K for 10 guests (getboothiq).
  - About $5K per dinner (Samaaro).
  - Co-hosting with a non-competing company that sells to the same buyer splits cost and merges guest lists (SignalFire; Gong and 6sense did this).

### 2.10 Conferences: no booth, side events, pre-booked meetings

1. **Pick events by ICP density.** 400 target attendees beat 4,000 attendees of whom 10% fit (https://notanotherceo.substack.com/p/the-11-step-playbook-to-crush-it). Ask organizers for attendee lists, last year's list, or at least titles and company profiles (oakesd).
2. **Pre-book meetings.**
   - One sales leader pays for a rep's ticket only after they have booked at least 10 prospect meetings (oakesd).
   - Contact existing pipeline early, and new ICP prospects about 2 weeks out.
   - Tier the list: 20–40 tier-1 accounts get personalized multi-touch outreach; 60–100 tier-2 get two touches. Plan on 3–4 named contacts per meeting you want (https://lemniscategrowth.com/blogs/pre-booked-meetings-at-conferences.html).
3. **Side event instead of a booth.**
   - A dinner or happy hour just outside the venue, co-hosted with a company that has the same ICP.
   - BetterCloud rented the bar across from the venue for about the cost of a booth, and another year the hotel lobby bar that attendees passed at check-in (notanotherceo).
4. **Next-step ladder on the floor**, in order of preference (oakesd):
   1. Book next week's meeting on their phone.
   2. Get their number and confirm by text.
   3. Get their email.
   4. Hand over a card.
5. **Logistics:** write 30-second notes after each conversation. Hold a morning team huddle. Follow up within 48 hours. Also write to the ICP attendees you missed, which has produced deals (oakesd). One speedrun founder filled her calendar with in-person bank meetings from a last-minute conference campaign (speedrun).

### 2.11 Webinars and roundtables

**Webinars**
- ON24 platform averages: 57% (2024 data) to 60% (2025) of registrants attend (https://www.on24.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-webinar-benchmarks-report/). Third-party data puts live attendance nearer 35–45%. Startups will see far smaller absolute numbers than ON24's averages.
- BVP notes webinars feed both inbound and outbound and support co-selling.
- Formats that suit startups: a customer co-host, a live teardown or build, or 45 minutes of Q&A.

**Roundtables**
- 6–12 executives under the Chatham House rule, with a moderator. Limit to non-competing companies (https://www.ortusclub.com/blog/thought-leadership/virtual-events-b2b-executives/).
- Before inviting, write a one-page brief: purpose, value to participants, desired outcome, topics in and out of scope, recording rules, and who owns follow-up.
- A virtual roundtable for 6–8 people over 60 minutes is the cheapest "dinner rehearsal."

## 3. Warm intros

### 3.1 Mining the network (works without paid data)

1. **Map your 1st-degree network.** Founders, angels, investors, advisors, ex-colleagues, customers, and your YC batch or alumni.
   - Vanta mined YC alumni; Amplitude got intros into a network of ex-Zynga PMs; Gusto told everyone it knew and asked for referrals (Lenny, first 10 customers).
   - One speedrun founder built a dashboard combining public data with the founders' LinkedIn connections because legal buyers respond to warm intros (speedrun).
2. **Find who your connectors know** (https://www.ascend.vc/blog/best-practices-for-requesting-warm-intros-from-your-investors).
   - Open the investor's LinkedIn connections and filter by title and company.
   - Build a list of **no more than 20 targets**, each with name, title, company and LinkedIn URL.
   - Send the list and ask which ones they're comfortable introducing.
3. **Write one forwardable email per chosen target.** Add company background below your signature, and link the target's profile.
4. **Use double opt-in.** The connector checks with the target before introducing. This etiquette traces to Fred Wilson in 2009 (https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/the-double-opt-in-email-introduction/). Unvetted one-sided intros annoy recipients (https://waseem.substack.com/p/double-opt-in-intro).
5. **Close the loop.** Move the connector to BCC on your first reply, and later tell them how it went.
6. **Make it a habit.** Every monthly investor update gets a standing "Asks" section with 3–5 named targets.
   - First Round ran outbound for Sprig with a target of 5 meetings a week, checked every two weeks (Lenny).
7. **Customer referrals.** At a win moment (go-live, a measured result), ask for two named peers.

### 3.2 Templates

**Ask to the connector (the wrapper)**
```
Subject: Quick intro ask — [Target name], [Company]

Hi [Investor] — 3 people from the list you marked that I'd love intros to. Forwardable note for [Target] below; happy to rewrite. No worries at all if timing's off.
```

**Forwardable blurb**

Structure: one line on who you are, why the target should care, one proof point, one small ask, and "CC me" (https://www.commsor.com/post/forwardable-email; https://nickraushenbush.substack.com/p/the-double-opt-in-email-7a2975f3bf02).

```
Subject: Intro: [Founder] ([Startup]) <> [Target], re: [their specific problem]

[Connector] — would you be open to introducing me to [Target]?

I saw [Target]'s team [specific trigger: launched X / is hiring 4 platform engineers / spoke at Y about Z]. We help [role] at [company type] [outcome] — e.g., [peer customer] cut [metric] by [N] in [time].

I'd value 20 minutes of [Target]'s perspective on [problem] — even if we're not a fit, I'll share what we're seeing across [N] similar teams.

Thanks, [Founder]
—
[Startup]: [one line]. Backed by [investors]. [1 traction line].
```

**Close the loop (after the meeting)**
```
[Connector] — thanks again for the intro to [Target]. We met Tuesday; [one concrete outcome]. Owe you one — anyone I can help you meet?
```

## 4. Founder brand and content that drives inbound

### 4.1 LinkedIn in 2026

Source: Richard van der Blom, analysis of 1.3M posts (https://creatorscience.com/podcast/richard-van-der-blom-2/).

**Reach**
- Down about 60% over two years for active creators. Engagement fell only 20–25%, so engagement per view is rising.
- Suggested posts to non-followers make up about 9% of the feed. You earn them through engagement in the first minutes.

**Cadence**
- 2–4 posts a week (2–3 for most people), down from 5–6.
- Posting irregularly, when you have something worth saying, raised reach about 10–15%.

**Format mix**

| Format | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Text plus image | 2 per week | Core format |
| Carousel / document | 1 per week | |
| Text-only | Avoid | Only works for top-5% writers |
| Polls, video | Avoid | |
| Standalone articles | Avoid | About 0.4× reach |
| LinkedIn newsletter | Recommended | Outperforms posts on reach, engagement and conversion; ~80% of his own LinkedIn conversions |

**Topic and profile**
- Keep about 80% of posts on one core topic plus related subtopics.
- Off-topic comments dilute how LinkedIn classifies you.
- A profile rewrite lifted reach within three weeks.

**Caveat:** creator audiences are not B2B buyers. Measure inbound DMs and meetings, not likes.

**Founder anecdotes** (secondary sources, unverified): RB2B's Adam Robinson posts daily, mostly stories, with a hard CTA in only about 1 post in 10 (https://saastock.com/blog/how-adam-robinson-cracked-founder-led-marketing). Vendor guides estimate inbound DMs that mention your posts start in 3–6 weeks and sourced pipeline in 9–12 weeks.

**Post formats that suit technical founders** (my synthesis):
1. **"What I learned from N calls with [persona]."** A counterintuitive insight, in the same spirit as Jen Abel's outbound advice.
2. **Build-in-public numbers:** a metric, what changed, why.
3. **Teardown or how-we-did-X,** with a screenshot or code.
4. **Customer story** in the customer's words, tagged, with permission.
5. **Contrarian take** on a live debate in the category.
6. **Launch or milestone post.**
7. **Dinner recap:** themes from the room, Chatham-safe, tagging attendees who agree to it.

### 4.2 Launch channels

**Show HN** (https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)
- Must be something people can try now: ideally no signup, not a landing page, not a waitlist, not a minor version bump.
- Asking friends to upvote or comment is banned.
- Post body, in order: who you are; one sentence on what it does; the problem; the backstory; the solution with technical detail; what's different; a request for feedback.
- Tone: modest, no superlatives. Show transparent pricing and link the GitHub repo.
- Answer every comment fast. With critics, find something to agree with first (https://newslepear.beehiiv.com/p/56-how-to-launch-on-hacker-news-brand-vs-product-messaging-on-the-homepage-and-an-awesome-1-2-3-how).
- Fits dev tools and infrastructure; non-technical B2B struggles there.
- Precedents: Segment launched on HN; Front reached about 3,000 beta signups from HN, Twitter and its blog (Lenny).

**Product Hunt**
- Expect roughly **50–300 signups** for a B2B tool. About 60% of traffic arrives on day one. Visitor-to-signup conversion runs about 1–2%. Rank did not predict revenue (https://hub.causo.ai/guides/product-hunt-launch-2026-realistic-playbook ; https://hub.causo.ai/guides/product-hunt-traffic-data-2026).
- Treat it as a credibility asset, not a pipeline engine.

**X launch videos** (vendor benchmarks)
- AI tools are about 51% of startup launches on X (https://ugcscout.com/blog/startup-launch-video-benchmarks-x).
- The first 3 seconds decide it. Name the pain and cut for muted autoplay. "We added AI" is not a launch.
- Line up about 50 genuine supporters to engage in the first 30 minutes (https://okara.ai/viral-launch-x-handbook).

**Cadence:** "launch again and again" (Kat Manalac, YC). Aim for one meaningful launch a month across HN, YC Launch, LinkedIn, X or a newsletter.

## 5. Rituals and dashboards

### 5.1 Daily rhythm (founding GTM)

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 8:00–8:30 | Signals and inbox triage. Reply to anything warm in under an hour. |
| 8:30–10:00 | **Power hour.** 10–20 hand-written touches plus follow-ups due. |
| 10:00–16:00 | Calls. A 10-minute brief before each; recap plus booked next step after each. |
| 16:00–16:30 | LinkedIn engagement (25–30 minutes) and one content draft. |
| 16:30–17:00 | **Close-out.** Every open deal has a buyer-committed, dated next step; CRM is current. |

### 5.2 Weekly rhythm

**Monday pipeline review (30 minutes)** (https://louddemand.com/resources/pipeline-review-agenda/ ; https://saleslabelconsulting.com/pipeline-review-meeting/)

| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Snapshot: total pipeline, coverage, week-over-week change | 5 min |
| 3–5 priority deals, chosen for risk or stage rather than size | 20 min |
| Blockers | 10 min |
| Owned next steps | 5 min |

**Questions to ask of every deal:**
- Did it move in the last week, with evidence from the buyer?
- Is the next step committed by the buyer and dated?
- Is it stalled?
- Is it multi-threaded (at least 3 contacts)?
- Do we know the champion, the decision process, the timeline, the paperwork process, and the success metrics?

**Rules:**
- Every deal discussed leaves with an owner and a date.
- Review deals, not people.
- To check whether the review format itself works, track three numbers over 8 weeks: pipeline coverage, week-over-week pipeline change, and the count of deals with an overdue next step (https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/sales-deal-review-process).

**Rest of the week**
- **Tuesday:** content (2 posts from last week's call insights).
- **Wednesday:** warm-intro asks and investor or advisor touches.
- **Thursday:** events: dinner funnel and conference pre-booking.
- **Friday retro (30–45 minutes):**
  - Reply rate by segment and message, and which angles won.
  - Top 3 objections heard.
  - Review 2–3 call recordings.
  - Wins and losses with reasons.
  - One experiment for next week.
- **Forecast:** at seed, a monthly forecast call is enough. Run a weekly deal review alongside it.

### 5.3 Metrics to watch

**Leading indicators**
- New conversations held per week (PostHog's bar: about 10).
- Meetings booked from each source: warm intro, dinner, content, cold, event.
- Warm-intro funnel: asks sent → intros made → meetings.
- Dinner funnel: invited → accepted → attended → meeting.
- Reply rate by segment.
- Speed to lead (median minutes).

**Pipeline**
- New pipeline in dollars per week.
- Coverage of about 3× the target (a common rule of thumb).
- Stage conversion and time in stage.
- Share of deals with at least 3 contacts.
- Count of deals with an overdue next step.

**Outcomes**
- Win rate. About 25–35% is cited as healthy for enterprise (Jen Abel, secondhand).
- ACV and sales cycle length.
- Logo quality: tier-one logos pull in referrals.

**Customers**
- Time to value, retention or NRR, references available, expansion pipeline.

## 6. What separates elite operators from average ones

| Dimension | Elite | Average |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed** | Under 1 hour to warm replies; recap the same day; dinner follow-up the next morning. | Next day or never. |
| **Next steps** | Books the next meeting on the call. Every deal has a dated step the buyer agreed to. Reads "I'll email you" as a no. | Next steps are the rep's own tasks. |
| **Multi-threading** | Maps 4 or more stakeholders. Goes to an executive when deals stall. | Single-threaded with a friendly user. |
| **Listening** | Gong 2025 data (326K calls): won deals had 57% rep talk time versus 62% in lost deals. Won calls had **15–16 questions versus ~20** in lost ones. Shorter monologues, a 2-second pause before replying, restating what the buyer said (https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/). | Pitches features; works through a question checklist. |
| **Qualification** | Says "this might not be a fit" on the first call. Measures whether the pain is real. | Keeps zombie deals to pad pipeline. |
| **Pricing courage** | Lands at $75–150K in enterprise. Runs paid pilots with success metrics that trigger a contract. Discounts only for something in return. | $10K pilots that never expand. |
| **Relevance and taste** | A narrow, researched list; hand-written notes; one segment at a time; tier-one logos first (BVP, Jen Abel). | Volume sequences, which 73% of buyers say make them avoid the vendor. |
| **Network generosity** | Makes introductions for others; dinners create value between guests. | Only asks. |
| **Learning loop** | Treats every deal as market research and pushes insights into product and ICP (Flowla). | Keeps call insights in their head. |
| **Readiness** | Trust artifacts (security docs, references, ROI case) ready before they're asked for. | Scrambles at procurement. |

## IMPLICATIONS FOR THE PRODUCT

The design principle: **the AI does the 70% of non-selling work and prepares every human moment, and the founder spends 60–90 minutes a day on conversations and decisions.** It is a trust engine, not a sending engine.

### A. What the AI should own end to end (the founder only reviews)

1. **Research briefs.** Accounts, people, pre-call one-pagers, and the dinner briefing book: headshot link, role, triggers, pain hypothesis, 5 questions. Built from web search and fetch of public sources: team pages, press, job posts, podcasts, GitHub, conference speaker pages.
2. **Signals feed.** A daily web-search sweep of the top 20–50 accounts (funding, exec hires, job posts, launches, a target's public posts) and competitor moves. Ranked by "why now" strength.
3. **List building with no paid data.**
   - Target accounts from public sources.
   - Conference speaker and attendee lists.
   - Warm-path maps from the founder's own LinkedIn connections export (a CSV the user uploads), crossed with the target accounts.
   - One list of up to 20 targets per investor or advisor.
4. **Deal hygiene.** The founder pastes notes or a transcript. The AI produces:
   - the recap email draft
   - extracted next steps
   - updated stage and stakeholder map
   - a single-thread warning that suggests 2–3 likely additional stakeholders found by web search
5. **Monday pipeline brief.** Snapshot, 3–5 at-risk deals with evidence, overdue next steps, deals with fewer than 3 contacts, coverage against goal.
6. **Friday retro.** Reply rates by segment and angle, objection clusters, win/loss patterns, one recommended experiment.
7. **Event operations.**
   - Dinner planner: goal → theme → must-attend list → invite schedule → funnel tracker (confirm 15 to seat 12) → reminder timeline.
   - Seating chart, timed run of show, prompt bank, and the 24-hour debrief template.
   - The 90-day follow-up cadence (max 5 touches), plus the 30/90/180-day attribution report.
   - Conference pre-booking plan (tier 1 and tier 2).
8. **Asset drafting.** Case studies from call notes, battlecards, the investor-update "Asks" section, Show HN and Product Hunt drafts that follow the rules above, and a pricing-page critique.

### B. What the AI should co-pilot (it drafts and recommends; the founder edits and sends via copy or open-in-Gmail)

| Task | Why the human stays in the loop |
|---|---|
| Personalized outreach, capped at about 10–20 per day | It goes out under the founder's name. Relevance has to be checked against the 73% avoidance stat, and v1 has no sending anyway. |
| Forwardable intros and connector asks | They spend the investor's social capital, and double opt-in etiquette applies. |
| Dinner invites and 48-hour reminders | They work because they come personally from a named inviter. |
| LinkedIn and X posts | The founder's voice and story are the asset. The AI turns call insights into drafts in the 2–3 per week format mix. |
| Guest-list selection and seating | Requires judgment ("vetting is the entire game"). |
| Discovery prep, mutual action plans, pilot success metrics, pricing proposals | The AI drafts the structure; the founder co-authors it with the champion. |
| Which conferences to attend; what to launch | Strategic choices with budget behind them. |

### C. What must stay human (and why)

- **The conversations themselves:** discovery, demos, negotiation, closing. About 80% of early sales come from the founder's conviction (https://www.antoinebuteau.com/lessons-from-jen-abel/), and speedrun founders who automate heavily still keep demos, relationships and closing human (speedrun).
- **Hosting the room.** Dinners, roundtables and conference floors. Presence is the product.
- **Spending relationship capital.** Asking investors, customers and friends for favors.
- **Public engagement as the founder.** No auto-comments or pods: LinkedIn reportedly detects pods, and authenticity is the point.
- **Commitments.** Pricing, contracts, custom work, and anything that touches the founder's identity or reputation.
- **Hard guardrails:**
  - No auto-send.
  - No LinkedIn automation (ToS risk; speedrun founders cite a cap of about 200 connection requests a week).
  - No fabricated personalization or claims.
  - No mass sequences.

### D. The daily loop the product runs

1. **Overnight (AI):** signal sweep, follow-ups coming due, and drafts for each.
2. **"Today" screen (5 minutes)**, with no more than 5 ranked actions. Examples:
   - (1) Reply to [warm lead], drafted, with a timer since the lead came in.
   - (2) Three follow-ups due, drafted.
   - (3) Two meetings today, briefs attached.
   - (4) One intro ask ready for [investor].
   - (5) One post draft from yesterday's call insight.
3. **Power hour (45–60 minutes):** approve and edit 10–20 notes, open each in Gmail, mark sent. The AI logs the touch and sets the next step.
4. **After each call (3 minutes):** paste notes. The AI returns a recap draft, next step, stage change and stakeholder gaps.
5. **Close-out (2 minutes):** the app blocks "done for the day" until every open deal has a dated next step, or the deal is explicitly parked or killed.

### E. The weekly loop

| Day | Ritual the product runs | Founder time |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Pipeline brief plus the week's targets (conversations, intros, posts, event funnel) | 30 min |
| Tue | Content: 2 posts drafted from last week's insights; newsletter monthly | 45 min |
| Wed | Warm-intro day: connector lists and forwardables; investor "Asks" | 30 min |
| Thu | Events: dinner funnel actions (invites, confirms, reminders), conference pre-booking | 30 min |
| Fri | Retro: what worked, objections, win/loss, one experiment; ICP and messaging tweaks | 30 min |
| Monthly | One dinner or roundtable (full planner), investor update, one case study, one launch | 1 evening plus 2 hrs |

### F. The product's own scorecard

Measure what elite operators measure, not activity volume:
- Conversations held per week (target about 10)
- Warm intros made
- Dinner attendee→meeting rate (target 35%+)
- Median speed to lead
- Percentage of deals with at least 3 contacts and a dated next step
- New pipeline per week
- Founder hours spent on GTM admin, which should trend toward zero
