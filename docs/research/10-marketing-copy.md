# Marketing that sells and sounds human: what worked 2020–2026 (researched October 2026)

*Method note: researched 2026-10-26 for Studio (`src/lib/studio/`). Six parallel research passes covered Wispr Flow, B2B (Ramp first), developer and software launches, attention campaigns, AI tells and copy craft, and channel specs. The session's network policy blocked direct fetches of most sites, so most quotes and figures come from search-engine excerpts of the cited pages, public GitHub source or podcast transcripts; spot-check a line before using it publicly. The shared web-search budget ran out before B2B research beyond Ramp (Gong, Stripe, Vanta and others) and the B2B effectiveness literature (95:5, Binet & Field, Gartner) could be verified; those are left out rather than stated from memory. Company results are self-reported unless marked otherwise.*

The marketing campaigns that worked between 2020 and 2026 had the same shape in every segment. Each made **one true claim against an enemy the reader already resented**: the keyboard for Wispr Flow, the card issuer's interest in more spending for Ramp, Firebase for Supabase, ads in a chatbot for Claude. Each said it in plain, concrete words. A real person delivered it: a founder filming on an iPhone, a creator saying a line they would have said anyway, or the engineer who built the feature. And it was repeated through many cheap, stacked launch moments with a number attached. That shape is also what makes copy read as human. Wikipedia's editors describe AI writing as "regression to the mean", where specific facts are swapped for generic praise that sounds important. So the specificity that sells is the same property that defeats AI tells. Sounding machine-made has a measurable cost once readers notice. In seven preregistered experiments, perceived AI authorship of emotional messages lowered word of mouth and loyalty, and a 3,000-person survey tied suspected AI content to roughly half the trust. Yet AI work tested blind can score as well as human work, so the penalty comes from detection, not from the tool. Specificity also carries legal risk: Oatly's "73% less CO2e", DoNotPay's "robot lawyer" and Cluely's retracted $7M ARR show that claims which sound checkable must actually check out. Copy then has to fit platform limits that range from a 30-character Google Ads headline to a 3,000-character LinkedIn post, counted in four different units. Three caveats apply to everything below. Almost every campaign result is self-reported. No source links any specific line to conversion under controlled conditions. And most verbatim quotes come from search-engine excerpts, because direct page access was blocked during the research, so lines used as training examples should be spot-checked first. For Inroad, this points to a harness that pulls real specifics out of founders and customers, arranges them into proven structures for each channel, and runs a critic that checks specificity, substantiation, AI-tell density and platform limits before anything ships.

## Wispr Flow turned one villain into two years of copy

Wispr Flow is the clearest modern case of a startup whose copy, channels and product demo all said the same thing. The company dropped its silent-speech wearable on July 18, 2024 and shipped the wearable's software layer as a Mac dictation app. By founder Tanay Kothari's account, the team built a marketing operation in about six weeks and launched on October 1, 2024 ([Wispr](https://wisprflow.ai/post/pmf-through-pivot)). Launch day stacked four moments, each with its own hook:

- **Press got a number.** A Business Wire release was headlined "Wispr Raises $12M and Launches Wispr Flow to Replace Keyboards With Voice" ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240930079034/en/Wispr-Raises-$12M-and-Launches-Wispr-Flow-to-Replace-Keyboards-With-Voice)).
- **Hacker News got a deal.** The Show HN post offered an HN-only 33% discount and a permanent free tier of 2,000 words a week ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41696153)).
- **Product Hunt got the listing.**
- **X got a demo.** Kothari says he shot the video over a weekend with "a ring light and iPhone camera", at no cost ([X/@tankots](https://x.com/tankots?lang=en)).

The company reports "millions of views" and **#1 on Product Hunt for the day and the week**; both claims are self-reported ([Wispr](https://wisprflow.ai/post/pmf-through-pivot)). The top Hacker News comment asked whether the app was "fully local, or is there a cloud involved?" That objection came back a year later as a privacy controversy.

The argument never changed; only the wording got sharper. "Replace Keyboards With Voice" (2024) became the command "Don't type, just speak". That became a numeric claim, "4x faster than typing", paired with a banner admitting the category's history: "Voice that finally works is here" ([wisprflow.ai](https://wisprflow.ai/)). The "finally works" line speaks to readers who have been let down by Siri and built-in dictation, and a separate page argues against "built-in voice mode" directly ([Wispr](https://wisprflow.ai/why-flow)). The speed multiplier crept up from "up to three times faster than typing" in the 2024 release ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240930079034/en)) to **"4x" in 2026, and no public test backs either number**. The enemy frame paid off with creators because it matches what a happy user says unprompted. Creator Shorts open with lines like "I stopped typing months ago and I'm not going back" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/VZTg8yxkpwI)). Sprites.ai's frame-by-frame analysis of Wispr's Meta ads found the hook is usually "the line a creator would have said anyway", with on-screen captions present by 0.3 seconds in 65% of ads ([Sprites.ai](https://www.sprites.ai/blog/wispr-flow-ads-strategy)). The founder's own posts are lowercase and first-person: "i grew up in delhi dreaming of building tech millions of people couldn't live without" ([X/@tankots](https://x.com/tankots/status/2048605683969396844)). The 2026 India brand film rests on a line from Wispr's India lead: "Typing makes us edit ourselves down; speaking lets us be more of who we are" ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/advertising/wispr-flows-bologe-toh-tum-dikhoge-explores-what-we-say-better-out-loud-12483185)).

Two less visible mechanics fed the copy. First, Kothari personally onboarded the first 500 users over Google Meet and watched their reactions, with the rule "Find the things that repeatedly create delight, make sure that never dies" ([Frontlines](https://www.frontlines.io/podcasts/tanay-kothari/)). Those moments, such as filler words disappearing and text formatting itself, are exactly what the demos and creator ads show. That link is an interpretation of the sequence, not something the company has stated. Second, pricing and referral turn habit into revenue:

- **The free tier caps usage, not time**, so light users build the habit and heavy users hit the wall.
- **The referral pays only after the friend becomes a real user.** Both sides get a free month "when they hit 2,000 words or upgrade to Pro" ([Wispr](https://wisprflow.ai/students)).
- **Conversion is high but unaudited.** A teardown reports about **20% free-to-paid conversion** in early 2025, a company figure with no cohort detail ([Product Growth](https://www.productgrowth.blog/p/wispr-flow-growth-teardown)).

Each platform became its own launch moment: Windows in March 2025, iOS in June 2025, and Android in February 2026 with six months of Pro given away ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/wispr-flow-launches-an-android-app-for-ai-powered-dictation/)). India got local pricing at ₹320 a month, a Hinglish voice model and a film by a local agency. It became the No. 2 market, though with 14% of installs it produced only about 2% of in-app revenue ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/09/voice-ai-in-india-is-hard-wispr-flow-is-betting-on-it-anyway/)). Funding announcements were written for users. The Series B post was titled "Our Series B, and what it means for you" and led with a new speech model promising a "near-zero edit rate" ([Wispr](https://wisprflow.ai/post/series-b)).

From late 2025 the growth engine became expensive:

- **Newsletters.** Growth lead Matt Swulinski ran about 100 newsletter sponsorships a month through an agent-built "marketing OS" ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/podcasts/inside-the-ai-marketing-os-running-wispr-flow-s-growth-team)). One newsletter vendor credits its own placement with about $3.9M in tracked sales value and 4,500 attributed signups; the vendor published those figures itself ([Superhuman AI](https://advertise.superhuman.ai/cs-wispr)).
- **Creators.** Head of content Hector Serrano built a program that pays creators **7% of the ad spend behind each video**, uncapped, paid weekly, with a $1,000 floor for new creators ([UGC Roster](https://www.ugcroster.com/blog/wispr-flow-performance-based-creator-program)). About 50 creators now ship 500 to 700 Meta ads a month ([doomers.ai](https://doomers.ai/guides/ugc-creator-program)).

Outside counts show what that volume yields. Growthcurve found 1,549 live ads, of which only 26 had reached more than 100,000 people; the top 10% of ads drew 94% of all reach ([Growthcurve](https://growthcurve.co/wispr-flow-meta-ads-creative-testing-machine)). Sprites measured the median ad's lifespan falling from 20 days to 11 after the August 2026 raise ([Sprites.ai](https://www.sprites.ai/blog/wispr-flow-ads-strategy)). Critics doubt the economics:

- Laura Roeder wrote that "the odds are VERY good that these ads aren't profitable" and that Wispr's aim is "max growth, not ROAS" ([X/@lkr](https://x.com/lkr/status/2109242841968849171)).
- At a $30,000 monthly budget, 7% is about $2,100 to split across the whole creator roster ([Sundae Collective](https://sundaecollective.com/insights/pay-creators-on-performance-wispr-flow/)).
- When Swulinski left, Aakash Gupta observed that "The system broke the first time he tried to hand it to someone else" ([X/@aakashgupta](https://x.com/aakashgupta/status/2085376950961680662)).

The traction is large and almost all self-reported. In November 2025 Wispr told TechCrunch it had 70% 12-month retention and users at 270 of the Fortune 500 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/20/as-its-voice-dectation-app-takes-off-wispr-secures-25m-from-notable-capital/)). In August 2026 it raised $280M at a $2B valuation, citing "millions of users", more than 60 billion words dictated and **more than 125,000 companies** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/17/wispr-raises-280m-at-2b-valuation-as-it-looks-beyond-dictation/)). Reuters reported "more than 10,000 enterprises" for the same round ([Reuters/Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/wispr-flow-valued-2-billion-134218985.html)). A harness should treat these as examples of how a startup presents traction, not as benchmarks.

The 2025 privacy incident has a similar evidence problem. A user found screenshots of his active window being uploaded, and the CTO apologized, but the episode is documented mostly by competitors selling on-device dictation ([Voibe](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/is-wispr-flow-safe/)). Wispr's later HIPAA post names Ramp, Superhuman, Mercury and Clay as companies whose procurement it passed ([Wispr](https://wisprflow.ai/post/hipaa-is-here)), which reads as trust repair.

The lessons from Wispr that transfer are the cheap ones:

- a villain object plus a command to stop using it;
- a "finally works" line for categories with a history of disappointment;
- a founder demo whose payoff is visible within seconds;
- a launch day with a separate hook for each channel;
- onboarding calls mined for hook material;
- a referral that pays only after the friend becomes a real user.

The creator machine and the newsletter volume need venture money, and nobody has shown they are profitable.

## Ramp sold a corporate card built to make you spend less

Ramp's founding line argued against its own industry's business model. The February 12, 2020 launch post called Ramp "the first and only corporate card that helps your company spend less" ([Glyman](https://medium.com/@eglyman/ramp-the-corporate-card-that-helps-your-company-spend-less-97ca5a0f86f)), and TechCrunch's headline repeated the promise ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/02/12/ramp-is-a-corporate-card-focused-on-helping-you-spend-less)). Card issuers earn more when customers spend more. A card that promises less spending is believable because the promise seems to cost the seller something. Fortune was still calling it Ramp's "counterintuitive secret to success" in October 2025 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/15/eric-glyman-ramp-ceo-interview-leadership-next/)). Over six years the product description widened from "card" to "corporate card and expense management platform" in August 2020 ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/ramp-expands-into-expense-management-fastest-growing-corporate-card-in-america)) and later to spend management, but the promise stayed fixed.

The current slogan, **"Time is money. Save both."**, leads Ramp's savings page and calculator in search excerpts, and partner pages copy it word for word ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/savings); [OMNIA Partners](https://www.omniapartners.com/suppliers/ramp)). One researcher found it only on a third-party page, so the live wording should be checked. As copy, the line serves two buyers at once: money for the CFO who signs, time for the finance staff and employees who file expenses. That reading is ours; Ramp has not stated it as the intent.

Ramp's proof structure is the most reusable B2B pattern in the research, and so is its failure mode. A homepage capture pairs the slogan with a scoreboard in real units: "Over 20 million hours saved for 30,000+ customers" ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/?rc=FV6FQ2)). A dated release put the totals at $10 billion and 27.5 million hours as of August 31, 2025 ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/ramp-reaches-1-billion-in-annualized-revenue)). The savings percentage has a footnote and a stated method: "We calculate average savings as a percentage of an illustrative customer's total card spending." Ramp calls the result "an estimate, not a guarantee" and says it may count savings outside card spending ([Ramp Help Center](https://support.ramp.com/how-ramp-can-save-your-business-time-and-money)).

The wording drifts from page to page:

| Where | What it says |
|---|---|
| Card landing page ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/corporate-cards)) | "up to 5%¹ savings" |
| Help center ([Ramp Help Center](https://support.ramp.com/how-ramp-can-save-your-business-time-and-money)) | "5% or more on average" |
| A partner page ([Builtfirst](https://zendesk.builtfirst.com/ramp-expense-management)) | 3.5% average |
| Savings page ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/savings)) | 27.5M+ hours, $10B+ |
| Savings calculator ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/savings-calculator)) | 27M+ hours, $12B+ |

**"Up to" and "on average" are different claims that need different evidence.** A harness that treats them as synonyms will produce exactly this kind of inconsistency.

Ramp also writes the comparison pages its buyers search for, including pages about two rivals at once. Posts such as "Brex vs. Mercury vs. Ramp" place Ramp inside a comparison the buyer was already making. They compete on facts a reader can check: Brex Premium at $12 per user per month against Ramp Plus at $15, which openly shows a rival tier cheaper than Ramp's own ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/brex-vs-mercury-vs-ramp)). They also stress where Ramp is easier to qualify for: an EIN plus $25,000 in the bank, against $50,000 or more for Brex ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/brex-business-credit-card-requirements)). One alternatives page uses a reported Capital One acquisition of Brex to say "Ramp remains independent" ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/top-brex-alternatives)). This research could not independently confirm that acquisition, which shows the risk of building copy on a rival's news.

The Ramp AI Index turns transaction data Ramp already has into a monthly public statistic. It was built from card and bill-pay data covering 30,000+ businesses, now more than 70,000, and is published on the St. Louis Fed's FRED database ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/data/how-we-built-the-ramp-ai-index); [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/RAMPAICONSTRUCTION)). Its headlines read like news ("Business AI adoption flatlines") rather than marketing ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/data/business-ai-adoption-flatlines)). The March 2026 reading of **50.4% adoption** passed half for the first time ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/data/april-2026-ai-index)).

Since 2025 Ramp has added big brand campaigns. Each one dramatizes the same pain, with a famous face matched to the buyer:

- **Super Bowl, February 2025.** A 15-second spot showed investor Saquon Barkley "buried in a mountain of expense reports". It was conceived, shot and finished in seven days, and that production story became press coverage in its own right ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/09/here-are-five-startups-that-are-running-super-bowl-ads-this-year/)). Barkley's quoted line was "Ramp shares my obsession with speed and efficiency" ([AfroTech](https://afrotech.com/saquon-barkley-investor-ramp)).
- **Flatiron Plaza stunt, October 2025.** Actor Brian Baumgartner, known as the accountant Kevin Malone in *The Office*, processed receipts in a glass box for seven hours. Ramp estimated about 10,000 attendees, about 380,000 livestream viewers and at least 85 million views ([Marketing Brew](https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/10/22/ramp-viral-livestream-brian-baumgartner)). LBB later said more than 100 million ([LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/Ramp-Super-Bowl-LX-Brian-Baumgartner)). Both figures came from Ramp.
- **Super Bowl, February 2026.** The in-house spot "Multiply What's Possible" buried Baumgartner in receipts again ([LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/Ramp-Super-Bowl-LX-Brian-Baumgartner)).

On October 15, 2026 Ramp reported **annualized revenue above $2 billion**, 85,000+ customers and a $60B valuation ([PR Newswire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20261015ny73055/ramp-reaches-60-billion-valuation-as-annualized-revenue-tops-2-billion)). One fintech outlet put its share of the corporate card market near 1.5%, against roughly 33% for Amex ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2025/10/254295-fintech-ramp-aims-to-challenge-incumbents-in-corporate-card-market/)). No public data separates what the brand spending contributed from what product and sales did.

The other B2B cases follow the same logic at smaller scale. Gong bought Super Bowl airtime in only three metro areas for about $1.5M in 2021 ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctech/articles/0,7340,L-3928472,00.html)) and says the ad "helped create the company's biggest-ever sales pipeline" ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/we-just-got-tickets-to-the-big-game)). Clay named a job and then a profession. At its August 2025 Series C the CEO said "GTM engineering represents the first true AI-native profession" ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250805719448/en/AI-GTM-Leader-Clay-Raises-%2524100M-Series-C-to-Fuel-GTM-Engineering-Roles-Industrywide)). A podcast later titled an episode "He Created a Job Title 1,000+ People Now Have" ([Gainsight](https://www.gainsight.com/episode/he-created-a-job-title-1000-people-now-have-ft-yash-tekriwal-clay/)), while a critic asked whether the label was "Self-Serving Marketing" ([GTMBA](https://gtmba.substack.com/p/gtm-engineer-revolutionary-superhero)).

**The widely quoted B2B effectiveness research could not be verified here.** That includes the LinkedIn B2B Institute's 95:5 rule, Binet and Field's brand-versus-activation budget split, and Gartner's buying-group figures. They are left out of this report, and a harness should not hard-code those numbers until someone checks the original sources. What the verified Ramp material does support is a **proof-first order for B2B deliverables**: the claim, then a number with its unit, then a footnoted method, then an interactive calculator that admits "results are an estimate, and not a guarantee" ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/savings-calculator)).

## Developer tools borrowed a rival's name, then shipped in public

Developer and software launches produced the most durable short lines in the research. They share a habit of plain verbs and named comparisons. Each example below is followed by what it shows.

- **Supabase** introduced itself in 2020 as "an open source Firebase alternative" ([Supabase](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/www/_blog/2020-08-05-supabase-auth.mdx)), so a reader could place it in one clause. Its 2026 homepage reads "Build in a weekend / Scale to millions" above "Supabase is the Postgres development platform" ([Supabase source](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/www/data/home/content.tsx)).
- **Linear** used "The issue tracking tool you'll enjoy using", with the subline "It's built for high-performance teams", from February 2021 to July 2025 ([Linear](https://github.com/linear/linear/commit/3a1906970affdf824e8b7ec5512529067ca05b03)). It then switched to "The purpose-built tool for planning and building products" ([Linear](https://github.com/linear/linear/commit/b69a515c04710963dcdd6d6d8bca5e55f4be0dca)).
- **Cursor**'s README went through four one-line descriptions in two weeks of March 2023. They included "an editor built for pair programming with AI" ([Cursor](https://github.com/getcursor/cursor/commit/568c7df)) and the jab at a rival "an AI that's smarter than Copilot". One version opened with an honest admission of scope: "It's early days, but right now Cursor can help you with a few things..." ([Cursor](https://github.com/getcursor/cursor/commit/5473e8d4144d4b9f30a6afb3933d72c71e57528d)).
- **Granola** launched as "The AI notepad for people in back-to-back meetings" ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/granola)).
- **Vercel**'s README still reads "Develop. Preview. Ship." ([Vercel](https://github.com/vercel/vercel/blob/main/README.md)).
- **Notion**'s 2024 campaign introduced "Think it. Make it.", which Notion called its first brand tagline ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/the-thinking-behind-our-latest-brand-campaign)).

Taken together, they show a life cycle. **Young products borrow a well-known rival's category to be understood, then claim a category of their own once they have scale.**

Supabase's biggest early spike was an accident. An early GitHub follower posted it to Hacker News while the team was still alpha testing. It stayed on the front page for more than 24 hours on May 27, 2020 and brought **30,000 visitors, more than 1,400 signups in seven days, and a jump from 300 to 1,300 GitHub stars**. It also hit the team's server limits and caused a three-hour outage ([Supabase postmortem](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/www/_blog/2020-07-10-alpha-launch-postmortem.mdx)). The team turned that accident into a system called Launch Week, first run in March 2021. It bundles a quarter's features into one announcement a day for five days, ending with "One more thing" ([Supabase](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/www/_blog/2021-03-25-launch-week.mdx)). How it works ([Supabase](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/www/_blog/2021-11-26-supabase-how-we-launch.mdx)):

- **Scope is flexible.** Features ship before the week and get "full write-ups, marketing copy, and broad Top-of-Funnel" during it.
- **Launch morning is timed to the minute:** Product Hunt at 7:55am, the blog post at 8:00am, the launch tweet at 8:05am, and the ask to angel investors to share it at 8:10am.
- **The builder writes the announcement:** "the person who implemented the code will be the same person who writes the marketing content".

The cadence ran from the first Launch Week in 2021 to the fifteenth in July 2025. By June 2026 Supabase reported nearly 10 million developers and said more than 60% of new databases are launched by an AI tool; both figures are self-reported ([Supabase](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/www/_blog/2026-06-04-supabase-series-f.mdx)).

Invite-only launches worked when the gate screened for fit:

- **Linear.** Its 2019 waitlist was a survey about current tools, motivation and company size. About 4,000 people signed up in a month. Karri Saarinen read the answers in a spreadsheet and emailed invites by hand. At public launch "maybe one company didn't subscribe" to the paid plan, by his account ([Lenny's Podcast transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Karri%20Saarinen.txt)).
- **Superhuman.** It required live onboarding, with about 20 people doing onboarding at the peak, and gave both the referrer and the friend a free month. It positioned on one axis, speed, because "almost no software was being sold or has ever been sold on the value proposition of speed". Rahul Vohra's "cocktail party test" took the positioning from how users pitched the product to each other: "Dude, you have to use it, it's really fucking fast." ([Vohra transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Rahul%20Vohra.txt)). First Round reports that the share of users who would be "very disappointed" without Superhuman rose from 22% to 58% ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/)).
- **Arc.** Its invite-gifting system built a reported 500,000-person waitlist for the Windows beta ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/11/arc-browser-launches-its-windows-client-in-beta/)). Yet its founder admitted "we have a really hard time describing Arc" ([Miller transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Josh%20Miller.txt)). His May 2025 letter conceded Arc was "too different, with too many new things to learn, for too little reward" ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/05/27/mac-browser-arc-being-discontinued-in-favor-of-new-dia-app/)), and **Arc stops working on December 22, 2026** ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/10/23/arc-browser-on-its-way-out-as-makers-suggest-an-alternative/5305114)). Scarcity created demand but not understanding.

AI-native products swapped the single big launch for a constant drumbeat. Lovable's founder renamed GPT Engineer because "Almost no one knew how good our product had become" and did a "third launch" ([X/@antonosika](https://x.com/antonosika/status/1872678941133066658)). The company reported **$4M ARR in four weeks** and, less than three months after launch, 300,000 monthly active users of whom 30,000 paid ([Osika transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Anton%20Osika.txt)). Head of growth Elena Verna describes the method ([Verna transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Elena%20Verna%204.0.txt)):

- "shipping every day, every other day, multiple times per day and just talking about it constantly";
- engineers who "have to announce the thing that they've shipped";
- launches sorted into tiers 3, 2 and 1 by size;
- influencer marketing that is "10 times bigger for us than paid social".

Replit's agent launch in September 2024 came before a reported jump from about $10M to $100M ARR by June 2025, as reported by the company ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/replit-growth-journey)). A viral post about an 11-year-old who built an app helped ([Masad transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Amjad%20Masad.txt)).

The founders' own rules for copy agree with each other:

- Write "our own reasons why we're doing this", in the first audience's own language (Saarinen) ([Saarinen transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Karri%20Saarinen.txt)).
- Stay truthful, because "You can fool other people once or twice, then there's no future" (Ivan Zhao).
- Sell the finished result, since "Only hardcore Lego fans care about Lego bricks. Most people care about Lego boxes." ([Zhao transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Ivan%20Zhao.txt)).

The failures in this group happened in copy away from the headline:

- **Cursor's pricing change.** In June 2025 Cursor replaced "500 fast requests" with usage billing and explained it so loosely that Michael Truell apologized: "We recognize that we didn't handle this pricing rollout well and we're sorry." Refunds followed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/07/cursor-apologizes-for-unclear-pricing-changes-that-upset-users/)).
- **ElevenLabs' voice cloning.** It was opened in the January 2023 beta and abused for fake celebrity clips within weeks. Safeguards came afterwards ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/ai-voice-firm-4chan-celebrity-voices-emma-watson-joe-rogan-elevenlabs/)).
- **Notion's sprint feature.** It was underpowered and left users asking "what is this?" ([Zhao transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Ivan%20Zhao.txt)).

**Pricing pages, permission settings and change notices are launch copy too**, and readers punish vague wording there faster than in a tagline. None of these companies has published controlled evidence linking a line to conversion. Linear says it runs no A/B tests at all ([Saarinen transcript](https://github.com/mpnikhil/lenny-rag-mcp/blob/main/transcripts/Karri%20Saarinen.txt)).

## Provocation buys a spike; only provocation tied to the product keeps it

The attention campaigns of the AI era used fewer words than any other group:

- **Cluely** raised a $5.3M seed in April 2025 for a tool to "cheat on everything" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/21/columbia-student-suspended-over-interview-cheating-tool-raises-5-3m-to-cheat-on-everything)). Two months later it announced a $15M round led by a16z with a post ending in the same three words ([X/@im_roy_lee](https://x.com/im_roy_lee/status/1936138361011585190)).
- **Artisan**'s billboards said "Stop Hiring Humans", and the first one was misspelled "Stop Hirring Humans" ([Artisan](https://artisan.co/blog/stop-hiring-humans)).
- **Friend** spent over $1M on more than 11,000 New York subway car cards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/27/ai-startup-friend-spent-more-than-1m-on-all-those-subway-ads)) with lines like "I'll never bail on our dinner plans" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91413814/friend-ai-ad-campaign-founder-qa)). The ads left heavy white space so people would "socially comment".
- **Anthropic**'s Super Bowl LX spots targeted OpenAI's plan to test ads in ChatGPT and ended on "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude." ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt/so-clearly-dishonest-sam-altman-responds-to-anthropics-super-bowl-ad-targeting-chatgpt-heres-what-he-said)). This report was written by Claude, an Anthropic model; the campaign gets the same caveats and criticism here as the others.
- **Coinbase**'s 2022 Super Bowl spot had no headline at all, only a QR code bouncing around the screen like a DVD screensaver ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/92911/coinbase-qr-code-super-bowl-free-bitcoin-ad-20m-hits-minute)).

When a third party measured anything, it confirmed a spike in attention or downloads. **It rarely showed revenue.** Friend is the clearest case: by the founder's own figures, it spent more than twice its revenue. Bloomberg Law has called ARR Silicon Valley's "least trusted" metric ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/tech-and-telecom-law/silicon-valleys-hottest-ai-metric-is-also-its-least-trusted)).

The costs follow four patterns:

- **Regulators act when a specific claim covers less than readers would assume.** The ASA ruled Oatly's "73% less CO2e" misleading because the evidence covered only one product, the Barista Edition. It also faulted "climate experts say" for presenting one expert's view as consensus. The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) fined DoNotPay $193,000 and barred it from claiming its chatbot matched a human lawyer, a claim it had never tested ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87474)).
- **Rage-bait draws rage.** After the threats and the vandalism, Artisan's CEO now says "I think the backlash is earned" ([SF Standard](https://sfstandard.com/2026/09/02/anti-ai-billboard-graffiti/)).
- **Rivals contest named attacks.** Altman's response put the accuracy of Anthropic's ads up for public debate. Critics noted that OpenAI had itself announced sponsored placements ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt/so-clearly-dishonest-sam-altman-responds-to-anthropics-super-bowl-ad-targeting-chatgpt-heres-what-he-said)).
- **A brand voice cannot shield an unpopular company decision.** Duolingo's "unhinged" persona is credited with growing its TikTok from about 50,000 to over 16M followers ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot)). It still became a target the moment the AI-first memo landed.

Provocation worked when it was about the product's real job. Claude's promise of no ads is a commitment about the product itself, and Coinbase's QR code *was* the sign-up path. It failed when it insulted the people the company needed: Artisan's line insulted the workers its buyers manage, and Friend's lines implied a dig at New Yorkers' own friends. **The rage-bait campaigns took their messages back within roughly 6 to 18 months**, once they needed enterprise buyers, recruits or partners.

The one long-run effectiveness finding in this group favors repetition over one-off stunts. System1's analysis of cases from the UK advertising trade body IPA reports that campaigns built around a recurring character, which System1 calls a "fluent device", are **37% more likely to grow market share** and 30% more likely to grow profit. System1 sells ad testing, so these are vendor figures ([System1](https://system1group.com/blog/the-seven-habits-of-highly-effective-characters)).

At seed stage, the cheap moves are the copy itself:

- a contrarian command;
- a "never" or "not us" contrast;
- a founder-shot scene;
- a manifesto;
- critics' quotes turned into content;
- a single billboard, photographed for social media;
- a mechanic that asks the audience to do something, like scanning a code.

The expensive moves are media buys (national TV, citywide outdoor ads, celebrities) and stunts that need a reputation a startup lacks. Perplexity's $34.5B offer for Chrome, made while it was valued at about $18B ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/12/perplexity-offers-to-buy-chrome-for-billions-more-than-its-raised)), drew "unserious bid" headlines ([Telecoms.com](https://www.telecoms.com/digital-ecosystem/perplexity-makes-unserious-bid-to-buy-google-s-chrome-browser)).

## Five traits recur in every segment that worked

Across the four groups, the campaigns that worked shared five traits:

1. **A named enemy.** The keyboard, a card issuer's incentives, Firebase, slow email, ads in AI.
2. **One claim on one axis.** Superhuman's speed and Ramp's lower spending; Arc, which never found its line, shows what happens without one.
3. **A human messenger whose words were real.** Kothari's phone video, Saarinen's founders writing their own reasons, creators' natural confessions, engineers announcing their own work.
4. **Proof a reader could check**, ideally with a unit and a method.
5. **Many cheap, separate moments.** Stacked launch days, platform launches, Launch Weeks, daily changelog posts, funding announcements rewritten for users.

## AI copy fails by going generic, and readers who notice punish it

### Regression to the mean is the root tell

Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" guide, kept by its AI Cleanup volunteers, gives the clearest account of why machine text sounds machine-made. Language models "omit specific, unusual, nuanced facts" and replace them with "generic, positive descriptions." In the guide's example, "inventor of the first train-coupling device" becomes "a revolutionary titan of industry", so the subject ends up both less specific and more exaggerated ([Wikipedia mirror](https://github.com/softaworks/agent-toolkit/blob/main/skills/writing-clearly-and-concisely/signs-of-ai-writing.md); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)).

The guide lists the tells that follow from this:

- inflated significance ("stands as a testament", "plays a pivotal role");
- empty "-ing" endings tacked onto sentences ("…, highlighting its commitment");
- brochure language ("nestled", "boasts");
- "not just X, it's Y" constructions;
- strings of negatives ("No X. No Y. Just Z.");
- reflexive groups of three;
- ranges whose ends have nothing in common ("from problem-solving to artistic expression");
- vague attributions ("industry reports");
- a list of overused vocabulary;
- formatting by rule: em dashes, emoji bullets, bold labels at the start of list items, title-case headings.

Two caveats from the guide shape any linter built on it. It is "descriptive, not prescriptive", and it says tells "co-occur", so one or two prove little. It also lists **ineffective indicators**: perfect grammar, "bland" prose and fancy words. A linter that flags those will punish good human writing.

Corpus studies put numbers on the vocabulary and show how quickly it changes:

- **The excess-words study.** Kobak and colleagues analyzed about 14 million PubMed abstracts from 2010 to 2024. They estimate that at least 13.5% of 2024 abstracts were processed by a language model ([Science Advances](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adt3813)).
- **The most overused words.** Using the authors' published data, the research team computed how often each word appeared in 2024 compared with the expected rate: "delves" 28.2 times, "underscores" 13.8, "showcasing" 10.7, "meticulously" 10.5. Marketing-flavored words were also high: "groundbreaking" 4.9, "revolutionizing" 4.1, "seamlessly" and "leveraging" 3.1, "transformative" 3.0 ([berenslab data](https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab)). These are medical abstracts, not ads, so the exact ratios depend on the field.
- **Word-level signals fade.** "Delves" peaked at 39.5 times the expected rate and fell to 8.0 by mid-2025 ([berenslab](https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab)).
- **Structural signals grew instead.** A Washington Post analysis of 328,744 shared ChatGPT messages found em dashes went from fewer than 1 response in 10 to more than half by summer 2025. "Not just X, but Y" appeared in 6% of chats in July 2025, and ✅ was the model's favorite emoji ([Boston Globe/WaPo](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/11/13/business/chatgpt-writing-style-clues/)).
- **Grammar differs too.** A 2025 PNAS study found instruction-tuned models use clauses built on "-ing" verbs at **2 to 5 times the human rate** ([CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/news/news-stories/2025/large-language-models-writing-text)).

Among marketing clichés, only one has a published multiplier: the detector vendor GPTZero says "today's fast-paced world" appears at 107 times the human rate ([GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/most-common-ai-vocabulary/)). Social-post tics such as "Let that sink in" or "I'm thrilled to announce" have no frequency data. They were LinkedIn habits before ChatGPT, so they mark generic copy more reliably than AI copy.

### The penalty arrives when readers detect AI or are told

The commercial cost of AI-sounding copy depends on whether the audience perceives it:

- **Emotional messages take the biggest hit.** Kirk and Givi ran seven preregistered experiments. When consumers believed an emotional marketing message came from AI, they reported **less positive word of mouth and lower loyalty**, driven by lower perceived authenticity and then moral disgust. The effect weakened for factual messages and **when AI only edited a human draft** ([Journal of Business Research](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v186y2025ics0148296324004880.html)).
- **AI ads are remembered less.** NielsenIQ tested AI-generated ads on more than 2,000 people. They were often rated "annoying", "boring" and "confusing", and even high-quality AI ads triggered weaker memory activation than traditional ads ([NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2024/niq-research-uncovers-hidden-consumer-attitudes-toward-ai-generated-ads/)).
- **Suspected AI content loses trust.** A Raptive survey of 3,000 US adults linked it to nearly 50% lower trust and 14% lower purchase consideration. Raptive is a publisher with a commercial interest in human-written content ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/raptive-study-shows-ai-content-cuts-reader-trust-by-half/)).
- **Even mentioning AI can hurt.** Putting the term "artificial intelligence" in otherwise identical product descriptions lowered purchase intent, most of all for high-risk products such as expensive electronics, medical devices and financial services ([WSU](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2024/07/30/using-the-term-artificial-intelligence-in-product-descriptions-reduces-purchase-intentions/)).
- **Blind tests tell a different story.** Coca-Cola's 2024 AI holiday ad earned System1's top rating of 5.9 stars from viewers who were not told it was AI-made ([System1](https://system1group.com/ad-of-the-week/ai-or-no-ai-coke-gets-the-christmas-love)). The backlash came from people who knew.

So the penalty follows detection. Detection is more likely with exactly the audience most startups sell to. Heavy users of language models identify AI-written articles **about 90% of the time**, while people who rarely use them do only slightly better than chance ([ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.267/)). Founders, developers and operators reading X and LinkedIn are disproportionately heavy users. That is an inference, but it means startup copy faces the sharpest readers there are.

Generic AI text is also now the background everyone competes against. Pew found signs of AI writing on 9.6% of a random sample of web pages in July 2026, and on about 35% of pages published since ChatGPT launched; commercial .com pages were the most likely ([Pew Research](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/08/20/how-much-of-the-internet-is-written-with-ai/)). The AI-detection company Originality.ai estimated that 54% of long LinkedIn posts in October 2024 were likely AI-written ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91237998/how-linkedin-opened-the-door-to-ai-slop)). When the median post reads like this, specificity is what sets a post apart.

### Tested craft research points the same way as the tell list

The controlled evidence on what sells leads to the same place as the tell list:

- **Concrete language sells.** In five studies, Packard and Berger found that when employees spoke more concretely ("that t-shirt in grey" rather than "that"), customers were **more satisfied, more willing to buy, and bought more**. Customers read concrete language as a sign the employee was listening ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/how-language-boosts-customer-satisfaction-wharton-jonah-berger)).
- **Headline wording changes clicks at scale.** Across about 105,000 tested Upworthy headline variations, each extra negative word raised click-through by 2.3%, and sad words worked better than angry ones ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2023/03/negative-words-in-news-headlines-generate-more-clicks-but-sad-words-are-more-effective-than-angry-or-scary-ones/)). This is news data, and no evidence was found on whether negative framing is safe for brands.
- **Put the offer in the headline.** 37signals moved "30-day free trial" into the headline of its Highrise signup page, and the page **converted 30% better** ([Signal v. Noise](https://signalvnoise.com/posts/1525-writing-decisions-headline-tests-on-the-highrise-signup-page)).
- **Measure paid conversions, not signups.** Later Highrise tests found that total signups hid a drop in paying customers ([Signal v. Noise](https://signalvnoise.com/posts/2991-behind-the-scenes-ab-testing-part-3-final)).

Practitioner methods fill in the rest, without controlled data:

- **Harry Dry's three questions** ("Can I visualize it? Can I falsify it? Can nobody else say this?") turn the "regression to the mean" diagnosis into a test a reviewer can run ([Demand Curve](https://www.demandcurve.com/newsletters/growth-newsletter-202)).
- **Copyhackers' review mining** builds headlines from the exact phrases customers repeat, on the principle "Review mining data should write your copy, you shouldn't write copy" ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/write-copy-amazon-review-mining/)).
- **Gary Provost's passage on sentence rhythm** argues for mixing short and long sentences ([Wikipedia: Gary Provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Provost)).

Classic authorities often quoted alongside these, including Ogilvy's headline economics and Schwartz's five levels of customer awareness, were not verified in this research and should be treated as expert opinion.

### A linter should score density, not single words

Combined, the evidence supports a critic with three layers. The first is a **specificity floor**: every headline or ad carries at least one number, proper noun, concrete physical noun, or verbatim customer phrase, and body copy carries one about every 40 words. The second is a **set of copywriting tests run in order**: could a competitor publish this line unchanged, could it be proven false, can a reader picture it, does every sentence add new information, and does the rhythm vary when read aloud. The third is a **tell scan scored by density**, so isolated hits do not fail copy that is otherwise good. The research team's suggested scoring adds up weighted hits per 100 words and fails long copy at 6 or more, or any headline, overlay or ad at 3 or more. The tell lists should be updated every quarter, because the word-level signals fade within a year ([berenslab](https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab)).

| Family of tells | Example | What the evidence rests on | Suggested treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chatbot leftovers and placeholders | "I hope this helps", "[Your Company]", "2025-XX-XX" | Wikipedia guide | Block |
| "Not X, but Y" constructions | "It's not just X, it's Y"; "not only… but also" | Wikipedia; 6% of ChatGPT chats ([WaPo](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/11/13/business/chatgpt-writing-style-clues/)) | Rewrite on one hit |
| Runs of negatives or fragments | "No X. No Y. Just Z."; "every. single. day." | Wikipedia; practitioner lists | Rewrite on one hit |
| Inflated significance | "stands as a testament", "plays a pivotal role" | Wikipedia | Rewrite on one hit |
| Rare AI vocabulary | delve, underscore, showcase, meticulous, intricate, realm, tapestry, testament, groundbreaking | Overuse ratios of about 5x to 28x ([berenslab](https://github.com/berenslab/llm-excess-vocab)) | Rewrite on one hit in short copy |
| Common AI vocabulary | crucial, enhance, foster, leverage, seamless, streamline, comprehensive, transformative, unlock | Kobak et al.; Wikipedia | Flag; rewrite at 2 or more per 100 words |
| Marketing clichés | "In today's fast-paced world", "unlock the power of", "take it to the next level" | GPTZero (107x for the first); practitioner lists | Rewrite in headlines |
| Empty "-ing" endings | ", highlighting its commitment to…" | Wikipedia; 2 to 5 times the human rate ([CMU/PNAS](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/news/news-stories/2025/large-language-models-writing-text)) | Flag |
| Vague attribution | "Experts say", "studies show" | Wikipedia; the ASA's Oatly ruling | Rewrite unless a name or number sits nearby |
| Staged openers and closers | "Here's the thing", "Let that sink in", "Thoughts?" | Practitioner lists; no frequency data | Rewrite in social posts |
| Formatting by rule | Em dashes in short ads, emoji bullets, bold list labels, ✅ | Wikipedia; WaPo | Ignore alone; flag when they pile up |

Two more rules come from the cost evidence. **Lint emotional brand and launch storytelling hardest**, because that is where perceived AI authorship costs the most word of mouth. And in consumer benefit lines, **lead with the outcome, not "AI-powered"**, especially in finance, health and expensive hardware ([WSU](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2024/07/30/using-the-term-artificial-intelligence-in-product-descriptions-reduces-purchase-intentions/)).

## From thirty characters to three thousand: the limits copy must fit

Every deliverable has to fit a platform limit, and the limits are counted in **four different units**:

- **X's weighted characters.** Each URL counts as 23, and certain character ranges count double ([X Docs](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/counting-characters)).
- **Bluesky's grapheme clusters**, which are user-perceived characters.
- **UTF-8 bytes**, used for Apple keywords and YouTube API descriptions.
- **Google Ads' double-width counting** for Chinese, Japanese and Korean characters.

A simple string-length count gets several of these wrong.

Most of the figures below come from search excerpts of the official pages, because direct access was blocked; only Apple's product-page guide was read in full. Community rules matter as much as character counts, and they lean against marketing voice:

- **Show HN** is "for something you've made that other people can play with". Landing pages, sign-up pages and fundraisers do not qualify, and the maker must be around to discuss it ([HN Show HN Guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)). Hacker News titles must not editorialize or use uppercase, exclamation points or self-praise ([HN Guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html)).
- **Product Hunt** says the tagline should avoid catchphrases and slogans ([Product Hunt Help](https://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/5473056-how-to-post-main-info)). Asking people to upvote "is against our policies" and can get a launch disqualified ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/launch/sharing-your-launch)).
- **Reddit** says promotion is "not inherently considered to be spam", and its etiquette page suggests roughly one in ten of your submissions be your own content. Individual communities can ban promotion outright ([Reddit Help](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043504051-Spam); [Reddiquette](https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/205926439-Reddiquette)).

For video, an old but platform-sourced Facebook and Nielsen finding still shapes the format: **up to 47% of a video campaign's value was delivered in its first 3 seconds**, and about 41% of videos were "basically meaningless without sound" ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebook-offers-tips-for-converting-tv-ads-to-mobile/448510/); [MarTech](https://martech.org/facebook-releases-new-video-ad-features-including-automated-captions)). That is why Wispr's captions appear within a third of a second. On-screen text in the opening seconds should state the proposition with a concrete noun or number, readable with the sound off.

Several official sources contradict each other:

- the maximum intro text for LinkedIn sponsored post ads (600 or 3,000 characters);
- the Product Hunt description limit (260 or 500);
- recommended text lengths for Meta carousel ads;
- the hook window on TikTok (3 or 6 seconds);
- which X subscription tiers get long posts.

So the harness should store each limit as a record: its value, whether it is hard, recommended or observed, its counting unit, how it was verified, its source and the date it was checked. It should fail copy that breaks a hard limit and only warn on recommended ones. Limits change: X's long-post cap went from 4,000 to 25,000 characters ([X API changelog](https://docs.x.com/changelog)), and YouTube Shorts went to three minutes in October 2024 ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)). A person should recheck the table on a schedule.

## Conclusion

In every campaign that worked, the human-sounding material came from something real: Kothari's onboarding calls, the pitches Vohra heard users give each other, Saarinen's founders writing their own reasons, Supabase engineers announcing their own features, creators saying what they would have said anyway, Ramp's transaction data. A language model left alone does the opposite and drifts toward the generic. So Inroad's harness should treat generation as arranging real material, not inventing it. Its first step should be an intake that draws the specifics out of the founder:

- the enemy;
- the one claim on one axis;
- the first audience and the words they use;
- customers' own pitches and phrases;
- an honest list of what the product cannot do yet;
- a bank of proof points, each with its source, scope, date and a "self-reported" flag.

The model's job is to shape that material into the structures and channel formats above. This design also lowers the AI penalty, since Kirk and Givi found it weakens when AI edits a human draft rather than writing from nothing. Specificity is at once the strongest tested driver of response, the clearest sign of human writing and the main source of legal exposure. So the critic has to ask two questions of every specific: is it there, and is it true within its stated scope.

Everything in this field also decays, so the harness needs dates and versions throughout. Linear's tagline lasted four and a half years. The rage-bait lines lasted six to eighteen months, and Wispr's ad creatives about eleven days. "Delves" lost most of its excess frequency within a year, X's long-post cap rose more than sixfold, and Ramp's own totals disagree from page to page. Spec limits, tell lists and claims all need check dates, review schedules and "last verified" stamps. Because no line in this research has controlled evidence behind it, the harness should generate variants across distinct angles (pain, outcome, proof, objection) and measure them against paid conversion rather than signups. The same five traits apply to every kind of company; what changes by segment is which one leads.
